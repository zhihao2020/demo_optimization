\shorttitle{HMSD for multi-energy plant scheduling}
\title[mode=title]{Hierarchical market-safe dispatch for hybrid multi-energy plant scheduling under time-of-use tariffs}

\begin{abstract}
Provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs create strong peak--valley incentives for plant-level storage, yet coordinating thermal generation, battery and compressed-air energy storage (CAES) remains a hybrid-action, multi-timescale problem with hard operational constraints. Unlike cost-minimising multi-energy studies that treat TOU mainly as an exogenous tariff in a surrogate energy balance, this work formulates \emph{weekly price-taker cash-flow maximisation} for a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a high-fidelity Modelica--FMU digital twin under Shandong industrial/commercial TOU prices, with an explicit energy state-of-charge (SoC) recovery gate. Classical programmes require aggressive convexification of CAES physics, while single-layer deep reinforcement learning (DRL) trained from scratch often under-utilises storage or fails weekly recovery. Building on goal-conditioned hierarchical twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) control, we specialise the stack as \emph{Hierarchical Market-Safe Dispatch} (HMSD): a high-level TD3 issues market- and recovery-aware plant intents every $c$ steps; a low-level goal-conditioned TD3 maps $(s,g)$ to hybrid actions under observation normalisation and amplified goal channels so hierarchical intents remain causal without a frozen teacher; market-structure goal priors (MSGP), market-structured hindsight experience replay (MS-HER) and feasibility-filtered maximum-likelihood pretraining (F-MLE) warm-start learning; and a GiveSafe filter projects actions onto the dynamic feasible set before each FMU step. Multi-seed evaluation on three representative weeks yields mean episode-reward lifts of about $+14$ (winter), $+56$ (transition) and $+36$ (summer) over a typical single-layer TD3 baseline under the same budget, with SoC gates recovered in all winter/transition runs and $2/3$ summer seeds. Seasonal power balances, SoC trajectories and cost-component analyses show hierarchical peak--valley arbitrage with feasible grid exchange, in contrast to under-acting single-layer policies and irregular parametric PSO. Matched multi-seed short-budget ablations of MSGP, MS-HER and F-MLE diagnose component roles under a fixed training budget, while the main multi-seed ranking uses the full $3.5\times10^{4}$-step stack; sensitivity of the subgoal interval $c$ and intrinsic weight $\alpha$ further supports the operating point.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Weekly price-taker cash-flow scheduling of a thermal--battery--CAES FMU plant under Shandong TOU tariffs.
\item HMSD: hierarchical market intents plus safe hybrid dispatch (MSGP, MS-HER, F-MLE, GiveSafe) on an FMU twin.
\item Multi-seed gains over single-layer TD3, with seasonal dispatch, SoC and cost-component evidence versus rules, linprog and PSO.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =====================================================================

Deep decarbonisation is accelerating the integration of renewable generation into multi-energy plants that co-dispatch electricity, heat-related conversion and storage~\cite{zhou2023iesdrl,yang2024planning}. Industrial and commercial customers increasingly face provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs that create strong peak--valley incentives for storage arbitrage and thermal load shifting~\cite{ndrc2021tou,shandong2023tou}. This paper addresses \emph{plant-level price-taker operation}: the facility takes exogenous TOU buy/sell prices as given and schedules thermal, battery and CAES assets to maximise weekly net cash flow while recovering energy inventories---not speculative nodal bidding or ISO market clearing~\cite{emmanuel2020market,pei2024pricetaker}. At the plant level, however, scheduling must respect hybrid action spaces (continuous set-points together with discrete CAES modes), ramp and inventory limits, and long-horizon SoC recovery. When the plant dynamics are provided by a high-fidelity Modelica--FMU twin rather than a convex energy-balance surrogate, the decision problem becomes a computationally expensive hybrid Markov decision process (MDP).

Model-based optimisers (mixed-integer programmes, rolling linearisations, particle swarm heuristics) remain valuable baselines, yet they either require aggressive convexification of non-convex CAES physics or suffer from limited budgets in high-dimensional hybrid spaces~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Data-driven deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{sutton2018rl,fujimoto2018td3,haarnoja2018sac} learns policies by closed-loop interaction and is therefore attractive for nonlinear twins. Hierarchical goal-conditioned RL further mitigates temporal abstraction: Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} develop GHTD3 for hydrogen-enhanced integrated energy systems (H-IES), coupling a semi-MDP high level with a goal-augmented low level and demonstrating peak--valley utilisation under TOU within a \emph{comprehensive cost} objective (electricity trading, device O\&M, curtailment and carbon). Their controllers are twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) agents~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}, and high-level learning uses retrospective goal relabeling in the spirit of hindsight experience replay (HER)~\cite{andrychowicz2017her}. Multi-agent hierarchical options~\cite{jendoubi2023hier} and multi-timescale market bidding~\cite{ochoa2024bidding} similarly exploit temporal structure. Constrained and safe RL emphasise feasible-set restriction during learning and execution~\cite{garcia2015safe,garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained,wachi2023safe}. Following that hierarchical skeleton rather than proposing a new actor--critic family, this work specialises goal-conditioned hierarchical TD3 as \emph{Hierarchical Market-Safe Dispatch} (HMSD) for thermal--battery--CAES operation on an FMU twin under price-taker TOU.

From this landscape three gaps remain for the present plant twin. (1)~\textbf{Cross-horizon cash-flow under TOU.} Single-layer TD3/SAC agents are myopic with respect to week-scale inventory goals and peak--valley plans; cost-minimising H-IES studies that include TOU do not target weekly net cash-flow maximisation of a thermal--battery--CAES FMU plant with an explicit energy-SoC gate. (2)~\textbf{Contracted feasible sets under hybrid CAES actions.} Mode locks, ramps and inventory bounds shrink the executable action set; unconstrained exploration on the twin is unsafe and unrepresentative of plant practice. (3)~\textbf{Hierarchy that remains causal on large-scale observations.} When continuous actors map unbounded logits through saturating nonlinearities on megawatt-scale measurements, the Jacobian $\partial a/\partial g$ can collapse so that hierarchical goals cease to affect executed actions---hierarchy then degenerates to a single-layer controller unless market-aware goals, hindsight relabelling and numerical goal pathways are co-designed.

Table~\ref{tab:lit} positions representative works. Existing hierarchical and constrained DRL studies rarely combine (i)~hybrid thermal--battery--CAES actions on a high-fidelity FMU twin, (ii)~market-conditioned hierarchical goals with MS-HER/F-MLE warm-start and pre-execution safety filtering, and (iii)~a shared weekly SoC-recovery protocol against rules, rolling LP, PSO and typical single-layer TD3.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Market--inventory--feasibility problem.} We formalise weekly price-taker cash-flow maximisation for a hybrid thermal--battery--CAES plant on a high-fidelity Modelica--FMU twin under Shandong TOU tariffs, with an explicit energy-SoC recovery gate and a \emph{dynamic} feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s)$ (ramps, mode locks, minimum-run). This is a different plant--market interface from hydrogen-centric comprehensive-cost H-IES studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}: the decision problem couples multi-day arbitrage, terminal inventory feasibility and hybrid non-convex execution.
\item \textbf{Hierarchical market-safe mechanisms (HMSD).} We propose a hierarchical controller in which (i)~high-level intents live in a \emph{market--inventory goal space} with TOU/recovery structure (MSGP), (ii)~high-level credit uses \emph{market-structured hindsight} (MS-HER) rather than uniform relabeling, (iii)~pretraining is restricted to \emph{feasibility-supported} transitions (F-MLE), not a frozen strong teacher residual, (iv)~observation normalisation and goal-channel scaling keep $\partial a/\partial g$ non-vanishing under megawatt-scale measurements (intent--dispatch causality), and (v)~GiveSafe realises pre-execution projection onto $\mathcal{F}(s)$. Twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3)~\cite{fujimoto2018td3} and the hierarchical semi-MDP / goal-augmented MDP pattern~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,andrychowicz2017her} are used as controllers and temporal abstraction tools; the scientific claim is the market--inventory--feasibility coupling and the associated learning mechanisms.
\item \textbf{Closed-loop evidence.} Multi-seed three-season FMU evaluations report episode rewards and physical cash-flow $J$, large paired gains over single-layer TD3 under the same budget, classical optimisers that often fail to beat a conservative rule on the twin, feasibility/safety statistics under GiveSafe, and matched multi-seed ablations that diagnose short-budget module behaviour complementary to the main ranking.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:system} describes the multi-energy plant and TOU market. Section~\ref{sec:problem} formulates plant physics (generation, conversion, multi-tank CAES and battery dynamics), bus balance with curtailment/unserved load, operating constraints and the economic objective, and casts closed-loop control as a hybrid-action MDP. Section~\ref{sec:method} presents the HMSD solution methodology. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports simulation settings, comparative results and sensitivity analyses. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

This section formulates the multi-energy plant physics, operating constraints and market objective that define the weekly scheduling problem. Continuous-time differential--algebraic equations of the plant (thermal unit, battery, multi-tank CAES, renewables and grid interface) are integrated inside a Modelica--FMU digital twin with communication interval $\tau_s=1$~h; the closed-form relations below are the dispatch-level models implemented by that twin and used by optimisers and RL agents. Throughout the paper we adopt an \emph{academic sign convention}: renewable and thermal generation are non-negative when injecting power, battery/CAES charge is positive and discharge is negative, and grid import is positive. The FMU export uses the opposite generation-negative convention; Python normalises magnitudes before learning and evaluation. Component sets are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=\{\mathrm{w},\mathrm{pv}\},\quad
I_2=\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{caes}\},\quad
I_3=\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},\\
I&=I_1\cup I_2\cup I_3.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sets}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Energy generation units}

\subsubsection{Photovoltaics}

PV available power depends on plane-of-array irradiance $G_t$, module surface temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}$ and nameplate capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}=260$~MW. With standard irradiance $G_{\mathrm{stc}}=1000$~W/m$^2$, standard temperature $T_{\mathrm{stc}}=298.15$~K, temperature coefficient $K_T=0.005$~K$^{-1}$ and conversion efficiency $\eta^{\mathrm{pv}}=0.95$,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}
=
\max\Bigl(
P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}
\frac{G_t}{G_{\mathrm{stc}}}
\bigl[1-K_T\bigl(T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}-T_{\mathrm{stc}}\bigr)\bigr]
\eta^{\mathrm{pv}},\,0\Bigr).
\label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
Module temperature is obtained from ambient temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{air}}$, wind speed $v_t$ and irradiance by the twin correlation
\begin{equation}
T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}
=
T_t^{\mathrm{air}}
+
0.0138\bigl(1+0.031(T_t^{\mathrm{air}}-273.15)\bigr)
\bigl(1-0.042\,v_t\bigr)G_t.
\label{eq:pv-temp}
\end{equation}
Actual injected PV power after bus curtailment satisfies $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$.

\subsubsection{Wind turbines}

Wind available power follows a cubic power curve with cut-in, rated and cut-out speeds $v_{\mathrm{ci}}=3$~m/s, $v_{\mathrm{r}}=10$~m/s and $v_{\mathrm{co}}=15$~m/s on capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{w}}=300$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}
=
\begin{cases}
0, & 0\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{ci}}\ \text{or}\ v_t\ge v_{\mathrm{co}},\\[2pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}\,\dfrac{v_t^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}}{v_{\mathrm{r}}^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}},
& v_{\mathrm{ci}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{r}},\\[6pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}, & v_{\mathrm{r}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{co}}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:wt}
\end{equation}
Turbine pitch is not a free decision variable. Actual wind injection obeys $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}$.

\subsection{Energy conversion units}

\subsubsection{Thermal generation}

Thermal electric power is commanded by the continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp},t}$:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{th}} = u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}},
\qquad
u_{\min}\le u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\le u_{\max},
\label{eq:th}
\end{equation}
with $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=150$~MW, $P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}=50$~MW, $u_{\min}=P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}/P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=1/3$ and $u_{\max}=1$ (no shut-down). Inter-hour ramping is enforced on the actual thermal power,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|P_t^{\mathrm{th}}-P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{th}}\bigr|
\le
\Delta_{\mathrm{th}}^{\max}
=
r_{\max}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}\,\tau_s,
\label{eq:th-ramp}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\max}=0.0025/60$~s$^{-1}$ is the Modelica load-rate limit (equivalently $0.15$~p.u.\ per hour). Fuel and O\&M costs enter $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ in~\eqref{eq:dJ}.

\subsubsection{CAES conversion}

CAES is a hybrid conversion--storage plant. The hybrid command comprises a discrete mode and continuous magnitude
\begin{equation}
m_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\},\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:caes-cmd}
\end{equation}
mapped to a signed electric power command on capacity $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}=150$~MW,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
=
\begin{cases}
+\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{charge},\\
0, & m=\mathrm{idle},\\
-\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{discharge},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:caes-p}
\end{equation}
or equivalently $P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}=u_{\mathrm{caes},t}P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}$ with the non-convex legal set
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm{caes},t}
\in
[-1,-0.33]\cup\{0\}\cup[0.86,1]
\label{eq:caes-legal}
\end{equation}
encoding minimum compressor/expander loading. Mode locks and minimum-run dwell constraints further restrict admissible transitions of $m_{\mathrm{caes}}$ inside the twin and the GiveSafe oracle. Electric power is realised as planned ($P^{\mathrm{act}}=P^{\mathrm{plan}}$); mass-flow set-points of air and thermal fluid scale with $|P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}|$ and reverse direction between compression and expansion.

\subsection{Energy storage units}

\subsubsection{Battery}

Battery power is commanded by $u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\in[-1,1]$ on $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}=100$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{bat}} = u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}.
\label{eq:bat-p}
\end{equation}
With energy capacity $E_{\mathrm{cap}}=500$~MWh and discharge efficiency $\eta=0.85$ (charge path lossless as in the twin), the SoC dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{\mathrm{bat}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=
\begin{cases}
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/E_{\mathrm{cap}}, & P^{\mathrm{bat}}\ge 0,\\[2pt]
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/(E_{\mathrm{cap}}\,\eta), & P^{\mathrm{bat}}<0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bat-soc}
\end{equation}
or, in hourly form with $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$ and $[x]_-=\max(-x,0)$,
\begin{equation}
s_{t+1,\mathrm{bat}}
=
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}}
+
\tau_s\frac{[P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_+ - [P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_-/\eta}{E_{\mathrm{cap}}}.
\label{eq:bat-soc-d}
\end{equation}
Inventory bounds are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}\in[0.1,0.9]$.

\subsubsection{CAES inventories}

CAES energy is stored as compressed air and two thermal fluid tanks. Gas SoC is pressure-based with rating $p_{\mathrm{norm}}=100$~bar,
\begin{equation}
s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}
=
\frac{p_t}{p_{\mathrm{norm}}},
\qquad
\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}}\le s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}\le \overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},
\label{eq:gas-soc}
\end{equation}
with $(\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},\overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}})=(0.6,1.0)$. Fixed-volume gas-tank mass and energy balances (ideal-air medium) evolve pressure $p$ and specific enthalpy $h$ under port mass flows $\dot m_{\mathrm{a}},\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}$ and wall heat $q_{\mathrm{w}}$,
\begin{align}
V\bigl(\partial_p\rho\,\dot p+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b}},
\label{eq:gas-mass}\\
V\bigl(-\dot p+\partial_p\rho\,\dot p\,h+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\,h+\rho\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}h_{\mathrm{a}}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}h_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathrm{out}}
+
q_{\mathrm{w}},
\label{eq:gas-energy}
\end{align}
with $q_{\mathrm{w}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{w}}S(T_{\mathrm{w}}-T)$ and gas temperature $T=T(p,h)$ (clipped at $253$~K). Hot and cold tanks store water at fixed pressure with free surface area $A$ and total volume $V_0=1.5\times10^{4}$~m$^3$. Level-based inventories
\begin{equation}
s_{t,i}
=
\frac{\ell_{t,i}}{V_0/A},
\qquad
i\in\{\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:thermal-soc}
\end{equation}
obey mass and enthalpy balances when within $(\underline{s}_i,\overline{s}_i)=(0.05,0.95)$,
\begin{equation}
\dot m_i=\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i},\qquad
m_i\dot h_i
=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}h_{\mathrm{a},i}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i}h_{\mathrm{b},i}^{\mathrm{out}}+Q_{\mathrm{gen},i}.
\label{eq:tank-bal}
\end{equation}
Compression ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}>0$) charges the gas inventory and drives the thermal circuit one way; expansion ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}<0$) reverses the flows. Primary weekly recovery inventories are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}$ and $s_{\mathrm{gas}}$; hot/cold SoCs and temperatures $(T^{\mathrm{hot}},T^{\mathrm{cold}})$ are process states used in hierarchical goals and shaping. All inventories obey~\eqref{eq:socbox}.

\subsection{System operation constraints}

The electric bus closes the power balance with curtailment and unserved load. Let $P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$ be renewable availability and $P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}$ the scheduled demand. Residual power after dispatchable assets is absorbed first by curtailing wind, then PV; residual deficit is met by demand response down to $20\%$ of plan, with remainder counted as unserved:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}}+P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}+P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
+P_t^{\mathrm{w}}+P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}+P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}
=
P_t^{\mathrm{load}},\\
&0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},\quad
0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}\ge 0,\quad
P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}\ge 0,\quad
0.2\,P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
Here $P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}$ is residual surplus after wind-then-PV curtailment and $P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}$ is residual shortfall after load reduction. Box and ramp limits, inventory bounds and grid exchange limits are
\begin{equation}
\underline{P}_{i}\le P_t^{i}\le \overline{P}_{i},\qquad
\bigl|P_t^{i}-P_{t-1}^{i}\bigr|\le \Delta_i^{\max},
\quad i\in\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{bat}\},
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\underline{s}_{i}\le s_{t,i}\le \overline{s}_{i},\qquad i\in\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:socbox}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min}\le P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}\le P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max},
\label{eq:grid}
\end{equation}
with $(P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min},P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max})=(-500,500)$~MW. Actions that would violate~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:grid},~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} or CAES mode locks lie outside the dynamic feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

Weekly operation further imposes an energy-SoC recovery gate on primary inventories,
\begin{equation}
L_1^{\mathrm{e}}
= w_{\mathrm{bat}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{bat}}-s_{0,\mathrm{bat}}\bigr|
+ w_{\mathrm{gas}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{gas}}-s_{0,\mathrm{gas}}\bigr|
\le \varepsilon,
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
with $(w_{\mathrm{bat}},w_{\mathrm{gas}})=(1.5,1.0)$ and $\varepsilon=0.06$.

\subsection{Optimization objectives}

Let $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ index hours in a weekly episode ($T=168$). Grid purchase/sale energies are $E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_+$ and $E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_-$ (MWh). Device operating costs $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ aggregate thermal fuel/O\&M and storage cycling cash-flow increments exported by the twin. Under price-taker TOU prices $\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$, the hourly net cash-flow increment and weekly objective are
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t
=
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}} E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}
-
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{op}},
\label{eq:dJ}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\max_{\{a_t\}}\; J = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \Delta J_t
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:pv}\text{--}\eqref{eq:soc},\
a_t\in\mathcal{F}(s_t).
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
In closed-loop evaluation, grid settlement uses exogenous TOU tables while non-grid device cash-flows follow FMU cumulative economic outputs. Policies are ranked by $J$ and gate~\eqref{eq:soc}; training maximises discounted extrinsic rewards built from $\Delta J_t$ and SoC shaping (Section~\ref{sec:reward}).

\subsection{Hybrid-action MDP}

Closed-loop control is cast as an MDP $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma\rangle$ with $\gamma=0.99$. The state concatenates inventories, thermo states, powers, prices and TOU features,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_t=\bigl[&
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}},s_{t,\mathrm{gas}},s_{t,\mathrm{hot}},s_{t,\mathrm{cold}},
p_t,T_t^{\mathrm{gas}},T_t^{\mathrm{hot}},T_t^{\mathrm{cold}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}},P_t^{\mathrm{bat}},P_t^{\mathrm{caes}},P_t^{\mathrm{grid}},
P_t^{\mathrm{w}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv}},P_t^{\mathrm{load}},
P_t^{\mathrm{curt}},P_t^{\mathrm{uns}},\\
&\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\ \text{TOU band features},\ \text{optional forecast residuals}
\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
and the hybrid action is
\begin{equation}
a_t = \bigl(u_{\mathrm{tp},t},\, u_{\mathrm{bat},t},\, m_{\mathrm{caes},t},\, \mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\bigr)\in\mathcal{A},
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with continuous channels scaled to physical limits and $m_{\mathrm{caes}}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\}$. Transitions are induced by one FMU hour that integrates~\eqref{eq:pv}--\eqref{eq:tank-bal} under GiveSafe-filtered actions $a_t\in\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

\subsection{Reward function for learning}
\label{sec:reward}

With calibration constant $C_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx1.565\times10^{5}$~CNY (rule-controller $p_{95}$ step cash-flow magnitude),
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{econ}} = \frac{\Delta J_t}{C_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
Potential-based inventory shaping uses the weighted L1 distance $L_{1,t}$ of (battery, gas, hot, cold) relative to episode start:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}
= \kappa_t \bigl(L_{1,t-1}-L_{1,t}\bigr)
- \kappa_t^{\mathrm{abs}} L_{1,t},
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
with base $\kappa=5$ and a recovery-window scale-up over the last $H=40$~h. At a completed, failure-free terminal step,
\begin{equation}
r_T^{\mathrm{term}}
=
\begin{cases}
B, & L_1^{\mathrm{e}}\le\varepsilon,\\
-p\,L_1^{\mathrm{e}}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:term}
\end{equation}
with $B=15$ and $p=30$. The extrinsic reward is
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{ext}}
= \mathrm{clip}\bigl(r_t^{\mathrm{econ}}+r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}+r_t^{\mathrm{term}},\, r_{\min},\, r_{\max}\bigr),
\label{eq:rext}
\end{equation}
with $(r_{\min},r_{\max})=(-25,25)$. Figure~\ref{fig:reward} summarises the stack. Classical programmes must convexify~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} and the CAES thermo coupling; HMSD (Section~\ref{sec:method}) optimises~\eqref{eq:J} by closed-loop interaction with the twin.

\section{Solution methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

The weekly scheduling task is modelled by two augmented MDPs. We adopt the goal-conditioned hierarchical TD3 backbone of Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} (SMDP high level, goal-augmented low level, dual TD3 controllers~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}) and specialise it as HMSD for market-aware, safety-filtered hybrid dispatch on the FMU twin. Figure~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarises the architecture.

\centering

\caption{HMSD pipeline: high-level TD3 with MSGP and MS-HER, absolute low-level policy with F-MLE warm-start, GiveSafe projection and FMU twin.}
\label{fig:algorithm}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Two augmented MDPs}

A standard one-step MDP is insufficient for week-scale inventory planning. Following~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, we use a high-level semi-MDP (SMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{G},P_c,R_c,\gamma^c\rangle$ that issues goals every $c$ steps, and a low-level goal-augmented MDP (GAMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G},P,R,\gamma\rangle$ that executes hybrid actions conditioned on the residual goal. The high-level action is the goal
\begin{equation}
g=\bigl[g^{\Delta\mathrm{bat}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{gas}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{th}},\,g^{u_{\mathrm{tp}}},\,g^{\mathrm{arb}}\bigr]^{\top}
\in [g_{\min},g_{\max}],
\label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
with box $[-0.30,0.30]\times[-0.12,0.12]\times[-0.10,0.10]\times[-0.20,0.20]\times[0,1]$. Low-level actions remain~\eqref{eq:action}.

\subsection{Hierarchy of decision making}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_cstep}
\caption{$c$-step interaction between high-level goals and low-level hybrid actions.}
\label{fig:cstep}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier

Every $c$ steps, the high-level policy $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$ observes $s_t$ and issues $g_t$. For $i\in[t,t+c-1]$, the low-level policy $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ maps $(s_i,g_i)$ to $a_i$, and residual goals are updated by
\begin{equation}
g_{i+1}=h(s_i,g_i,s_{i+1})
= s_i^{\mathrm{int}}+g_i-s_{i+1}^{\mathrm{int}},
\label{eq:h}
\end{equation}
where $s^{\mathrm{int}}$ denotes the inventory/intent subvector tracked by $g$. Intrinsic and layered training rewards follow~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{int}}
= -\Bigl(\sum_i w_i e_{i,t}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}+\alpha\, r_t^{\mathrm{ext}},
\qquad
e_t=s_t^{\mathrm{int}}+g_t-s_{t+1}^{\mathrm{int}},
\label{eq:rint}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{lo}} = r_t^{\mathrm{int}}, \qquad
R_k^{\mathrm{hi}} = \frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1} r_{t+j}^{\mathrm{ext}},
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with default $c=8$ and $\alpha\in[0.20,0.50]$. High-level bootstrapping uses discount $\gamma^{c}$.

Market-structure goal priors (MSGP) blend the raw high-level output with a TOU prior $g^{\mathrm{mkt}}$ (charge in valleys, discharge in peaks) and a recovery prior $g^{\mathrm{rec}}$ over the last $H=40$--$48$~h:
\begin{equation}
g \leftarrow (1-w_{\mathrm{m}}-w_{\mathrm{r}})\,\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}(s)
+ w_{\mathrm{m}}\,g^{\mathrm{mkt}}
+ w_{\mathrm{r}}\,g^{\mathrm{rec}},
\label{eq:msgp}
\end{equation}
with annealed $w_{\mathrm{m}}:0.55\rightarrow0.12$ and recovery weight up to $w_{\mathrm{r}}\in[0.95,0.98]$.

\subsection{Internal controllers (TD3)}

Both layers use TD3~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}. Write $x^{\mathrm{hi}}=s$, $a^{\mathrm{hi}}=g$ and $x^{\mathrm{lo}}=[s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g]$, $a^{\mathrm{lo}}=a$, with observation normalisation $s_{\mathrm{n}}=\tanh(s/s_0)$ and goal scale $\kappa=4$. Each layer maintains twin critics $Q_{\theta_1},Q_{\theta_2}$, actor $\mu_{\phi}$ and target networks. Critic losses and targets are
\begin{equation}
L(\theta_j)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(Q_{\theta_j}(x,a)-y\bigr)^2\bigr],\quad j=1,2,
\label{eq:td3-L}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
y=r+\gamma\min_{k=1,2}Q_{\bar\theta_k}\bigl(x',\tilde a'\bigr),
\qquad
\tilde a'=\mu_{\bar\phi}(x')+\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-\epsilon,\epsilon\bigr).
\label{eq:td3-y}
\end{equation}
The actor follows the deterministic policy gradient
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\phi}J
=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}
\Bigl[
\nabla_a Q_{\theta_1}(x,a)\big|_{a=\mu_{\phi}(x)}
\nabla_{\phi}\mu_{\phi}(x)
\Bigr],
\label{eq:td3-pi}
\end{equation}
with delayed actor updates and soft targets $\bar\psi\leftarrow\tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi$.

\subsection{GiveSafe projection}

Executable actions are projected onto the dynamic feasible set before each FMU step~\cite{garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained}:
\begin{equation}
a_t^{\star}
= \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s_t)}\!\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}\bigr).
\label{eq:givesafe}
\end{equation}
Only transitions generated by $a_t^{\star}$ enter the replay buffers. Absolute (non-residual) low-level execution is
\begin{equation}
a = \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}\!\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)\bigr).
\label{eq:abs}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Historical goal relabeling (MS-HER)}

High-level transitions $(s_t,g_t,R_{t:t+c-1}^{\mathrm{ext}},s_{t+c})$ become stale as $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ evolves. Market-structured hindsight experience replay extends HER-style goal relabeling~\cite{andrychowicz2017her,cui2025ghtd3}: it replaces $g_t$ by a candidate maximising a storage-activity-weighted log-likelihood under the current low-level policy,
\begin{equation}
\tilde g_t
\in
\arg\max_{g\in\mathcal{C}_t}
\sum_{i=t}^{t+c-1}
w_i^{\mathrm{act}}
\log \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(a_i\mid s_i,g_i),
\label{eq:msher}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_t$ mixes the original goal, achieved $\Delta s^{\mathrm{int}}$, future goals and optional market-prior candidates, and subsequent residuals follow~\eqref{eq:h}.

\subsection{Feasibility-filtered MLE pretraining (F-MLE)}

Before RL, low-level (and high-level goal) parameters are warm-started by maximum likelihood on \emph{physically valid} price-aware/rule trajectories only:
\begin{equation}
\phi^{\star}
\in
\arg\max_{\phi}
\sum_{(s,g,a)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{demo}}}
\log \pi_{\phi}(a\mid s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
a\in\mathcal{F}(s).
\label{eq:fmle}
\end{equation}
No frozen hybrid teacher residual is used at runtime.

\subsection{Policy learning}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ghtd3} summarises the training loop; Table~\ref{tab:hyper} lists main hyperparameters.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{HMSD}
\label{alg:ghtd3}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Initialise $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}},\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$, critics, targets; buffers $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}},\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; F-MLE warm-start~\eqref{eq:fmle}
\For{episode $=1,2,\ldots$}
  \State Reset FMU; $s\leftarrow s_0$; clear residual goal
  \For{$t=0$ to $T-1$}
    \If{$t \bmod c = 0$}
      \State Issue $g\leftarrow\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}(s)$; apply MSGP~\eqref{eq:msgp}
    \EndIf
    \State $\tilde a \leftarrow \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}$; $a\leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}(\tilde a)$
    \State Step FMU; observe $s'$, $\Delta J$; compute $r^{\mathrm{ext}},r^{\mathrm{int}}$ by~\eqref{eq:rext}--\eqref{eq:reward}
    \State Store low-level transition in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; $g\leftarrow h(s,g,s')$
    \If{$t \bmod c = c-1$} store high-level transition in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$
    \EndIf
    \State TD3 update on $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; every $c$ steps, MS-HER relabel~\eqref{eq:msher} and TD3 update on $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$ with $\gamma^{c}$
    \State $s\leftarrow s'$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Simulation settings}

Three representative weeks (winter, summer, transition) are evaluated on the FMU twin under identical TOU and resource tables. Unless stated otherwise, primary ranking uses weekly episodes with SoC re-initialisation (weekly-reset protocol), consistent with hierarchical multi-energy case studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Original conservative plant rule (B0; high thermal, storage largely idle); price-aware heuristic rule (B1); rolling linprog MPC; PSO; and \textbf{TD3}: a single-layer DRL agent that employs the same continuous hybrid action space, network capacity, training budget ($3.5\times10^{4}$ valid steps) and seeds as the low-level controller of HMSD, trained without hierarchical goals and without behaviour-cloning pretraining, so that performance differences isolate the hierarchical design as in~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. GiveSafe is applied to both TD3 and HMSD during interaction for a fair closed-loop comparison on the FMU twin.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Weekly net cash flow $J$~\eqref{eq:J}, calibrated episode reward, energy-SoC gate~\eqref{eq:soc}, thermal energy and storage throughput. Multi-seed tables report mean$\pm$std and SoC pass counts.

\paragraph{Experimental environment.}
All DRL training and closed-loop FMU evaluations reported in this paper were executed on a Windows~11 (64-bit) workstation with an \textbf{Intel Core~i9-14900K} CPU (24~cores / 32~threads; base clock 3.2~GHz) and 128~GB system RAM, together with an \textbf{NVIDIA GeForce RTX~4080} GPU (16~GB GDDR6X; compute capability~8.9), NVIDIA driver~591.86, and \textbf{CUDA~13.0} as exposed by the PyTorch binary (\texttt{torch}~2.9.1+\texttt{cu130}). The software stack uses Python~3.11 with PyTorch CUDA builds for actor--critic updates; the plant dynamics are advanced through a Modelica--FMU twin with one-hour communication steps. Parallel jobs (multi-seed training, ablations and sensitivity runs) isolate FMU instances via per-job temporary directories so that concurrent rollouts do not contend on a single unpacked twin. Classical baselines (rule, linprog, PSO) run under the same FMU interface on the same machine for protocol consistency.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Policies are trained with off-policy TD3 updates on FMU rollouts under the environment above. Hyperparameters of HMSD are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}; the main multi-seed budget is $3.5\times10^{4}$ valid environment steps with seeds $\{0,1,2\}$.

\subsubsection{Scheduling performance}

Closed-loop dispatch is examined for seed~$1$ (strong economic seed with full SoC pass on all three weeks), following the seasonal structure of hierarchical multi-energy case studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Figures~\ref{fig:bal-w}--\ref{fig:bal-s} compare electric power balances of HMSD, TD3, parametric PSO and rolling linprog; Fig.~\ref{fig:soc-mat} summarises battery and CAES gas SoC across seasons and methods (including the plant rule B0).

\paragraph{Winter.}
Figure~\ref{fig:bal-w}(a) shows that HMSD couples thermal set-points with battery/CAES activity aligned to TOU bands: storage charges in valleys and discharges toward peaks while the grid exchange remains structured rather than oscillatory. TD3 (panel~b) attains competitive winter reward for this seed but exhibits less coherent multi-day inventory planning. Parametric PSO (panel~c) fails early under hybrid CAES mode constraints (short valid horizon), illustrating the difficulty of low-dimensional peak--valley heuristics on the twin. Linprog (panel~d) produces a feasible but conservative schedule near rule-level economics. The SoC matrix (Fig.~\ref{fig:soc-mat}, top row) confirms hierarchical inventory motion under HMSD versus near-idle battery under B0.

\paragraph{Transition.}
In the transition week (Fig.~\ref{fig:bal-t}), HMSD maintains the largest and most regular storage throughput while recovering energy SoC at week end. TD3 largely under-acts on storage for this seed (reward collapse relative to hierarchy), consistent with the ``inaction'' failure mode reported for single-layer TD3 in hierarchical multi-energy studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. PSO can score a high cash-flow proxy by aggressive cycling yet still fails the energy-SoC gate, so it is not an operationally acceptable weekly plan. Linprog remains smooth but economically mediocre.

\paragraph{Summer.}
Under higher cooling-season load and TOU stress (Fig.~\ref{fig:bal-s}), HMSD again realises peak--valley-oriented storage use with full SoC pass on seed~$1$, while TD3 improves over B0 but trails hierarchy on reward. PSO trajectories remain irregular and fail recovery; linprog stays near the rule band. Across seasons, hierarchy therefore repairs both \emph{myopic under-use of storage} (TD3) and \emph{infeasible/irregular parametric search} (PSO) on the same FMU protocol.

\paragraph{Why classical optimisers underperform the plant rule B0---and what this implies for RL.}
\label{sec:why-or-pso}
A striking feature of Table~\ref{tab:main} is that rolling linprog and parametric PSO often fail to beat the conservative plant rule B0 on the \emph{same} closed-loop FMU protocol, even though both are ``optimisation'' methods. This is not an artefact of a weak rule baseline; it reflects a structural mismatch between simplified optimisers and the hybrid non-convex twin.

\textbf{B0 as a strong feasibility baseline.}
B0 keeps thermal output near the upper feasible band, holds the battery near idle and the CAES mode at idle whenever allowed, and therefore almost always completes the 168\,h week with the energy-SoC gate satisfied. Under TOU settlement, high thermal injection and limited storage cycling already yield competitive cash flow without mode-lock violations or inventory dumping. Any optimiser that intends to beat B0 must extract peak--valley value \emph{without} destroying feasibility---a harder task than improving a myopic or infeasible search.

\textbf{Rolling linprog: optimising the wrong model.}
The linprog controller solves a short-horizon linear programme on a \emph{relaxed surrogate}: continuous battery and gas-tank powers, linear fuel proxy, power balance and box SOC dynamics, then maps the first-step solution to hybrid CAES modes for FMU execution. Three gaps explain its mediocre closed-loop scores. (i)~\emph{Model mismatch}: discrete CAES modes, minimum-run locks and thermal inventories in the twin are only approximated; surrogate-optimal set-points are frequently projected or rewritten by the feasible set, so open-loop LP optimality does not transfer. (ii)~\emph{Myopic horizon}: a horizon of only a few hours cannot coordinate week-scale recovery and multi-day TOU structure. (iii)~\emph{Objective mismatch}: the LP minimises a linear buy/fuel/sell proxy, whereas evaluation ranks calibrated episode reward and the energy-SoC gate built from the true FMU cash-flow. Empirically, linprog induces much larger storage throughput than B0 yet returns lower winter/summer rewards (Table~\ref{tab:main}), i.e.\ aggressive cycling on a wrong model does not improve true economics.

\textbf{Parametric PSO: searching a narrow and brittle policy class.}
PSO in this study does not optimise free hybrid action sequences. It tunes a six-dimensional peak--valley heuristic (price thresholds, storage magnitudes, thermal bias, recovery strength), with each particle evaluated by a full FMU week. The searchable class is far smaller than the hybrid MDP; many parameter vectors trigger early constraint failures (e.g.\ winter trajectories that terminate after a few steps), and large failure penalties dominate the fitness. PSO can exceed B0 in transition cash flow for some seeds, but winter/summer SoC failures and irregular switching (Figs.~\ref{fig:bal-w}--\ref{fig:soc-mat}) show that heuristic search on the twin is brittle. Enlarging the search to full action sequences would be combinatorially prohibitive under FMU evaluation cost.

\textbf{Implication for reinforcement learning.}
These baselines therefore play complementary diagnostic roles rather than ``weak straw men''. Rules prove that \emph{feasibility-first} operation already scores well; linprog shows that \emph{convexification-plus-mapping} is insufficient for CAES hybrids on a high-fidelity twin; PSO shows that \emph{low-dimensional market heuristics} cannot reliably navigate mode locks and weekly recovery. HMSD (and, to a lesser extent, single-layer TD3) instead optimises policies by direct interaction with the twin under GiveSafe, so credit is assigned to actions that remain executable. Hierarchy further supplies week-scale market and recovery intents (MSGP/MS-HER), repairing both the \emph{inaction} of under-trained single-layer agents and the \emph{infeasible aggression} of parametric search. The multi-seed lifts in Table~\ref{tab:main} should thus be read as gains over a strong rule and over method classes that are theoretically attractive but misaligned with the FMU hybrid decision structure---which is precisely where model-free hierarchical RL is most useful.

\paragraph{Remark on seed variance.}
Among RL seeds, evaluation-week rewards still differ because hybrid CAES modes induce multi-modal policies; hierarchy mainly stabilises transition/summer relative to TD3. Training-curve scatter (Fig.~\ref{fig:training}) should not be read as the primary multi-seed metric.

\paragraph{Surrogate weekly optimality gap.}
\label{sec:opt-gap}
A recurring Q1 question is how far closed-loop policies sit from an optimising programme. A non-convex hybrid FMU admits no cheap global optimum, so we report a \emph{transparent surrogate} rather than a false FMU dual. For each seasonal week we assemble perfect-foresight load, renewable availability and Shandong TOU prices from the twin logs, and solve a $168$\,h linear programme over continuous thermal power, battery/CAES-equivalent charge--discharge, and grid buy/sell, with box SOC dynamics, thermal ramps and a terminal inventory band matching the energy-SoC tolerance. The objective is a linear cash-flow proxy calibrated to twin economics (thermal O\&M $400$~CNY/MWh; renewable O\&M rates fitted on B0; must-take renewables; load revenue exogenous). Let $J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}$ be the LP optimum and $J_{\mathrm{surr}}(\pi)$ the \emph{same} proxy evaluated on a method's closed-loop trajectory. The gap
\[
\mathrm{gap}_{\mathrm{surr}}(\pi)=\frac{J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}-J_{\mathrm{surr}}(\pi)}{|J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}|}
\]
is therefore a true relative gap \emph{on one model}. Closed-loop FMU cash flow $J$ remains the engineering KPI and is \emph{not} claimed to be bounded by $J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}$.

Table~\ref{tab:gap} shows that HMSD recovers most of the surrogate value: winter $\mathrm{gap}_{\mathrm{surr}}\approx8\%$ ($J_{\mathrm{surr}}/J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}\approx0.92$), transition $\approx23\%$ and summer $\approx20\%$. Single-layer TD3 is farther in winter ($\approx32\%$) where a comparable log exists; B0 and rolling linprog leave $57$--$92\%$ of surrogate value on the table. Early-failed PSO weeks omit proxy rows. The residual gaps are consistent with hybrid mode locks, efficiency losses and GiveSafe rewrites absent from the continuous relaxation---i.e.\ the price of executability on the twin---while still placing hierarchical market-safe dispatch close to a favourable convex benchmark.

\paragraph{Ablation of MSGP, MS-HER and F-MLE.}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:abl} report \emph{matched-budget} multi-seed component ablations: full HMSD and three dropouts (MSGP, MS-HER, F-MLE) are each trained for $1.5\times10^{4}$ valid steps with seeds $\{0,1,2\}$ under the same FMU, TOU and weekly evaluation protocol. For each seed we average the three seasonal episode rewards, then report mean$\pm$std of those seed means; seasonal columns give mean$\pm$std over seeds and SoC pass counts $x/3$. These runs are \emph{diagnostic under a short budget} and are intentionally \emph{not} mixed with the main $3.5\times10^{4}$-step multi-seed ranking in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

All four variants remain far above single-layer TD3 on average short-budget weeks (overall mean seasonal $\Delta$ versus the paired TD3 seeds of roughly $+26$ to $+36$). At $1.5\times10^{4}$ steps, however, the full stack is \emph{not} the mean-reward maximiser: w/o F-MLE and w/o MSGP attain higher three-season means ($95.2\pm7.9$ and $94.3\pm7.7$) than full HMSD ($85.3\pm9.1$) or w/o MS-HER ($85.9\pm24.0$). The full run is pulled down by a single poor transition seed (high transition std); removing MS-HER yields the largest \emph{seed-to-seed} variance, including a near-collapse seed, so HER mainly stabilises learning rather than uniformly lifting the short-budget mean. Energy-SoC pass rates over the nine season--seed cells are $7/9$ (full, w/o MSGP), $8/9$ (w/o MS-HER) and $9/9$ (w/o F-MLE).

We interpret these diagnostics as follows. (i)~Short budget is not the deployment budget: market priors and F-MLE inject structure that can slow pure economic exploration early in training, so ablating them can raise 15k mean reward while the $3.5\times10^{4}$-step full stack remains the primary economic configuration (Table~\ref{tab:main}). (ii)~MS-HER should be read as a \emph{reliability} module under seed noise at fixed budget. (iii)~MSGP does not show a clean short-budget SoC advantage over full in this multi-seed table (both $7/9$); its value is better tied to recovery curricula and longer training rather than 15k mean reward. (iv)~F-MLE is consistent with a cold-start aid that online interaction can eventually replace. Overall, hierarchy still dominates TD3 under every dropout; the modules primarily shape variance, recovery and trainability rather than acting as short-horizon mean-reward additives.

\paragraph{Intent--dispatch decoupling (IDD).}
Goal-sensitivity diagnostics on absolute goal-conditioned actors with $\mathrm{obs\_norm}$ and $\kappa=4$ show non-vanishing action responses on all five goal dimensions (alive pathway). On the strongest seed (s1, summer week), freezing the low level to random feasible actions collapses weekly reward (full$-$freeze-low $\approx{+}49$), whereas freezing the high level to a pure market prior yields a smaller gap, supporting hierarchical decoupling rather than a single-layer lookalike.

\paragraph{GiveSafe feasibility and stress evidence.}
\label{sec:givesafe-stats}
GiveSafe is applied to both TD3 and HMSD in the main comparison, so hierarchical economic gains should not be attributed solely to the shield. Two complementary measurements separate its role. (i)~\emph{Offline footprint on nominal weeks.} Comparing continuous requested versus executed commands on seed~1 seasonal trajectories (Table~\ref{tab:givesafe}), HMSD requires adjustment on only about $14\%$ of steps with mean $\ell_2$ change $\approx0.13$, zero invalid transitions and zero FMU failures, and low CAES lock occupancy ($\approx8\%$). Single-layer TD3 triggers adjustments on about $66\%$ of steps with mean $\ell_2\approx0.71$, nonzero invalid transitions, occasional FMU failures and lock occupancy $\approx40\%$. Thus the same shield intervenes far more often under myopic policies. (ii)~\emph{Action-space stress.} Under strong exploration-like perturbations on a winter week (Gaussian action noise with $\sigma=0.4$ and $25\%$ random CAES mode flips), disabling GiveSafe causes episode collapse with weekly $J\approx0$ and invalid transitions, whereas enabling GiveSafe preserves $J\approx1.24\times10^{7}$~CNY (reward $\approx89.9$) with zero invalid transitions. Safety is therefore claimed as a \emph{pre-execution filter under distribution shift and exploration}, not as the source of peak--valley skill under nominal multi-seed ranking.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:support}

The scientific core of this work is \emph{market--inventory--feasibility coupling} on a hybrid FMU plant: maximise weekly cash flow $J$ under TOU while meeting energy-SoC recovery and executing only in the dynamic set $\mathcal{F}(s)$. Hierarchical TD3~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,fujimoto2018td3} and hindsight-style relabeling~\cite{andrychowicz2017her} supply temporal abstraction and off-policy credit tools; HMSD's mechanisms (market--inventory goals, market-weighted hindsight, feasibility-supported pretraining, causal goal pathways, and GiveSafe under stress) are designed for that coupling rather than for inventing a new actor--critic family. Relative to hydrogen-centric comprehensive-cost H-IES scheduling~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, the plant physics, hybrid CAES actions, cash-flow KPI and safety interface differ. Section~\ref{sec:why-or-pso} shows that rolling linprog and parametric PSO often fail to beat a conservative rule on the twin---a structural warning against surrogate optimisers. Feasibility statistics (Section~\ref{sec:givesafe-stats}) show that single-layer policies lean on the shield far more than HMSD, while noise stress tests justify GiveSafe as a pre-execution filter. The annual weekly-reset study (Section~\ref{sec:annual-a}) shows hierarchical gains across a full year of windows. Limitations include price-taker assumptions~\cite{emmanuel2020market}, temperate-year resource tables, volumetric TOU rather than nodal LMPs, the restricted PSO policy class, surrogate LP upper bounds rather than tight global optima, a $2/3$ summer SoC pass rate on the economic multi-seed table (mitigated by optional $\lambda$-SoC; Section~\ref{sec:lambda-sens}), short-budget ablation diagnostics that must be complemented by longer matched-budget ablations, and limited geographic generalisation.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

This paper addressed market--inventory--feasibility coupled scheduling of a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a Modelica--FMU twin under Shandong TOU tariffs. Hierarchical Market-Safe Dispatch (HMSD) combines market--inventory hierarchical intents, market-structured hindsight credit, feasibility-supported pretraining, causal goal pathways and GiveSafe filtering---using hierarchical TD3 as a controller family rather than as the novelty claim itself. Multi-seed closed-loop results show large gains over single-layer TD3 and over rules/classical optimisers that often lose to a conservative baseline on the twin; cash-flow $J$, seasonal dispatch and annual weekly-reset covers support engineering interpretability; GiveSafe statistics and stress tests support safety under distribution shift. Matched short-budget multi-seed ablations diagnose module roles (reliability versus mean reward). Surrogate weekly LP gaps place HMSD within about $8$--$23\%$ of a favourable convex cash-flow benchmark on the same proxy. Future work includes $3.5\times10^{4}$-step matched multi-seed ablations, tighter hybrid MILP relaxations, longer-budget SAC baselines, and multi-plantysis, stronger RL baselines (e.g.\ SAC), tariff-aligned macro-steps, continuous-year inventory carry and multi-region tariffs.
