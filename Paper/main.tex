\shorttitle{When hierarchy helps weekly CAES-plant dispatch}
\title[mode=title]{When hierarchy helps: weekly economic dispatch of a thermal--battery--CAES plant under nonconvex commands and a comprehensive monetary objective}

\begin{abstract}
Weekly price-taker dispatch of a thermal--battery--compressed-air (CAES) plant is not a standard box-constrained control task. CAES electric commands lie in a disconnected legal set; thermal units ramp; battery and gas inventories must recover at week end; and the monetary score couples time-of-use (TOU) cash with a China National ETS carbon charge, spill/unserved penalties and battery cycle wear. Convex programmes relax the legal set. Flat deep reinforcement learning (DRL) can interact with a physics twin, but a single timescale does not decide whether the week needs inventory transfer or sparse cycling. This paper states that problem on a MWORKS Sysplorer plant exported as an FMI functional mock-up unit (FMU) and \emph{proposes} Hierarchical Multi-energy Safe Dispatch (HMSD): a two-timescale decision algorithm, not a stack of optional extras. Every $c$ hours a high-level policy issues a two-dimensional inventory increment; a low-level policy maps the state--goal pair to a continuous device command, including a CAES scalar that is mapped onto the disconnected legal set. Both layers use TD3 only as the inner optimiser. The defining equations are that the low-level reward equals the plant's extrinsic comprehensive reward (the same $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$ as flat TD3) and that only $a\in\mathcal{F}(s)$ steps the FMU. Any winter/transition lift is then a property of the inventory-goal algorithm, not of a second economic task. On pre-specified seed-$0$ held-out weeks, hierarchy repairs the inaction of flat TD3 in winter and transition and beats a plant rule, parametric particle-swarm search and rolling linear programming. In summer the same inventory goals induce over-cycling: flat TD3 uses less battery throughput and less thermal energy and records a higher comprehensive cash score. The operational claim is therefore regime-dependent---use hierarchy when multi-day inventory is the bottleneck; do not assume it dominates a sparse flat policy in every season.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Weekly comprehensive monetary dispatch on a Sysplorer FMU: TOU cash, ETS carbon, spill/unserved energy, battery wear and an energy-SoC recovery gate, with CAES commands in a disconnected legal set.
\item Transfer: GHTD3-style inventory goals on a thermal--battery--CAES FMU; micro-change: both timescales use the same plant reward $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$ as flat TD3.
\item Principle: inventory increments have no wear coordinate, so hierarchy repairs winter inaction and over-cycles in summer---a regime, not uniform dominance.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =====================================================================

Deep decarbonisation and China's national carbon market couple electricity markets with abatement costs at multi-energy plants~\cite{zhou2023iesdrl,yang2024planning,icap2025chinaets}. Provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs create peak--valley incentives~\cite{ndrc2021tou,shandong2023tou}, while an emissions trading scheme (ETS) attaches a monetary charge to thermal output and grid imports. This paper studies \emph{plant-level price-taker operation} of a thermal--battery--CAES facility: maximise a \emph{comprehensive monetary objective}---weekly net cash flow net of ETS-priced carbon, curtailment/unserved penalties and battery cycle cost---subject to energy SoC recovery, rather than ISO market clearing~\cite{emmanuel2020market,pei2024pricetaker}. Hybrid continuous--discrete actions, CAES mode locks and thermal ramps render unconstrained plant interaction operationally unsafe and computationally demanding.

Model-based programmes remain useful baselines but typically require convexification of CAES thermodynamics or face restrictive budgets on hybrid spaces~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{sutton2018rl,fujimoto2018td3,haarnoja2018sac} learns closed-loop policies by interaction. Hierarchical goal-conditioned control is a natural fit when week-scale inventory recovery and carbon exposure coexist with hour-scale actuators. Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} develop GHTD3 for hydrogen-enhanced integrated energy systems: a high-level semi-MDP issues storage goals and a low-level goal-augmented MDP tracks them with an intrinsic reward. We propose \emph{Hierarchical Multi-energy Safe Dispatch} (HMSD) for the thermal--battery--CAES plant. TD3 remains the inner optimiser~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}. The algorithm is the coupling: inventory-increment actions, $r^{\mathrm{lo}}=r^{\mathrm{ext}}$ on both timescales, and a continuous CAES proposal mapped onto a disconnected legal set before each FMU step~\cite{garcia2015safe,sun2024constrained}.

Three gaps remain. (1)~\textbf{A thermal--CAES twin with a disconnected command set.} Hydrogen-centric hierarchical studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} do not treat price-taker cash plus ETS-priced carbon on a Sysplorer FMU whose CAES actuator is legally idle or in a compressor/expander band, never in between. (2)~\textbf{Same-objective hierarchy.} If the low-level critic tracks inventory while the flat baseline maximises money, a hierarchical win is not evidence that inventory goals help the plant. (3)~\textbf{When hierarchy is the wrong bias.} Inventory goals fix myopic inaction, but they also \emph{ask} the plant to move stock. Weeks in which sparse cycling is cheaper will punish that request; an energy paper should report the regime, not only the favourable seasons.

The contributions are a transfer, a micro-change, and a principle---not a new actor--critic family.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Transfer.} Goal-conditioned hierarchical TD3~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} is moved from a hydrogen IES to a thermal--battery--CAES Sysplorer FMU under Shandong TOU and a National ETS carbon price. The plant geometry is different: $z_{\mathrm{caes}}$ is mapped onto the disconnected legal set~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} and only $a\in\mathcal{F}(s)$ steps the twin. Weekly $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$ couples cash, carbon, spill/unserved energy and battery wear with an energy-SoC gate.
\item \textbf{Micro-innovation.} HMSD (Section~\ref{sec:hmsd-def}) keeps TD3 as the inner optimiser and inventory increments as the high-level action. The one equation that changes relative to GHTD3 is $r^{\mathrm{lo}}=r^{\mathrm{ext}}=R^{\mathrm{hi}}$ (block mean): the low level maximises the same plant score as flat TD3, so a hierarchical lift cannot be an artefact of intrinsic tracking. Optional budget coordinates (discharge / extra thermal) extend the same principle and are reported only after the corresponding runs finish.
\item \textbf{Principle.} An inventory increment has no wear coordinate. The same bias that repairs winter/transition inaction over-cycles the battery and the thermal unit in summer, where a sparse flat TD3 records a higher $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$. Hierarchy is therefore a seasonal operating rule, not a uniformly better controller.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Plant topology and modelling workflow}

Figure~\ref{fig:topology} summarises the multi-energy plant. The supply layer comprises wind, photovoltaic (PV) generation and a grid interface under TOU buy/sell prices. The conversion layer centres on a thermal unit commanded by a continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp}}$ and a CAES train commanded by discrete mode and continuous magnitude. The storage layer comprises a battery with continuous power command $u_{\mathrm{bat}}$ and CAES gas and thermal inventories (gas tank, hot and cold tanks). The demand layer balances electric load and grid exchange.

Plant dynamics follow first-principles multi-physics: thermal ramping and fuel use; electrochemical battery SoC with round-trip efficiency; multi-tank CAES gas storage with thermo-mechanical coupling and mode locks; renewable availability; and nodal power balance with curtailment and unserved residual. The complete Modelica model is constructed and parameterised in \textbf{MWORKS Sysplorer}, then exported as an FMI-compliant FMU. Closed-loop optimisers and learning agents interact with this FMU at a communication interval of one hour, reading plant states and writing hybrid set-points. Exogenous TOU prices and resource profiles provide market and boundary conditions without replacing the internal differential--algebraic dynamics.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

This section formulates the multi-energy plant physics, operating constraints and market objective that define the weekly scheduling problem. Continuous-time differential--algebraic equations of the plant (thermal unit, battery, multi-tank CAES, renewables and grid interface) are integrated in the Sysplorer-exported FMU with communication interval $\tau_s=1$~h; the relations below are the dispatch-level models realised by that twin and used by optimisers and learning agents. We adopt the following sign convention throughout: renewable and thermal generation are non-negative when injecting power; battery and CAES charge is positive and discharge is negative; grid import is positive. Component sets are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=\{\mathrm{w},\mathrm{pv}\},\quad
I_2=\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{caes}\},\quad
I_3=\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},\\
I&=I_1\cup I_2\cup I_3.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sets}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Energy generation units}

\subsubsection{Photovoltaics}

PV available power depends on plane-of-array irradiance $G_t$, module surface temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}$ and nameplate capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}=260$~MW. With standard irradiance $G_{\mathrm{stc}}=1000$~W/m$^2$, standard temperature $T_{\mathrm{stc}}=298.15$~K, temperature coefficient $K_T=0.005$~K$^{-1}$ and conversion efficiency $\eta^{\mathrm{pv}}=0.95$,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}
=
\max\Bigl(
P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}
\frac{G_t}{G_{\mathrm{stc}}}
\bigl[1-K_T\bigl(T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}-T_{\mathrm{stc}}\bigr)\bigr]
\eta^{\mathrm{pv}},\,0\Bigr).
\label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
Module temperature is obtained from ambient temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{air}}$, wind speed $v_t$ and irradiance by the twin correlation
\begin{equation}
T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}
=
T_t^{\mathrm{air}}
+
0.0138\bigl(1+0.031(T_t^{\mathrm{air}}-273.15)\bigr)
\bigl(1-0.042\,v_t\bigr)G_t.
\label{eq:pv-temp}
\end{equation}
Actual injected PV power after bus curtailment satisfies $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$.

\subsubsection{Wind turbines}

Wind available power follows a cubic power curve with cut-in, rated and cut-out speeds $v_{\mathrm{ci}}=3$~m/s, $v_{\mathrm{r}}=10$~m/s and $v_{\mathrm{co}}=15$~m/s on capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{w}}=300$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}
=
\begin{cases}
0, & 0\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{ci}}\ \text{or}\ v_t\ge v_{\mathrm{co}},\\[2pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}\,\dfrac{v_t^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}}{v_{\mathrm{r}}^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}},
& v_{\mathrm{ci}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{r}},\\[6pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}, & v_{\mathrm{r}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{co}}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:wt}
\end{equation}
Turbine pitch is not a free decision variable. Actual wind injection obeys $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}$.

\subsection{Energy conversion units}

\subsubsection{Thermal generation}

Thermal electric power is commanded by the continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp},t}$:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{th}} = u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}},
\qquad
u_{\min}\le u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\le u_{\max},
\label{eq:th}
\end{equation}
with $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=150$~MW, $P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}=50$~MW, $u_{\min}=P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}/P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=1/3$ and $u_{\max}=1$ (no shut-down). Inter-hour ramping is enforced on the actual thermal power,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|P_t^{\mathrm{th}}-P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{th}}\bigr|
\le
\Delta_{\mathrm{th}}^{\max}
=
r_{\max}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}\,\tau_s,
\label{eq:th-ramp}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\max}=0.0025/60$~s$^{-1}$ is the Modelica load-rate limit (equivalently $0.15$~p.u.\ per hour). Fuel and O\&M costs enter $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ in~\eqref{eq:dJ}.

\subsubsection{CAES conversion}

CAES is a hybrid conversion--storage plant. The hybrid command comprises a discrete mode and continuous magnitude
\begin{equation}
m_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\},\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:caes-cmd}
\end{equation}
mapped to a signed electric power command on capacity $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}=150$~MW,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
=
\begin{cases}
+\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{charge},\\
0, & m=\mathrm{idle},\\
-\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{discharge},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:caes-p}
\end{equation}
or equivalently $P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}=u_{\mathrm{caes},t}P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}$ with the non-convex legal set
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm{caes},t}
\in
[-1,-0.33]\cup\{0\}\cup[0.86,1]
\label{eq:caes-legal}
\end{equation}
encoding minimum compressor/expander loading. Mode locks and minimum-run dwell constraints further restrict admissible transitions of $m_{\mathrm{caes}}$. Electric power is realised as planned ($P^{\mathrm{act}}=P^{\mathrm{plan}}$); mass-flow set-points of air and thermal fluid scale with $|P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}|$ and reverse direction between compression and expansion.

\subsection{Energy storage units}

\subsubsection{Battery}

Battery power is commanded by $u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\in[-1,1]$ on $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}=100$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{bat}} = u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}.
\label{eq:bat-p}
\end{equation}
With energy capacity $E_{\mathrm{cap}}=500$~MWh and discharge efficiency $\eta=0.85$ (charge path lossless as in the twin), the SoC dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{\mathrm{bat}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=
\begin{cases}
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/E_{\mathrm{cap}}, & P^{\mathrm{bat}}\ge 0,\\[2pt]
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/(E_{\mathrm{cap}}\,\eta), & P^{\mathrm{bat}}<0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bat-soc}
\end{equation}
or, in hourly form with $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$ and $[x]_-=\max(-x,0)$,
\begin{equation}
s_{t+1,\mathrm{bat}}
=
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}}
+
\tau_s\frac{[P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_+ - [P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_-/\eta}{E_{\mathrm{cap}}}.
\label{eq:bat-soc-d}
\end{equation}
Inventory bounds are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}\in[0.1,0.9]$.

\subsubsection{CAES inventories}

CAES energy is stored as compressed air and two thermal fluid tanks. Gas SoC is pressure-based with rating $p_{\mathrm{norm}}=100$~bar,
\begin{equation}
s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}
=
\frac{p_t}{p_{\mathrm{norm}}},
\qquad
\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}}\le s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}\le \overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},
\label{eq:gas-soc}
\end{equation}
with $(\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},\overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}})=(0.6,1.0)$. Fixed-volume gas-tank mass and energy balances (ideal-air medium) evolve pressure $p$ and specific enthalpy $h$ under port mass flows $\dot m_{\mathrm{a}},\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}$ and wall heat $q_{\mathrm{w}}$,
\begin{align}
V\bigl(\partial_p\rho\,\dot p+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b}},
\label{eq:gas-mass}\\
V\bigl(-\dot p+\partial_p\rho\,\dot p\,h+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\,h+\rho\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}h_{\mathrm{a}}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}h_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathrm{out}}
+
q_{\mathrm{w}},
\label{eq:gas-energy}
\end{align}
with $q_{\mathrm{w}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{w}}S(T_{\mathrm{w}}-T)$ and gas temperature $T=T(p,h)$ (clipped at $253$~K). Hot and cold tanks store water at fixed pressure with free surface area $A$ and total volume $V_0=1.5\times10^{4}$~m$^3$. Level-based inventories
\begin{equation}
s_{t,i}
=
\frac{\ell_{t,i}}{V_0/A},
\qquad
i\in\{\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:thermal-soc}
\end{equation}
obey mass and enthalpy balances when within $(\underline{s}_i,\overline{s}_i)=(0.05,0.95)$,
\begin{equation}
\dot m_i=\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i},\qquad
m_i\dot h_i
=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}h_{\mathrm{a},i}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i}h_{\mathrm{b},i}^{\mathrm{out}}+Q_{\mathrm{gen},i}.
\label{eq:tank-bal}
\end{equation}
Compression ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}>0$) charges the gas inventory and drives the thermal circuit one way; expansion ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}<0$) reverses the flows. Primary weekly recovery inventories are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}$ and $s_{\mathrm{gas}}$; hot/cold SoCs and temperatures $(T^{\mathrm{hot}},T^{\mathrm{cold}})$ are process states used in hierarchical goals and shaping. All inventories obey~\eqref{eq:socbox}.

\subsection{System operation constraints}

The electric bus closes the power balance with curtailment and unserved load. Let $P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$ be renewable availability and $P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}$ the scheduled demand. Residual power after dispatchable assets is absorbed first by curtailing wind, then PV; residual deficit is met by demand response down to $20\%$ of plan, with remainder counted as unserved:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}}+P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}+P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
+P_t^{\mathrm{w}}+P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}+P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}
=
P_t^{\mathrm{load}},\\
&0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},\quad
0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}\ge 0,\quad
P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}\ge 0,\quad
0.2\,P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
Here $P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}$ is residual surplus after wind-then-PV curtailment and $P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}$ is residual shortfall after load reduction. Box and ramp limits, inventory bounds and grid exchange limits are
\begin{equation}
\underline{P}_{i}\le P_t^{i}\le \overline{P}_{i},\qquad
\bigl|P_t^{i}-P_{t-1}^{i}\bigr|\le \Delta_i^{\max},
\quad i\in\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{bat}\},
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\underline{s}_{i}\le s_{t,i}\le \overline{s}_{i},\qquad i\in\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:socbox}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min}\le P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}\le P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max},
\label{eq:grid}
\end{equation}
with $(P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min},P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max})=(-500,500)$~MW. Actions that would violate~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:grid},~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} or CAES mode locks lie outside the dynamic feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

Weekly operation records an energy-SoC recovery score on primary inventories, following the soft indicator of~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} rather than a hard action override,
\begin{equation}
L_1^{\mathrm{e}}
= \bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{bat}}-s_{0,\mathrm{bat}}\bigr|
+ \bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{gas}}-s_{0,\mathrm{gas}}\bigr|
\le \varepsilon,
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon=0.06$. Operating boxes remain $s_{\mathrm{bat}}\in[0.1,0.9]$. The battery command is never rewritten at week-end; only the terminal bonus in~\eqref{eq:term} encourages return to the initial inventory.

\subsection{Optimization objectives}

Let $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ index hours in a weekly episode ($T=168$). Grid purchase/sale energies are $E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_+$ and $E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_-$ (MWh). Device operating costs $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ aggregate thermal fuel/O\&M and storage energy-bookkeeping cash increments exported by the twin (battery cycle life is not in Modelica income; see $C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}$). Under price-taker TOU prices $\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$, the hourly \emph{cash} increment is
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{cash}}
=
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}} E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}
-
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{op}}.
\label{eq:dJ}
\end{equation}
Step CO$_2$ mass uses emission factors $\eta_{\mathrm{th}},\eta_{\mathrm{grid}}$ (tCO$_2$/MWh) on thermal energy and grid imports,
\begin{equation}
\Delta m_t
=
\eta_{\mathrm{th}} E_t^{\mathrm{th}}
+
\eta_{\mathrm{grid}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}},
\label{eq:co2}
\end{equation}
with carbon priced at a China National ETS operating point $\pi_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=80$~CNY/tCO$_2$e (secondary-market band spanning the 2025 ICAP average and 2024 year-end closes)~\cite{icap2025chinaets}. Carbon is treated as an external ETS-style charge rather than a second fuel bill. Curtailment and unserved energies $E_t^{\mathrm{curt}},E_t^{\mathrm{uns}}$ follow bus residuals and enter a Cui-style spill cost~\cite{cui2025ghtd3},
\begin{equation}
C_t^{CUT}
=
\nu_{\mathrm{curt}} E_t^{\mathrm{curt}}
+
\nu_{\mathrm{uns}} E_t^{\mathrm{uns}},
\label{eq:cut}
\end{equation}
with operating points $\nu_{\mathrm{curt}}=300$~CNY/MWh and $\nu_{\mathrm{uns}}=1000$~CNY/MWh. Battery \emph{cycle} cost is accounted outside pure energy bookkeeping: device-level battery income in the plant model reflects energy transfer only, whereas capital wear is represented by a convex cumulative-discharge term $\psi(\delta)=a_0\delta^{p}$ with $p\approx 2.03$~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,cui2024caesbess}. We calibrate $a_0$ so that lifetime discharge exhausts capital cost (LCOS-style amortisation~\cite{xu2022lcos}). Let $\delta_t$ be episode cumulative \emph{discharge} energy (MWh; charge does not increase $\delta$) and $\delta_0=\rho E_{\mathrm{life}}$ a mid-life offset ($\rho=0.25$) so that weekly episodes are not stuck near $\delta=0$, where the marginal cost $a_0 p\delta^{p-1}$ vanishes. With lifetime discharge $E_{\mathrm{life}}=N_{\mathrm{cycle}}\mathrm{DoD}_{\mathrm{eq}}E_{\mathrm{cap}}$ and total capital $\mathrm{Capex}=c_{\mathrm{kWh}}E_{\mathrm{cap}}$,
\begin{equation}
\psi(\delta)=a_0\,\delta^{p},\quad
a_0=\frac{\mathrm{Capex}}{E_{\mathrm{life}}^{p}},\quad
p=2.03,\quad
C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}
=
\psi(\delta_0+\delta_t)-\psi(\delta_0+\delta_{t-1}),
\label{eq:deg}
\end{equation}
hence $\psi(E_{\mathrm{life}})=\mathrm{Capex}$ and degradation becomes strictly more expensive as cumulative discharge grows. Default plant-scale numbers match the battery capacity ($E_{\mathrm{cap}}=400$~MWh) with a stationary LFP-class economic point $c_{\mathrm{kWh}}=1000$~CNY/kWh, $N_{\mathrm{cycle}}=5000$ and $\mathrm{DoD}_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.8$~\cite{xu2022lcos}. The comprehensive monetary objective is
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}
=
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{cash}}
-
\pi_{\mathrm{CO_2}}\,\Delta m_t
-
C_t^{CUT}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{deg}},
\qquad
\max_{\{a_t\}}\; J^{\mathrm{gen}} = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:pv}\text{--}\eqref{eq:soc},\
a_t\in\mathcal{F}(s_t).
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
Hour-scale hybrid actions thus couple to week-scale inventory recovery, carbon exposure, renewable spill and storage wear---the multi-timescale structure hierarchical RL targets. Policies are ranked by calibrated episode reward under~\eqref{eq:J} and gate~\eqref{eq:soc} (Section~\ref{sec:reward}).

\subsection{Hybrid-action MDP}

Closed-loop control is cast as an MDP $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma\rangle$ with $\gamma=0.99$. The state concatenates inventories, thermo states, powers, prices and TOU features,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_t=\bigl[&
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}},s_{t,\mathrm{gas}},s_{t,\mathrm{hot}},s_{t,\mathrm{cold}},
p_t,T_t^{\mathrm{gas}},T_t^{\mathrm{hot}},T_t^{\mathrm{cold}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}},P_t^{\mathrm{bat}},P_t^{\mathrm{caes}},P_t^{\mathrm{grid}},
P_t^{\mathrm{w}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv}},P_t^{\mathrm{load}},
P_t^{\mathrm{curt}},P_t^{\mathrm{uns}},\\
&\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\ \text{TOU band features},\ \text{optional forecast residuals}
\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
and the device action is the continuous triple
\begin{equation}
a_t = \bigl(u_{\mathrm{tp},t},\, u_{\mathrm{bat},t},\, z_{\mathrm{caes},t}\bigr)\in\mathcal{A},
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with $u_{\mathrm{tp}}$ in the thermal load-rate box, $u_{\mathrm{bat}}\in[-1,1]$ and $z_{\mathrm{caes}}\in[-1,1]$. The CAES channel is mapped to a legal command $u_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in[-1,-0.33]\cup\{0\}\cup[0.86,1]$ (mode and magnitude are derived, not independently issued). Transitions are induced by one plant hour that integrates~\eqref{eq:pv}--\eqref{eq:tank-bal} under feasible actions $a_t\in\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

\subsection{Reward function for learning}
\label{sec:reward}

With calibration constant $C_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx1.565\times10^{5}$~CNY (rule-controller $p_{95}$ step cash magnitude),
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{econ}} = \frac{\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}}{C_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
Potential-based inventory shaping uses the weighted L1 distance $L_{1,t}$ of (battery, gas, hot, cold) relative to episode start:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}
= \kappa_t \bigl(L_{1,t-1}-L_{1,t}\bigr)
- \kappa_t^{\mathrm{abs}} L_{1,t},
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
with base $\kappa=5$ and a recovery-window scale-up over the last $H=40$~h. At a completed, failure-free terminal step the Cui-style indicator is
\begin{equation}
r_T^{\mathrm{term}}
=
\begin{cases}
B, & L_1^{\mathrm{e}}\le\varepsilon,\\
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:term}
\end{equation}
with $B=15$. Missing the gate costs the bonus only; it does not apply a proportional fail penalty and does not invalidate the week. The extrinsic reward is the sum of the three terms
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{ext}}
= r_t^{\mathrm{econ}}+r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}+r_t^{\mathrm{term}}.
\label{eq:rext}
\end{equation}
Classical programmes must convexify~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} and the CAES thermo-mechanical coupling; HMSD (Section~\ref{sec:method}) optimises~\eqref{eq:J} by closed-loop interaction with the plant twin.

\label{sec:method}

We propose HMSD as one algorithm: two MDPs, one plant reward, one legal-set map. TD3 is only the inner optimiser~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}. Figure~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarises the loop; Section~\ref{sec:hmsd-def} writes the defining equations.

\subsection{Two augmented MDPs}

A standard one-step MDP is insufficient for week-scale inventory planning. Following~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, we use a high-level semi-MDP (SMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{G},P_c,R_c,\gamma^c\rangle$ that issues goals every $c$ steps, and a low-level goal-augmented MDP (GAMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G},P,R,\gamma\rangle$ that executes device actions conditioned on the residual goal. The high-level action is the inventory increment
\begin{equation}
g=\bigl[g^{\Delta\mathrm{bat}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{gas}}\bigr]^{\top}
\in
[-0.30,0.30]\times[-0.12,0.12].
\label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
Low-level actions remain~\eqref{eq:action}. Restricting $g$ to the two primary energy inventories (battery and CAES gas) keeps the goal causally tied to the SoC gate~\eqref{eq:soc} and avoids unused thermal/arbitrage channels that would collapse the hierarchy toward a flat actor.

\subsection{Hierarchy of decision making}

Every $c$ steps, the high-level policy $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$ observes $s_t$ and issues $g_t$. For $i\in[t,t+c-1]$, the low-level policy $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ maps $(s_i,g_i)$ to a device action, and residual goals are updated by
\begin{equation}
g_{i+1}=h(s_i,g_i,s_{i+1})
= s_i^{\mathrm{int}}+g_i-s_{i+1}^{\mathrm{int}},
\label{eq:h}
\end{equation}
where $s^{\mathrm{int}}=[s_{\mathrm{bat}},s_{\mathrm{gas}}]$ is the inventory subvector tracked by $g$.

\subsection{The proposed algorithm}
\label{sec:hmsd-def}

HMSD is the tuple
$\bigl(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{hi}},\,\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{lo}},\,\Phi,\,\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}\bigr)$.
The high-level object is the SMDP
$\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{hi}}=\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{G},P_c,R^{\mathrm{hi}},\gamma^{c}\rangle$
with action $g$ in~\eqref{eq:goal}.
The low-level object is the GAMDP
$\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{lo}}=\langle\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{G},\mathcal{A},P,r^{\mathrm{lo}},\gamma\rangle$
with action~\eqref{eq:action}.
The two maps that make the algorithm plant-legal are the CAES embedding $\Phi:[-1,1]\to[-1,-0.33]\cup\{0\}\cup[0.86,1]$ and the feasible-set projector $\Pi_{\mathcal{F}}$. The \emph{same-objective} coupling, which distinguishes HMSD from GHTD3~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, is
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{lo}} = r_t^{\mathrm{ext}}, \qquad
R_k^{\mathrm{hi}} = \frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1} r_{t+j}^{\mathrm{ext}},
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with $c=8$. GHTD3 trains the low level on an intrinsic tracking reward $-\|e\|+\alpha r^{\mathrm{ext}}$. HMSD does not: both timescales maximise the plant score~\eqref{eq:rext}. Residual goals~\eqref{eq:h} are a state transform, not a second objective. High-level bootstrapping uses $\gamma^{c}$. Inner updates remain TD3 (Section~\ref{sec:td3-inner}).

The two-dimensional definition above has no wear coordinate, so a week that prefers sparse cycling can beat HMSD with a flat policy on the same $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$. An optional budgeted extension issues remaining allowances that count down inside the block: battery SoC discharge $g^{\mathrm{wear}}\in[0,0.50]$ (HMSD-W) and, if enabled, extra thermal load $g^{\mathrm{th}}\in[0,1]$ (HMSD-B). Overshoot is a light penalty; $r^{\mathrm{lo}}$ remains $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$. Winter/summer tests of W are running; B waits for GPU slack. Neither is mixed into the two-dimensional mainline table until those evaluations exist.

\subsection{Internal controllers (TD3)}
\label{sec:td3-inner}

Both layers use TD3~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}. Write $x^{\mathrm{hi}}=s$, $a^{\mathrm{hi}}=g$ and $x^{\mathrm{lo}}=[s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g]$, $a^{\mathrm{lo}}=a$, with observation normalisation $s_{\mathrm{n}}=\tanh(s/s_0)$ and goal scale $\kappa=4$. Each layer maintains twin critics $Q_{\theta_1},Q_{\theta_2}$, actor $\mu_{\phi}$ and target networks. Critic losses and targets are
\begin{equation}
L(\theta_j)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(Q_{\theta_j}(x,a)-y\bigr)^2\bigr],\quad j=1,2,
\label{eq:td3-L}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
y=r+\gamma\min_{k=1,2}Q_{\bar\theta_k}\bigl(x',\tilde a'\bigr),
\qquad
\tilde a'=\mu_{\bar\phi}(x')+\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-\epsilon,\epsilon\bigr).
\label{eq:td3-y}
\end{equation}
The actor follows the deterministic policy gradient
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\phi}J
=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}
\Bigl[
\nabla_a Q_{\theta_1}(x,a)\big|_{a=\mu_{\phi}(x)}
\nabla_{\phi}\mu_{\phi}(x)
\Bigr],
\label{eq:td3-pi}
\end{equation}
with delayed actor updates and soft targets $\bar\psi\leftarrow\tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi$.

\subsection{Feasible-set projection}

Executable actions are projected onto the dynamic feasible set before each plant step~\cite{garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained}:
\begin{equation}
a_t^{\star}
= \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s_t)}\!\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}\bigr).
\label{eq:givesafe}
\end{equation}
The projector enforces thermal ramps and boxes, battery bounds, CAES mode locks and the disconnected legal set~\eqref{eq:caes-legal}. Only an accepted command advances the FMU. This is an execution constraint, not a claimed economic module.

\subsection{Policy learning}

Algorithms~\ref{alg:hmsd}--\ref{alg:td3-update} summarise hierarchical interaction and internal TD3 updates; Table~\ref{tab:hyper} lists main hyperparameters.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{HMSD hierarchical policy learning}
\label{alg:hmsd}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require High/low actors $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}},\mu^{\mathrm{lo}}$ and twin critics with targets; buffers $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}},\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; exploration coefficient $\varepsilon\leftarrow\varepsilon_0$
\For{episode $=1,2,\ldots,E_{\max}$}
  \State Reset plant twin on a training-week start; $s\leftarrow s_0$; clear residual goal; $s_{\mathrm{prev}}\leftarrow s$
  \For{$t=0$ to $T-1$}
    \If{$t \bmod c = 0$}
      \State With probability $1-\varepsilon$: $g\leftarrow\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}(s)$; otherwise sample $g$ uniformly in the goal box
      \State Record block start $s^{\mathrm{blk}}\leftarrow s$, $g^{\mathrm{blk}}\leftarrow g$
    \Else
      \State $g\leftarrow h(s_{\mathrm{prev}},g,s)$ \Comment{residual goal~\eqref{eq:h}}
    \EndIf
    \State $\tilde a\leftarrow\mu^{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}$; $a\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}(\tilde a)$ \Comment{\eqref{eq:givesafe}}
    \If{$a$ is infeasible}
      \State \textbf{continue} \Comment{do not step the plant}
    \EndIf
    \State Apply $a$ to the plant twin; observe $s'$, $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$ via~\eqref{eq:rext}
    \State $g'\leftarrow h(s,g,s')$; store $(s,g,a,r^{\mathrm{ext}},s',g')$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$
    \If{$t \bmod c = c-1$}
      \State Store high-level transition $(s^{\mathrm{blk}},g^{\mathrm{blk}},R^{\mathrm{hi}},s')$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$ with $R^{\mathrm{hi}}$ from~\eqref{eq:reward}
    \EndIf
    \State Sample a mini-batch from $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; update the low-level controller (Algorithm~\ref{alg:td3-update}, discount $\gamma$)
    \If{$t \bmod c = 0$ \textbf{and} $|\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}|$ is sufficient}
      \State Update the high-level controller (Algorithm~\ref{alg:td3-update}, discount $\gamma^{c}$)
    \EndIf
    \State $s_{\mathrm{prev}}\leftarrow s$; $s\leftarrow s'$
  \EndFor
  \State $\varepsilon\leftarrow\max(\varepsilon_{\min},\varepsilon-\Delta\varepsilon)$
\EndFor
\Ensure Learned policies $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$, $\mu^{\mathrm{lo}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Internal TD3 controller update (high or low level)}
\label{alg:td3-update}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Mini-batch $\mathcal{B}$; networks $(\mu_{\phi},Q_{\theta_1},Q_{\theta_2})$ and targets $(\mu_{\bar\phi},Q_{\bar\theta_1},Q_{\bar\theta_2})$; discount $\Gamma\in\{\gamma,\gamma^{c}\}$
\For{each transition $(x,a,r,x')\in\mathcal{B}$}
  \State $\tilde a'\leftarrow\mu_{\bar\phi}(x')+\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-\epsilon,\epsilon\bigr)$
  \State $y\leftarrow r+\Gamma\min_{k=1,2}Q_{\bar\theta_k}(x',\tilde a')$
\EndFor
\State $\theta_j\leftarrow\theta_j-\alpha_{Q}\nabla_{\theta_j}L(\theta_j)$ for $j=1,2$ with $L$ in~\eqref{eq:td3-L}
\If{update index is a multiple of the policy delay $d$}
  \State $\phi\leftarrow\phi+\alpha_{\mu}\nabla_{\phi}J$ with $J$ in~\eqref{eq:td3-pi}
  \State Soft-update targets: $\bar\psi\leftarrow\tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi$ for $\psi\in\{\phi,\theta_1,\theta_2\}$
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Simulation results}

\subsection{Simulation settings}

Three representative weeks (winter, transition and summer) are evaluated on the plant twin under identical TOU and resource tables, following the seasonal protocol of hierarchical multi-energy studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Each season uses a pool of training weeks and a held-out evaluation week on the same 8760~h FMU calendar: winter trains on weeks $0$--$4$ and evaluates week~$5$; transition trains on weeks $13$--$17$ and evaluates week~$18$; summer trains on weeks $26$--$30$ and evaluates week~$31$. Episodes last $T=168$~h with energy SoC re-initialised at each reset. All reported numbers use a \emph{pre-specified} seed~$0$. Three additional seeds were started for engineering checks; they are not averaged here, because that sample cannot support a significance claim and picking the best seed would be selection bias.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Conservative plant rule B0 (high thermal commitment, storage largely idle); price-aware peak--valley rule B1; rolling linprog on an eight-hour linear surrogate (first step executed on the FMU); six-parameter peak--valley PSO (12 particles, 25 iterations, each particle scored by a full FMU week); single-layer \textbf{TD3} with the same action space, network width and safety filter as the HMSD low level, trained without goals; single-layer \textbf{SAC} with a clamped temperature as a maximum-entropy counterpart; and a \textbf{GHTD3-style} counterpart on the same FMU and weeks (inventory goals and a low-level \emph{intrinsic} tracking reward as in~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}). HMSD versus that counterpart isolates $r^{\mathrm{lo}}=r^{\mathrm{ext}}$. GiveSafe stays on for every learning agent because the twin cannot execute illegal CAES commands.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Primary metrics are the calibrated episode reward under~\eqref{eq:J}, weekly comprehensive cash $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$, operating cash $J^{\mathrm{cash}}$, the energy-SoC gate~\eqref{eq:soc}, thermal energy and storage throughput. Unserved energy is zero on all completed seed-$0$ weeks reported below.

\paragraph{Experimental setting.}
Actor--critic updates run on a GPU workstation; the plant advances through the Sysplorer FMU at one-hour steps. Learning agents are trained for $E_{\max}=5\times10^{3}$ episodes ($\approx 8.4\times10^{5}$ valid steps). HMSD hyperparameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}.

\subsubsection{Scheduling performance}

Closed-loop dispatch is examined for seed~$0$ on the three held-out weeks, following~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Figures~\ref{fig:bal-w}--\ref{fig:bal-s} compare electric power balances of HMSD, TD3, parametric PSO and rolling linprog; Fig.~\ref{fig:soc-mat} summarises battery and CAES gas SoC.

\paragraph{Winter.}
Figure~\ref{fig:bal-w} shows that HMSD couples a reduced thermal schedule ($9.70\times10^{3}$~MWh versus $2.52\times10^{4}$~MWh under B0) with large battery throughput ($7.50\times10^{3}$~MWh) and a structured grid exchange. Flat TD3 essentially idles: thermal energy falls to $3.98\times10^{2}$~MWh and battery throughput to $1.88\times10^{2}$~MWh, so the week fails the SoC gate ($L_1=0.60$). This is the same ``inaction'' mode reported for single-layer TD3 in~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Parametric PSO terminates after 19 valid hours. Rolling linprog keeps thermal output near full load and misses the SoC gate by a small margin ($L_1=0.062$).

\paragraph{Transition.}
In the transition week (Fig.~\ref{fig:bal-t}), HMSD again uses storage heavily (battery $9.25\times10^{3}$~MWh) and attains the best comprehensive cash among completed policies. It misses the energy-SoC gate ($L_1=0.080$). TD3 again under-acts (thermal $1.35\times10^{3}$~MWh). PSO scores a similar cash-flow magnitude but runs only 144 valid hours and also fails recovery. Linprog stays near full thermal output and returns a negative $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$.

\paragraph{Summer.}
Under the summer week (Fig.~\ref{fig:bal-s}) the ranking reverses on money. Both HMSD and TD3 pass the SoC gate, but TD3 uses much less battery ($6.44\times10^{2}$ versus $4.33\times10^{3}$~MWh) and less thermal energy ($9.03\times10^{3}$ versus $1.56\times10^{4}$~MWh). Hierarchy therefore does \emph{not} automatically improve summer economics: extra cycling raises carbon and degradation charges. PSO again fails to finish the week; linprog holds thermal at the 25200~MWh ceiling.

\subsubsection{Cost performance}

Table~\ref{tab:main} ranks seed-$0$ held-out weeks by calibrated reward and comprehensive cash $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$, in the same spirit as the seasonal cost table of~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Figure~\ref{fig:j} summarises $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:cumj} shows cumulative operating cash; Fig.~\ref{fig:costc} decomposes buy/sell and device terms.

In winter, HMSD attains $R=113.6$ and $J^{\mathrm{gen}}=1.599\times10^{7}$~CNY with a passed SoC gate, versus $R=-1.26$ and $J^{\mathrm{gen}}=2.92\times10^{5}$~CNY for TD3. SAC is the strongest flat learner ($R=102.9$) but still trails hierarchy. B0 is a feasible but expensive full-load schedule ($J^{\mathrm{gen}}=6.27\times10^{6}$~CNY). Linprog and PSO both fail recovery; PSO stops after 19~h.

In transition, HMSD remains best on money ($R=50.1$, $J^{\mathrm{gen}}=8.43\times10^{6}$~CNY) but misses the SoC gate ($L_1=0.080$). PSO is close on cash and also fails the gate. TD3 and linprog are not operationally useful.

In summer the comparison must be read the other way. TD3 records $R=97.9$ and $J^{\mathrm{gen}}=1.386\times10^{7}$~CNY; HMSD records $R=61.2$ and $J^{\mathrm{gen}}=7.94\times10^{6}$~CNY; both pass the gate. SAC lies between them. The gap is not a missing SoC bonus: HMSD pays about $1.48\times10^{6}$~CNY of external carbon-plus-degradation charges against $7.24\times10^{5}$~CNY for TD3, because it cycles the battery $4.33\times10^{3}$~MWh and burns $1.56\times10^{4}$~MWh of thermal energy. Hierarchy here over-uses storage relative to a sparse, high-value flat policy. We treat that as a residual limitation, not as a result to be repaired by stacking extra modules after seeing the table.

\paragraph{Regime-dependent value of inventory goals.}
\label{sec:regime}
Table~\ref{tab:mech} decomposes the two learning policies that finish the week. Unserved energy is zero in every completed seed-$0$ row; the ranking is not a reliability artefact. Winter and transition are \emph{inventory-scarce}: flat TD3 leaves thermal energy at $4.0\times10^{2}$ and $1.35\times10^{3}$~MWh and fails the SoC gate, while HMSD cuts thermal output relative to the B0 ceiling of $2.52\times10^{4}$~MWh and uses the battery as a multi-day buffer. Comprehensive cash then follows the inventory motion.

Summer is \emph{wear-scarce}. Both policies pass the gate. HMSD still issues inventory increments, so the battery throughput is $4.33\times10^{3}$~MWh against $6.44\times10^{2}$~MWh for TD3, and thermal energy is $1.56\times10^{4}$ against $9.03\times10^{3}$~MWh. External charges (carbon plus cycle wear) are $1.48\times10^{6}$~CNY against $7.24\times10^{5}$~CNY. The high-level action is an increment of stock, not a budget on how much wear that increment may cost; weeks in which sparse cycling is cheaper therefore punish the hierarchical request. We treat that as the main methodological limit of two-dimensional inventory goals, not as a missing replay module. Figure~\ref{fig:mech} shows the same split on thermal energy and battery throughput.

\paragraph{Why PSO and rolling linprog trail HMSD.}
\label{sec:why-or-pso}
Neither classical baseline searches the same object as HMSD. PSO tunes a six-dimensional peak--valley heuristic (price thresholds, storage magnitudes, thermal bias, recovery strength). Each particle is scored by one FMU week; winter seed~$0$ dies after 19~h, so the swarm never sees a completed legal trajectory. The searchable class is far smaller than the hourly MDP, which is why PSO can look competitive on a partial transition week and still fail the SoC gate. Rolling linprog optimises an eight-hour linear surrogate with a relaxed CAES power, then maps the first step onto the legal set. The surrogate misses mode locks, thermal inventories and the comprehensive $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$ terms, so closed-loop thermal energy stays near the 25200~MWh ceiling and $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$ stays near or below B0. These methods are diagnostic baselines, not straw men and not global bounds on the FMU.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:support}

Table~\ref{tab:main} should be read as a Cui-style single-run seasonal comparison, not as a statistical claim. The scientific content is the \emph{regime} in Table~\ref{tab:mech}, not a universal ranking.

In inventory-scarce weeks, a flat actor with the same $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$ idles: it avoids wear and also avoids making money. Inventory goals supply the missing week-scale request and HMSD recovers most of $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$. In the wear-scarce summer week the same request is the wrong bias: the plant is already able to pass the SoC gate with little cycling, and extra thermal energy plus battery throughput appear as carbon and $C^{\mathrm{deg}}$. Classical search (Section~\ref{sec:why-or-pso}) does not close this gap. B0 proves that a feasible full-load week already scores; PSO and an eight-hour linear surrogate do not navigate legal CAES bands and weekly recovery.

The paper therefore does not propose extra replay or market-prior modules. The limit is the high-level action itself---an increment of stock without a wear budget---and it is already visible in the summer row of Table~\ref{tab:mech}.

Limitations are those of a price-taker plant study: a single ETS operating point, temperate-year resources, a weekly-reset protocol, and a pre-specified seed rather than a large multi-seed test.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

Weekly thermal--battery--CAES dispatch under TOU prices and a National ETS carbon price is a comprehensive monetary problem on a disconnected CAES legal set. We stated it on a Sysplorer FMU and used same-objective hierarchical TD3 so that inventory goals can be compared with flat TD3 on one reward. Hierarchy repairs winter and transition inaction. It over-cycles in summer, where a sparse flat policy records a higher $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$. The contribution is that regime, together with an executable nonconvex command model---not a larger actor--critic family. Future work is a high-level wear budget on the same twin, tighter hybrid programmes, and further tariff and weather years.
