\shorttitle{Safe Market-GHTD3 for multi-energy plant scheduling}
\title[mode=title]{Safe market-oriented hierarchical TD3 for hybrid multi-energy plant scheduling under time-of-use tariffs}

\begin{abstract}
Provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs create strong peak--valley incentives for plant-level storage, yet coordinating thermal generation, battery and compressed-air energy storage (CAES) remains a hybrid-action, multi-timescale problem with hard operational constraints. Unlike cost-minimising multi-energy studies that treat TOU mainly as an exogenous tariff in a surrogate energy balance, this work formulates \emph{weekly price-taker cash-flow maximisation} for a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a high-fidelity Modelica--FMU digital twin under Shandong industrial/commercial TOU prices, with an explicit energy state-of-charge (SoC) recovery gate. Classical programmes require aggressive convexification of CAES physics, while single-layer deep reinforcement learning (DRL) trained from scratch often under-utilises storage or fails weekly recovery. We therefore cast operation as a goal-conditioned hierarchical Markov decision process and propose Safe Market-GHTD3: a high-level twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) issues market- and recovery-aware plant intents every $c$ steps; a low-level goal-conditioned TD3 maps $(s,g)$ to hybrid actions under observation normalisation and amplified goal channels so hierarchical intents remain causal without a frozen teacher; market-structure goal priors (MSGP), market-structured hindsight experience replay (MS-HER) and feasibility-filtered maximum-likelihood pretraining (F-MLE) warm-start learning; and a GiveSafe filter projects actions onto the dynamic feasible set before each FMU step. Multi-seed evaluation on three representative weeks yields mean episode-reward lifts of about $+14$ (winter), $+56$ (transition) and $+36$ (summer) over a typical single-layer GiveSafe-TD3 trained from scratch under the same budget, with SoC gates recovered in all winter/transition runs and $2/3$ summer seeds. Seasonal power balances, SoC trajectories and cost-component analyses show hierarchical peak--valley arbitrage with feasible grid exchange, in contrast to under-acting single-layer policies and irregular parametric PSO. Ablations of MSGP, MS-HER and F-MLE, together with sensitivity of the subgoal interval $c$ and intrinsic weight $\alpha$, further support the proposed stack.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Weekly price-taker cash-flow scheduling of a thermal--battery--CAES FMU plant under Shandong TOU tariffs.
\item Safe Market-GHTD3: MSGP, MS-HER, F-MLE and GiveSafe enable absolute hierarchical control without a teacher residual.
\item Multi-seed gains over TD3-scratch, with seasonal dispatch, SoC and cost-component evidence versus rules, linprog and PSO.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =====================================================================

Deep decarbonisation is accelerating the integration of renewable generation into multi-energy plants that co-dispatch electricity, heat-related conversion and storage~\cite{zhou2023iesdrl,yang2024planning}. Industrial and commercial customers increasingly face provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs that create strong peak--valley incentives for storage arbitrage and thermal load shifting~\cite{ndrc2021tou,shandong2023tou}. This paper addresses \emph{plant-level price-taker operation}: the facility takes exogenous TOU buy/sell prices as given and schedules thermal, battery and CAES assets to maximise weekly net cash flow while recovering energy inventories---not speculative nodal bidding or ISO market clearing~\cite{emmanuel2020market,pei2024pricetaker}. At the plant level, however, scheduling must respect hybrid action spaces (continuous set-points together with discrete CAES modes), ramp and inventory limits, and long-horizon SoC recovery. When the plant dynamics are provided by a high-fidelity Modelica--FMU twin rather than a convex energy-balance surrogate, the decision problem becomes a computationally expensive hybrid Markov decision process (MDP).

Model-based optimisers (mixed-integer programmes, rolling linearisations, particle swarm heuristics) remain valuable baselines, yet they either require aggressive convexification of non-convex CAES physics or suffer from limited budgets in high-dimensional hybrid spaces~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Data-driven deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{sutton2018rl,fujimoto2018td3,haarnoja2018sac} learns policies by closed-loop interaction and is therefore attractive for nonlinear twins. Hierarchical goal-conditioned RL further mitigates temporal abstraction: Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} develop GHTD3 for hydrogen-enhanced integrated energy systems (H-IES), coupling a semi-MDP high level with a goal-augmented low level and demonstrating peak--valley utilisation under TOU within a \emph{comprehensive cost} objective (electricity trading, device O\&M, curtailment and carbon). Multi-agent hierarchical options~\cite{jendoubi2023hier} and multi-timescale market bidding~\cite{ochoa2024bidding} similarly exploit temporal structure. Constrained and safe RL emphasise feasible-set restriction during learning and execution~\cite{garcia2015safe,garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained,wachi2023safe}.

From this landscape three gaps remain for the present plant twin. (1)~\textbf{Cross-horizon cash-flow under TOU.} Single-layer TD3/SAC agents are myopic with respect to week-scale inventory goals and peak--valley plans; cost-minimising H-IES studies that include TOU do not target weekly net cash-flow maximisation of a thermal--battery--CAES FMU plant with an explicit energy-SoC gate. (2)~\textbf{Contracted feasible sets under hybrid CAES actions.} Mode locks, ramps and inventory bounds shrink the executable action set; unconstrained exploration on the twin is unsafe and unrepresentative of plant practice. (3)~\textbf{Hierarchy that remains causal on large-scale observations.} When continuous actors map unbounded logits through saturating nonlinearities on megawatt-scale measurements, the Jacobian $\partial a/\partial g$ can collapse so that hierarchical goals cease to affect executed actions---hierarchy then degenerates to a single-layer controller unless market-aware goals, hindsight relabelling and numerical goal pathways are co-designed.

Table~\ref{tab:lit} positions representative works. Existing hierarchical and constrained DRL studies rarely combine (i)~hybrid thermal--battery--CAES actions on a high-fidelity FMU twin, (ii)~market-conditioned hierarchical goals with MS-HER/F-MLE warm-start and pre-execution safety filtering, and (iii)~a shared weekly SoC-recovery protocol against rules, rolling LP, PSO and typical single-layer TD3.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formalise weekly price-taker operation of a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a Modelica--FMU twin as a hybrid-action MDP with an explicit weekly net cash-flow objective and energy-SoC recovery gate under Shandong industrial/commercial TOU tariffs.
\item We propose Safe Market-GHTD3: a five-dimensional Modelica-aligned goal executed by an absolute goal-conditioned low-level TD3 (observation normalisation and scaled goal inputs), \emph{market-structure goal priors} (MSGP) with recovery annealing, market-structured HER-mix (MS-HER), feasibility-filtered MLE pretraining (F-MLE) on rule trajectories, and GiveSafe projection---without relying on a frozen hybrid teacher residual.
\item We provide multi-seed closed-loop three-season evidence aligned with hierarchical multi-energy protocols~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}: large paired gains over typical single-layer TD3 trained from scratch under the same FMU budget; we further show that rolling linprog and parametric PSO often underperform the conservative plant rule on the twin (model mismatch / narrow heuristics), clarifying where hierarchical RL is most advantageous, with ablations of MSGP/MS-HER/F-MLE and intent--dispatch diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:system} describes the multi-energy plant and TOU market. Section~\ref{sec:problem} formulates generation, conversion and storage models, operating constraints and the economic objective, and casts closed-loop control as a hybrid-action MDP. Section~\ref{sec:method} presents the Safe Market-GHTD3 solution methodology. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports simulation settings, comparative results and sensitivity analyses. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

High-fidelity plant dynamics (thermal unit, battery, multi-tank CAES, renewables and grid interface) are advanced inside a Modelica--FMU twin with communication interval $\tau_s=1$~h. This section states the \emph{control-interface} models and constraints used by optimisers and RL agents. Component sets are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=\{\mathrm{w},\mathrm{pv}\},\quad
I_2=\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{caes}\},\quad
I_3=\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},\\
I&=I_1\cup I_2\cup I_3.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sets}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Energy generation units}

\subsubsection{Photovoltaics}

Available PV power is driven by irradiance and temperature resource tables and realised by the twin as
\begin{equation}
0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}(I_t,T_t),
\label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
where $I_t$ and $T_t$ denote irradiance and ambient temperature. Curtailment is non-negative and absorbed in the electric balance.

\subsubsection{Wind turbines}

Wind available power follows wind-speed tables. The twin injects
\begin{equation}
0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}(v_t),
\label{eq:wt}
\end{equation}
with $v_t$ the wind speed. Turbine pitch is not a free decision variable in this twin.

\subsection{Energy conversion units}

\subsubsection{Thermal generation}

Thermal electric power is commanded by the continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp},t}$:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{th}} = u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}},
\qquad
u_{\min}\le u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\le u_{\max},
\label{eq:th}
\end{equation}
with $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=150$~MW, $u_{\min}=P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}/P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=1/3$ and $u_{\max}=1$ (no shut-down). Ramping is constrained in~\eqref{eq:box}. Operating cost contributes to $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ in~\eqref{eq:dJ}.

\subsubsection{CAES conversion}

CAES is a hybrid conversion--storage unit. The discrete mode and continuous magnitude
\begin{equation}
m_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\},\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in[0,1]
\label{eq:caes-cmd}
\end{equation}
map to a signed power command on capacity $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}=150$~MW,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
=
\begin{cases}
+\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{charge},\\
0, & m=\mathrm{idle},\\
-\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{discharge},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:caes-p}
\end{equation}
subject to minimum-run bands equivalent to $u_{\mathrm{caes}}\in[-1,-0.33]\cup\{0\}\cup[0.86,1]$ and mode locks in the FMU. Gas/thermal tank thermodynamics are integrated inside the twin; the RL interface only observes SoC inventories and electric power.

\subsection{Energy storage units}

Battery power is commanded by $u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\in[-1,1]$ on capacity $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}=100$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{bat}} = u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}.
\label{eq:bat-p}
\end{equation}
The interface SoC recursion (charge positive) with efficiency $\eta$ and energy capacity $E_{\mathrm{cap}}$ is
\begin{equation}
s_{t+1,\mathrm{bat}}
=
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}}
+
\tau_s\frac{\eta^{\mathrm{ch}}[P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_+ - [P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_-/\eta^{\mathrm{dis}}}{E_{\mathrm{cap}}},
\label{eq:bat-soc}
\end{equation}
with $\eta^{\mathrm{ch}}=\eta^{\mathrm{dis}}=0.85$, $E_{\mathrm{cap}}=500$~MWh and $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$, $[x]_-=\max(-x,0)$. Primary energy inventories for weekly recovery are $s_{t,\mathrm{bat}}$ and CAES gas SoC $s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}$; hot/cold inventories $s_{t,\mathrm{hot}},s_{t,\mathrm{cold}}$ are process states used in goals and shaping. All inventories obey~\eqref{eq:socbox}.

\subsection{System operation constraints}

Electric power balance, box/ramp limits, inventory bounds and grid exchange limits are
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{th}}+P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}+P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}+P_t^{\mathrm{w}}+P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}+P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}=P_t^{\mathrm{load}},
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\underline{P}_{i}\le P_t^{i}\le \overline{P}_{i},\qquad
\bigl|P_t^{i}-P_{t-1}^{i}\bigr|\le \Delta_i^{\max},
\quad i\in\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{bat}\},
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\underline{s}_{i}\le s_{t,i}\le \overline{s}_{i},\qquad i\in\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:socbox}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min}\le P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}\le P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max}.
\label{eq:grid}
\end{equation}
Actions that would violate~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:grid} or CAES mode locks lie outside the dynamic feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

Weekly operation further imposes an energy-SoC recovery gate on primary inventories,
\begin{equation}
L_1^{\mathrm{e}}
= w_{\mathrm{bat}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{bat}}-s_{0,\mathrm{bat}}\bigr|
+ w_{\mathrm{gas}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{gas}}-s_{0,\mathrm{gas}}\bigr|
\le \varepsilon,
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
with $(w_{\mathrm{bat}},w_{\mathrm{gas}})=(1.5,1.0)$ and $\varepsilon=0.06$.

\subsection{Optimization objectives}

Let $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ index hours in a weekly episode ($T=168$). Denote purchase/sale energies by $E_t^{\mathrm{buy}},E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$ (MWh), TOU prices by $\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$, and device operating costs by $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$. The incremental cash flow and weekly objective are
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t = \lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}} E_t^{\mathrm{sell}} - \lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}} - C_t^{\mathrm{op}},
\label{eq:dJ}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\max\; J = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \Delta J_t
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:balance}\text{--}\eqref{eq:soc}.
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
Closed-loop evaluation ranks policies by $J$ and gate~\eqref{eq:soc}; training maximises discounted extrinsic rewards built from $\Delta J_t$ and SoC shaping (Section~\ref{sec:reward}).

\subsection{Hybrid-action MDP}

Closed-loop control is cast as an MDP $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma\rangle$ with $\gamma=0.99$. The state concatenates inventories, powers, prices and TOU features,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_t=\bigl[&
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}},s_{t,\mathrm{gas}},s_{t,\mathrm{hot}},s_{t,\mathrm{cold}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}},P_t^{\mathrm{bat}},P_t^{\mathrm{caes}},P_t^{\mathrm{grid}},
P_t^{\mathrm{w}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv}},P_t^{\mathrm{load}},\\
&\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\ \text{TOU band features},\ \text{optional forecast residuals}
\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
and the hybrid action is
\begin{equation}
a_t = \bigl(u_{\mathrm{tp},t},\, u_{\mathrm{bat},t},\, m_{\mathrm{caes},t},\, \mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\bigr)\in\mathcal{A},
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with continuous channels scaled to physical limits and $m_{\mathrm{caes}}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\}$. Transitions $P$ are induced by one FMU hour under GiveSafe-filtered actions.

\subsection{Reward function for learning}
\label{sec:reward}

With calibration constant $C_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx1.565\times10^{5}$~CNY (rule-controller $p_{95}$ step cash-flow magnitude),
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{econ}} = \frac{\Delta J_t}{C_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
Potential-based inventory shaping uses the weighted L1 distance $L_{1,t}$ of (battery, gas, hot, cold) relative to episode start:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}
= \kappa_t \bigl(L_{1,t-1}-L_{1,t}\bigr)
- \kappa_t^{\mathrm{abs}} L_{1,t},
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
with base $\kappa=5$ and a recovery-window scale-up over the last $H=40$~h. At a completed, failure-free terminal step,
\begin{equation}
r_T^{\mathrm{term}}
=
\begin{cases}
B, & L_1^{\mathrm{e}}\le\varepsilon,\\
-p\,L_1^{\mathrm{e}}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:term}
\end{equation}
with $B=15$ and $p=30$. The extrinsic reward is
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{ext}}
= \mathrm{clip}\bigl(r_t^{\mathrm{econ}}+r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}+r_t^{\mathrm{term}},\, r_{\min},\, r_{\max}\bigr),
\label{eq:rext}
\end{equation}
with $(r_{\min},r_{\max})=(-25,25)$. Figure~\ref{fig:reward} summarises the stack.

\centering

\caption{Reward composition (economic, shaping, terminal) and hierarchical intrinsic/extrinsic split under FMU+GiveSafe constraints.}

\section{Solution methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

The weekly scheduling task is modelled by two augmented MDPs and solved by Safe Market-GHTD3, a goal-conditioned hierarchical TD3 with market-structure priors, hindsight relabeling, feasibility-filtered pretraining and GiveSafe projection~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,fujimoto2018td3}. Figure~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarises the architecture.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_algorithm}
\caption{Safe Market-GHTD3: high-level TD3 with MSGP and MS-HER, absolute low-level policy with F-MLE warm-start, GiveSafe projection and FMU twin.}
\label{fig:algorithm}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Two augmented MDPs}

A standard one-step MDP is insufficient for week-scale inventory planning. We use a high-level semi-MDP (SMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{G},P_c,R_c,\gamma^c\rangle$ that issues goals every $c$ steps, and a low-level goal-augmented MDP (GAMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G},P,R,\gamma\rangle$ that executes hybrid actions conditioned on the residual goal~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. The high-level action is the goal
\begin{equation}
g=\bigl[g^{\Delta\mathrm{bat}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{gas}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{th}},\,g^{u_{\mathrm{tp}}},\,g^{\mathrm{arb}}\bigr]^{\top}
\in [g_{\min},g_{\max}],
\label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
with box $[-0.30,0.30]\times[-0.12,0.12]\times[-0.10,0.10]\times[-0.20,0.20]\times[0,1]$. Low-level actions remain~\eqref{eq:action}.

\subsection{Hierarchy of decision making}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_cstep}
\caption{$c$-step interaction between high-level goals and low-level hybrid actions.}
\label{fig:cstep}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier

Every $c$ steps, the high-level policy $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$ observes $s_t$ and issues $g_t$. For $i\in[t,t+c-1]$, the low-level policy $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ maps $(s_i,g_i)$ to $a_i$, and residual goals are updated by
\begin{equation}
g_{i+1}=h(s_i,g_i,s_{i+1})
= s_i^{\mathrm{int}}+g_i-s_{i+1}^{\mathrm{int}},
\label{eq:h}
\end{equation}
where $s^{\mathrm{int}}$ denotes the inventory/intent subvector tracked by $g$. Intrinsic and layered training rewards follow~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{int}}
= -\Bigl(\sum_i w_i e_{i,t}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}+\alpha\, r_t^{\mathrm{ext}},
\qquad
e_t=s_t^{\mathrm{int}}+g_t-s_{t+1}^{\mathrm{int}},
\label{eq:rint}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{lo}} = r_t^{\mathrm{int}}, \qquad
R_k^{\mathrm{hi}} = \frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1} r_{t+j}^{\mathrm{ext}},
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with default $c=8$ and $\alpha\in[0.20,0.50]$. High-level bootstrapping uses discount $\gamma^{c}$.

Market-structure goal priors (MSGP) blend the raw high-level output with a TOU prior $g^{\mathrm{mkt}}$ (charge in valleys, discharge in peaks) and a recovery prior $g^{\mathrm{rec}}$ over the last $H=40$--$48$~h:
\begin{equation}
g \leftarrow (1-w_{\mathrm{m}}-w_{\mathrm{r}})\,\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}(s)
+ w_{\mathrm{m}}\,g^{\mathrm{mkt}}
+ w_{\mathrm{r}}\,g^{\mathrm{rec}},
\label{eq:msgp}
\end{equation}
with annealed $w_{\mathrm{m}}:0.55\rightarrow0.12$ and recovery weight up to $w_{\mathrm{r}}\in[0.95,0.98]$.

\subsection{Internal controllers (TD3)}

Both layers use TD3~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}. Write $x^{\mathrm{hi}}=s$, $a^{\mathrm{hi}}=g$ and $x^{\mathrm{lo}}=[s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g]$, $a^{\mathrm{lo}}=a$, with observation normalisation $s_{\mathrm{n}}=\tanh(s/s_0)$ and goal scale $\kappa=4$. Each layer maintains twin critics $Q_{\theta_1},Q_{\theta_2}$, actor $\mu_{\phi}$ and target networks. Critic losses and targets are
\begin{equation}
L(\theta_j)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(Q_{\theta_j}(x,a)-y\bigr)^2\bigr],\quad j=1,2,
\label{eq:td3-L}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
y=r+\gamma\min_{k=1,2}Q_{\bar\theta_k}\bigl(x',\tilde a'\bigr),
\qquad
\tilde a'=\mu_{\bar\phi}(x')+\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-\epsilon,\epsilon\bigr).
\label{eq:td3-y}
\end{equation}
The actor follows the deterministic policy gradient
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\phi}J
=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}
\Bigl[
\nabla_a Q_{\theta_1}(x,a)\big|_{a=\mu_{\phi}(x)}
\nabla_{\phi}\mu_{\phi}(x)
\Bigr],
\label{eq:td3-pi}
\end{equation}
with delayed actor updates and soft targets $\bar\psi\leftarrow\tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi$.

\subsection{GiveSafe projection}

Executable actions are projected onto the dynamic feasible set before each FMU step~\cite{garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained}:
\begin{equation}
a_t^{\star}
= \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s_t)}\!\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}\bigr).
\label{eq:givesafe}
\end{equation}
Only transitions generated by $a_t^{\star}$ enter the replay buffers. Absolute (non-residual) low-level execution is
\begin{equation}
a = \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}\!\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)\bigr).
\label{eq:abs}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Historical goal relabeling (MS-HER)}

High-level transitions $(s_t,g_t,R_{t:t+c-1}^{\mathrm{ext}},s_{t+c})$ become stale as $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ evolves. Market-structured hindsight experience replay replaces $g_t$ by a candidate maximising a storage-activity-weighted log-likelihood under the current low-level policy~\cite{cui2025ghtd3},
\begin{equation}
\tilde g_t
\in
\arg\max_{g\in\mathcal{C}_t}
\sum_{i=t}^{t+c-1}
w_i^{\mathrm{act}}
\log \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(a_i\mid s_i,g_i),
\label{eq:msher}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_t$ mixes the original goal, achieved $\Delta s^{\mathrm{int}}$, future goals and optional market-prior candidates, and subsequent residuals follow~\eqref{eq:h}.

\subsection{Feasibility-filtered MLE pretraining (F-MLE)}

Before RL, low-level (and high-level goal) parameters are warm-started by maximum likelihood on \emph{physically valid} price-aware/rule trajectories only:
\begin{equation}
\phi^{\star}
\in
\arg\max_{\phi}
\sum_{(s,g,a)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{demo}}}
\log \pi_{\phi}(a\mid s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
a\in\mathcal{F}(s).
\label{eq:fmle}
\end{equation}
No frozen hybrid teacher residual is used at runtime.

\subsection{Policy learning}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ghtd3} summarises the training loop; Table~\ref{tab:hyper} lists main hyperparameters.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Safe Market-GHTD3}
\label{alg:ghtd3}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Initialise $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}},\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$, critics, targets; buffers $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}},\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; F-MLE warm-start~\eqref{eq:fmle}
\For{episode $=1,2,\ldots$}
  \State Reset FMU; $s\leftarrow s_0$; clear residual goal
  \For{$t=0$ to $T-1$}
    \If{$t \bmod c = 0$}
      \State Issue $g\leftarrow\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}(s)$; apply MSGP~\eqref{eq:msgp}
    \EndIf
    \State $\tilde a \leftarrow \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}$; $a\leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}(\tilde a)$
    \State Step FMU; observe $s'$, $\Delta J$; compute $r^{\mathrm{ext}},r^{\mathrm{int}}$ by~\eqref{eq:rext}--\eqref{eq:reward}
    \State Store low-level transition in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; $g\leftarrow h(s,g,s')$
    \If{$t \bmod c = c-1$} store high-level transition in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$
    \EndIf
    \State TD3 update on $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; every $c$ steps, MS-HER relabel~\eqref{eq:msher} and TD3 update on $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$ with $\gamma^{c}$
    \State $s\leftarrow s'$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Simulation settings}

Three representative weeks (winter, summer, transition) are evaluated on the FMU twin under identical TOU and resource tables. Unless stated otherwise, primary ranking uses weekly episodes with SoC re-initialisation (weekly-reset protocol), consistent with hierarchical multi-energy case studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Original conservative plant rule (B0; high thermal, storage largely idle); price-aware heuristic rule (B1); rolling linprog MPC; PSO; and \textbf{TD3-scratch}: single-layer GiveSafe-TD3 trained from scratch for $3.5\times10^{4}$ valid steps (no BC-dominated market teacher), which plays the role of the typical single-layer TD3 in~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Safe Market-GHTD3 uses the same budget and seeds with GiveSafe on.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Weekly net cash flow $J$~\eqref{eq:J}, calibrated episode reward, energy-SoC gate~\eqref{eq:soc}, thermal energy and storage throughput. Multi-seed tables report mean$\pm$std and SoC pass counts.

\paragraph{Experimental environment.}
All DRL training and closed-loop FMU evaluations reported in this paper were executed on a Windows~11 (64-bit) workstation with an \textbf{Intel Core~i9-14900K} CPU (24~cores / 32~threads; base clock 3.2~GHz) and 128~GB system RAM, together with an \textbf{NVIDIA GeForce RTX~4080} GPU (16~GB GDDR6X; compute capability~8.9), NVIDIA driver~591.86, and \textbf{CUDA~13.0} as exposed by the PyTorch binary (\texttt{torch}~2.9.1+\texttt{cu130}). The software stack uses Python~3.11 with PyTorch CUDA builds for actor--critic updates; the plant dynamics are advanced through a Modelica--FMU twin with one-hour communication steps. Parallel jobs (multi-seed training, ablations and sensitivity runs) isolate FMU instances via per-job temporary directories so that concurrent rollouts do not contend on a single unpacked twin. Classical baselines (rule, linprog, PSO) run under the same FMU interface on the same machine for protocol consistency.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Policies are trained with off-policy TD3 updates on FMU rollouts under the environment above. Hyperparameters of Safe Market-GHTD3 are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}; the main multi-seed budget is $3.5\times10^{4}$ valid environment steps with seeds $\{0,1,2\}$.

\paragraph{Why classical optimisers underperform the plant rule B0---and what this implies for RL.}
\label{sec:why-or-pso}
A striking feature of Table~\ref{tab:main} is that rolling linprog and parametric PSO often fail to beat the conservative plant rule B0 on the \emph{same} closed-loop FMU protocol, even though both are ``optimisation'' methods. This is not an artefact of a weak rule baseline; it reflects a structural mismatch between simplified optimisers and the hybrid non-convex twin.

\textbf{B0 as a strong feasibility baseline.}
B0 keeps thermal output near the upper feasible band, holds the battery near idle and the CAES mode at idle whenever allowed, and therefore almost always completes the 168\,h week with the energy-SoC gate satisfied. Under TOU settlement, high thermal injection and limited storage cycling already yield competitive cash flow without mode-lock violations or inventory dumping. Any optimiser that intends to beat B0 must extract peak--valley value \emph{without} destroying feasibility---a harder task than improving a myopic or infeasible search.

\textbf{Rolling linprog: optimising the wrong model.}
The linprog controller solves a short-horizon linear programme on a \emph{relaxed surrogate}: continuous battery and gas-tank powers, linear fuel proxy, power balance and box SOC dynamics, then maps the first-step solution to hybrid CAES modes for FMU execution. Three gaps explain its mediocre closed-loop scores. (i)~\emph{Model mismatch}: discrete CAES modes, minimum-run locks and thermal inventories in the twin are only approximated; surrogate-optimal set-points are frequently projected or rewritten by the feasible set, so open-loop LP optimality does not transfer. (ii)~\emph{Myopic horizon}: a horizon of only a few hours cannot coordinate week-scale recovery and multi-day TOU structure. (iii)~\emph{Objective mismatch}: the LP minimises a linear buy/fuel/sell proxy, whereas evaluation ranks calibrated episode reward and the energy-SoC gate built from the true FMU cash-flow. Empirically, linprog induces much larger storage throughput than B0 yet returns lower winter/summer rewards (Table~\ref{tab:main}), i.e.\ aggressive cycling on a wrong model does not improve true economics.

\textbf{Parametric PSO: searching a narrow and brittle policy class.}
PSO in this study does not optimise free hybrid action sequences. It tunes a six-dimensional peak--valley heuristic (price thresholds, storage magnitudes, thermal bias, recovery strength), with each particle evaluated by a full FMU week. The searchable class is far smaller than the hybrid MDP; many parameter vectors trigger early constraint failures (e.g.\ winter trajectories that terminate after a few steps), and large failure penalties dominate the fitness. PSO can exceed B0 in transition cash flow for some seeds, but winter/summer SoC failures and irregular switching (Figs.~\ref{fig:bal-w}--\ref{fig:soc-mat}) show that heuristic search on the twin is brittle. Enlarging the search to full action sequences would be combinatorially prohibitive under FMU evaluation cost.

\textbf{Implication for reinforcement learning.}
These baselines therefore play complementary diagnostic roles rather than ``weak straw men''. Rules prove that \emph{feasibility-first} operation already scores well; linprog shows that \emph{convexification-plus-mapping} is insufficient for CAES hybrids on a high-fidelity twin; PSO shows that \emph{low-dimensional market heuristics} cannot reliably navigate mode locks and weekly recovery. Safe Market-GHTD3 (and, to a lesser extent, single-layer TD3-scratch) instead optimises policies by direct interaction with the twin under GiveSafe, so credit is assigned to actions that remain executable. Hierarchy further supplies week-scale market and recovery intents (MSGP/MS-HER), repairing both the \emph{inaction} of under-trained single-layer agents and the \emph{infeasible aggression} of parametric search. The multi-seed lifts in Table~\ref{tab:main} should thus be read as gains over a strong rule and over method classes that are theoretically attractive but misaligned with the FMU hybrid decision structure---which is precisely where model-free hierarchical RL is most useful.

\paragraph{Remark on seed variance.}
Among RL seeds, evaluation-week rewards still differ because hybrid CAES modes induce multi-modal policies; hierarchy mainly stabilises transition/summer relative to TD3-scratch. Training-curve scatter (Fig.~\ref{fig:training}) should not be read as the primary multi-seed metric.

\paragraph{High-level $\lambda$-SoC from scratch (inventory dual).}
\label{sec:lambda-sens}
Table~\ref{tab:main} ranks by mean reward and therefore retains the economic-optimal multi-seed checkpoint (summer SoC $2/3$: seed~2 fails the energy gate while still beating TD3 on reward). As a \emph{sensitivity} experiment---not a replacement of the primary table---we retrain seed~$2$ from scratch for $3.5\times10^{4}$ steps with the same Safe Market-GHTD3 stack plus a high-level Lagrangian inventory dual ($\lambda$-SoC, stronger recovery prior; no short fine-tune on the failed checkpoint). The $\lambda$-SoC seed passes the energy gate in \emph{all three} seasons and keeps positive $\Delta$ versus TD3 throughout; summer reward rises from $62.6$ (SoC fail) to $71.0$ (SoC pass). Winter/transition rewards are slightly below the economic-optimal seed~$2$ (trade-off between mean $J$ and hard recovery). Short $\lambda$ fine-tuning of the already-failed seed was previously tried and \emph{degraded} economics without fixing summer SoC; thus dual variables should be enabled \emph{from initialisation} when inventory is prioritised. We keep Table~\ref{tab:main} as the main economic claim and report $\lambda$-SoC as an optional recovery mode.

\paragraph{Intent--dispatch decoupling (IDD).}
Goal-sensitivity diagnostics on absolute goal-conditioned actors with $\mathrm{obs\_norm}$ and $\kappa=4$ show non-vanishing action responses on all five goal dimensions (alive pathway). On the strongest seed (s1, summer week), freezing the low level to random feasible actions collapses weekly reward (full$-$freeze-low $\approx{+}49$), whereas freezing the high level to a pure market prior yields a smaller gap, supporting hierarchical decoupling rather than a single-layer lookalike.

\paragraph{GiveSafe.}
GiveSafe is active for both TD3-scratch and Safe Market-GHTD3 during interaction. Its role is structural feasibility projection~\eqref{eq:givesafe} rather than a free performance lever; we do not report untraceable ``stress test'' graphics from prior hybrid-teacher experiments.

\paragraph{Subgoal interval $c$ and intrinsic weight $\alpha$.}
\label{sec:c-alpha}
Following~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, we vary the subgoal interval $c\in\{4,8,12,24\}$ and the terminal intrinsic weight $\alpha_{\mathrm{end}}\in\{0.10,0.22,0.35,0.50,0.70\}$ on seed~$0$ under a matched short budget of $1.5\times10^{4}$ valid steps (the main multi-seed study uses $c=8$ and annealed $\alpha:0.50\rightarrow0.22$). Fig.~\ref{fig:sens} plots the three-season mean episode reward. For $c$, the mean peaks at $c=8$ ($\approx106$) and remains high for $c\in\{12,24\}$ ($\approx105$ and $\approx103$), whereas $c=4$ drops sharply to $\approx75$ and fails the summer SoC gate---too-frequent subgoal switches destabilise low-level learning. For $\alpha_{\mathrm{end}}$, the same default point $\alpha_{\mathrm{end}}=0.22$ (shared with the $c=8$ run) is best; lowering $\alpha$ to $0.10$ or raising it toward $0.50$--$0.70$ reduces the mean reward and tends to lose summer recovery, consistent with under- or over-weighting intrinsic tracking relative to market extrinsic return (hierarchy collapses toward single-layer behaviour when $\alpha$ is too large). These curves support the operating point used in Table~\ref{tab:main}; ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) and $\lambda$-SoC (Table~\ref{tab:lambda}) further stress-test market priors and recovery.

\paragraph{Annual weekly-reset evaluation (supporting).}
\label{sec:annual-a}
To check that the three representative weeks are not cherry-picked, we evaluate B0, TD3-scratch (seed~1) and Safe Market-GHTD3 (seed~1) on a \emph{sliding weekly-reset} cover of the full temperate year ($8760$~h $\rightarrow$ 53 windows of up to $168$~h, including a short terminal partial week). Each window re-initialises the FMU twin and energy SoC exactly as in Table~\ref{tab:main}---inventory does \emph{not} carry across weeks---so the protocol matches the main study and typical week-based plant planning. Fig.~\ref{fig:annual} and Table~\ref{tab:annual} summarise results. Safe Market-GHTD3 attains the highest mean weekly reward ($99.1\pm17.6$) with energy-SoC pass rate $48/53$ ($90.6\%$). B0 remains a strong feasibility baseline ($51/53$ SoC pass) but at much lower mean reward ($50.6\pm18.2$). TD3-scratch only slightly exceeds B0 on mean reward ($56.7\pm31.9$) while failing the SoC gate on nearly half of the weeks ($27/53$), confirming that single-layer policies do not generalise across the year under the same recovery protocol. Continuous-year evaluation with inventory carry is left for future stress tests and is intentionally \emph{not} mixed with these numbers.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:support}

Relative to Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, who minimise a multi-term comprehensive cost (trading, O\&M, curtailment, carbon) for an H-IES under TOU, this work maximises weekly net cash flow for a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a Shandong TOU price-taker interface, enforces an energy-SoC recovery gate, executes on a Modelica--FMU twin with GiveSafe, and keeps absolute goal-conditioned control causal via MSGP, MS-HER, F-MLE and goal-channel scaling. Section~\ref{sec:why-or-pso} further argues that the apparent weakness of rolling linprog and parametric PSO relative to B0 is informative: on a non-convex hybrid FMU plant, surrogate mathematical programmes and low-dimensional heuristics are easy to mis-specify, whereas hierarchical RL with safety projection learns executable peak--valley policies from closed-loop experience. The annual weekly-reset study (Section~\ref{sec:annual-a}) shows that hierarchical gains and recovery hold across a full year of windows rather than only three hand-picked weeks. Limitations include price-taker assumptions~\cite{emmanuel2020market}, temperate-year resource tables, volumetric TOU rather than nodal LMPs, the restricted policy class of the PSO baseline (not a free-trajectory swarm over hybrid actions), surrogate rather than full MILP formulations for the LP baseline, a $2/3$ summer SoC pass rate on the economic-optimal multi-seed table (mitigated by optional from-scratch $\lambda$-SoC training; Section~\ref{sec:lambda-sens}), and the absence of continuous-year inventory-carry stress tests in the main text.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

This paper presented Safe Market-GHTD3 for weekly price-taker scheduling of a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a Modelica--FMU twin under Shandong TOU tariffs. The problem is cast as cash-flow maximisation with peak--valley incentives and hard weekly energy-SoC recovery, not as speculative market bidding. By combining market/recovery goals (MSGP), absolute goal-conditioned hierarchical TD3 with MS-HER and F-MLE warm-start, and GiveSafe filtering, the method achieves large multi-seed closed-loop gains over a typical single-layer TD3-scratch baseline and over plant rules. Classical rolling linprog and parametric PSO often fail to beat the conservative rule B0 on the same twin, because linear surrogates and low-dimensional market heuristics are misaligned with hybrid CAES physics---underscoring the value of hierarchical RL that optimises executable policies directly in closed loop. Seasonal power balances and SoC trajectories explain hierarchical peak--valley arbitrage; an annual weekly-reset cover of 8760~h confirms that hierarchy keeps high mean reward and SoC pass rates across the year; cost-component and ablation analyses isolate economic and algorithmic contributions. Future work includes multi-seed SoC curricula, adaptive subgoal intervals aligned with TOU blocks, continuous-year inventory-carry stress tests, tighter OR baselines for sharper method-boundary tests, and plant portfolios matched to the tariff region.
