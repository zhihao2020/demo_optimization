\shorttitle{Physics-constrained hybrid TD3 for multi-energy scheduling}
\title[mode=title]{Physics-Constrained Hybrid TD3 for Forecast-Aware Economic Scheduling of Multi-Energy Systems}

\begin{abstract}
Rising shares of wind and photovoltaic generation tighten the coupling among thermal units, battery storage, compressed-air energy storage (CAES) and the grid. This paper studies weekly price-taker operation of a thermal--BESS--CAES plant under a monthly Shandong agency-purchase time-of-use (TOU) tariff and a national emissions-trading charge. The plant is represented by a first-principles Modelica model, compiled from MWORKS Sysplorer as a functional mock-up unit (FMU). Device dynamics, inventory limits and interchange capacity together define a state-dependent feasible set \(\mathcal{A}_f(s)\) that is strictly smaller than the product of device boxes. Physics-constrained hybrid TD3 (PC-HybridTD3) learns a forecast-aware policy whose actions---continuous thermal and battery set-points together with a CAES mode--magnitude pair---are generated already inside \(\mathcal{A}_f(s)\). Twin critics evaluate the executed physical commands, and only hours that the FMU actually advances enter the economic Bellman update; a residual safety screen (GiveSafe) is kept as a last physical check. Continuous-projection TD3 and a component-wise hybrid variant isolate the action representation and the joint support. The policy is compared with a price-aware heuristic and a rolling mixed-integer linear programme on the same FMU.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A hybrid policy coordinates thermal, battery and CAES commands on a shared timescale.
\item Device limits and grid coupling are encoded as a joint, state-dependent feasible support.
\item Forecast-aware FMU-in-the-loop learning is assessed against same-plant classical baselines.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =====================================================================

High shares of wind and photovoltaic generation make plant-level multi-energy scheduling a coupled problem of heterogeneous dynamics, temporal inventory, operational constraints and nonlinear plant behaviour~\cite{zhou2023iesdrl,yang2024planning}. Thermal generation, battery energy storage (BESS), compressed-air energy storage (CAES) and the grid interchange must be coordinated under ramping, inventory and capacity limits. This paper studies weekly price-taker operation of a thermal--BESS--CAES facility~\cite{emmanuel2020market,pei2024pricetaker} that settles a monthly Shandong agency-purchase time-of-use (TOU) tariff~\cite{ndrc2021tou,shandong2023tou} together with a national emissions-trading charge~\cite{icap2025chinaets}. The objective is the expected sum of hourly operating cost
\begin{equation}
\min_{\pi}\;
\mathbb{E}\Biggl[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} C_t\Biggr],
\quad
C_t=
C_t^{\mathrm{grid}}
+C_t^{\mathrm{thermal}}
+C_t^{\mathrm{carbon}}
+C_t^{\mathrm{curt}}
+C_t^{\mathrm{uns}}
+C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}
+C_t^{\mathrm{su}}.
\label{eq:C}
\end{equation}
Wind, photovoltaic generation and load enter as exogenous boundaries. The decision problem is therefore plant-wide coordination rather than the isolated scheduling of any one storage device.

Model-based programmes remain natural baselines. Mixed-integer linear programmes and receding-horizon control make constraints explicit and the optimiser predictable~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, yet on a first-principles multi-physics twin they typically convexify thermodynamics, incur a non-trivial online solve, or both. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{sutton2018rl,fujimoto2018td3,haarnoja2018sac} instead learns a closed-loop policy from interaction and, once trained, can be evaluated on the same FMU after a single forward pass.

Most DRL schedulers still assume a regular continuous action box, or restore feasibility by clipping and penalties after the fact~\cite{zhou2023iesdrl,wu2024pricing,sun2024constrained}. Hierarchical methods address a different difficulty. Cui et al.\ use GHTD3 with high-level state-of-charge increment goals and low-level execution to plan across time scales, together with retrospective goal relabeling~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,cui2024caesbess}; multi-agent hierarchical managers follow a related path~\cite{jendoubi2023hier,ochoa2024bidding}. Option initiation sets can restrict when a mode may start, but they do not by themselves place a same-hour policy on a joint, state-dependent feasible set. The gap treated here is therefore distinct: heterogeneous device actions, a nonlinear FMU, and a coupled dynamic region in which safe economic dispatch must take place.

We propose physics-constrained hybrid TD3 (PC-HybridTD3), a forecast-aware actor on a single time scale that emits
$a_t=(u_t^{\mathrm{th}},u_t^{\mathrm{bat}},m_t^{\mathrm{CAES}},z_t^{\mathrm{CAES}})$
already decoded onto $\mathcal{A}_f(s_t)$, with GiveSafe kept as a residual physical check~\cite{garcia2015safe,sun2024constrained,fan2019hybrid}. In contrast to post-hoc projection,
\[
a_t\sim\pi_\theta(s_t)\;\Rightarrow\; a_t\in\mathcal{A}_f(s_t),
\qquad\text{rather than}\qquad
a_t=\Pi_{\mathcal{A}_f}\bigl(\pi_\theta(s_t)\bigr).
\]
Only hours executed by the FMU enter the economic Bellman update. Table~\ref{tab:lit} situates the work among related studies.

The paper makes three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Heterogeneous hybrid action.} Thermal generation and battery storage are regulated continuously, while CAES is represented by a discrete operating mode and a continuous loading level. The same policy therefore avoids the degeneration that arises when a conventional continuous projection is applied to a disconnected operating region.
\item \textbf{Joint feasible support.} Device limits and system coupling---thermal ramping, storage inventories, CAES thermodynamic restrictions and interchange capacity---are assembled into a state-dependent support $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$ and written into action generation, so that the policy acts inside a dynamically admissible region. GiveSafe remains a residual physical check.
\item \textbf{Forecast-aware closed-loop evaluation.} Real-time plant state, day-ahead resource and load forecasts, and the TOU path are presented to the actor under a single operating-cost objective. Held-out weeks and an 8760~h rollout compare the learned policy with a heuristic, a rolling MILP and continuous-action TD3 on the same FMU.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:system} introduces the plant and the monthly TOU setting. Section~\ref{sec:problem} states the physics, the operating cost and the hybrid MDP. Section~\ref{sec:method} develops PC-HybridTD3. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the numerical study. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{System description}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Plant topology and modelling workflow}

Figure~\ref{fig:topology} summarises the multi-energy plant. The supply layer comprises wind, photovoltaic (PV) generation and a grid interface under TOU buy/sell prices. The conversion layer centres on a thermal unit commanded by a continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp}}$ and a CAES train commanded by discrete mode and continuous magnitude. The storage layer comprises a battery with continuous power command $u_{\mathrm{bat}}$ and CAES gas and thermal inventories (gas tank, hot and cold tanks). The demand layer balances electric load and grid exchange.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_topology}
\caption{Topology of the thermal--BESS--CAES multi-energy plant (wind, PV, thermal, battery, CAES, grid and load). Device physics are modelled in MWORKS Sysplorer and exported as an FMI FMU.}
\label{fig:topology}
\end{figure}

Plant dynamics follow first-principles multi-physics: thermal ramping and fuel use; electrochemical battery SoC with round-trip efficiency; multi-tank CAES gas storage with thermo-mechanical coupling and mode locks; renewable availability; and nodal power balance with curtailment and unserved residual. The complete Modelica model is constructed and parameterised in MWORKS Sysplorer and exported as an FMI-compliant FMU. Closed-loop optimisers and learning agents interact with this object at a one-hour communication interval, reading plant states and writing hybrid set-points. Exogenous TOU prices and resource profiles set the market and weather boundary; they do not replace the internal gas--thermal DAEs.

\subsection{Price-taker TOU market}

The plant is a price-taker on the Shandong industrial and commercial TOU tariff. Relative to the flat energy price, peak hours are raised by 70\% and valley hours are lowered by 70\%; a sharp-peak uplift of 100\% and a deep-valley discount of 90\% apply on designated hours, following the 2026 State Grid Shandong clock and Shandong DRC Price [2023] No.~914~\cite{ndrc2021tou,shandong2023tou,shandong2026tou}. Table~\ref{tab:tou-windows} lists the five month-groups of that clock. Delivered 110~kV buy prices in Table~\ref{tab:tou-monthly} are taken from the monthly agency-purchase notices for January through August; September through December repeat the August table until later monthly notices appear. TOU-floating adders (capacity compensation, line loss and system operation) follow that month's energy base, whereas T\&D energy and funds do not float~\cite{ndrc2023td}. The two-part 110~kV T\&D energy charge is $0.1191$~CNY/kWh through July (third regulatory cycle) and $0.106$~CNY/kWh from 1~August~2026 (fourth cycle; Shandong DRC Price [2026] No.~556)~\cite{shandong2026td}. The hourly series used by the twin unfolds this monthly path on the official clocks. The export price $0.1875$~CNY/kWh is a flat case-study assumption, not a published monthly sell tariff. Figure~\ref{fig:boundary} shows wind, irradiance and load on the three held-out weeks, together with the corresponding purchase TOU. Table~\ref{tab:params} lists plant ratings and the remaining market operating points.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

The weekly problem (\(T=168\)~h) is defined by the plant physics, the operating constraints and the market objective. Continuous-time DAEs of the thermal unit, battery, multi-tank CAES, renewables and grid interface are integrated in the Sysplorer FMU with communication interval $\tau_s=1$~h; the relations below are the dispatch-level models realised by that twin. The 8760~h calendar rotates weekly start times. Signs are as follows: renewable and thermal generation are non-negative when injecting power; battery and CAES charge is positive and discharge is negative; grid import is positive. Component sets are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=\{\mathrm{w},\mathrm{pv}\},\quad
I_2=\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{caes}\},\quad
I_3=\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},\\
I&=I_1\cup I_2\cup I_3.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sets}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Energy generation units}

\subsubsection{Photovoltaics}

PV available power depends on plane-of-array irradiance $G_t$, module surface temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}$ and nameplate capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}=260$~MW. With standard irradiance $G_{\mathrm{stc}}=1000$~W/m$^2$, standard temperature $T_{\mathrm{stc}}=298.15$~K, temperature coefficient $K_T=0.005$~K$^{-1}$ and conversion efficiency $\eta^{\mathrm{pv}}=0.95$,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}
=
\max\Bigl(
P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}
\frac{G_t}{G_{\mathrm{stc}}}
\bigl[1-K_T\bigl(T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}-T_{\mathrm{stc}}\bigr)\bigr]
\eta^{\mathrm{pv}},\,0\Bigr).
\label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
Module temperature is obtained from ambient temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{air}}$, wind speed $v_t$ and irradiance by the twin correlation
\begin{equation}
T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}
=
T_t^{\mathrm{air}}
+
0.0138\bigl(1+0.031(T_t^{\mathrm{air}}-273.15)\bigr)
\bigl(1-0.042\,v_t\bigr)G_t.
\label{eq:pv-temp}
\end{equation}
Actual injected PV power after bus curtailment satisfies $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$.

\subsubsection{Wind turbines}

Wind available power follows a cubic power curve with cut-in, rated and cut-out speeds $v_{\mathrm{ci}}=3$~m/s, $v_{\mathrm{r}}=10$~m/s and $v_{\mathrm{co}}=15$~m/s on capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{w}}=300$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}
=
\begin{cases}
0, & 0\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{ci}}\ \text{or}\ v_t\ge v_{\mathrm{co}},\\[2pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}\,\dfrac{v_t^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}}{v_{\mathrm{r}}^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}},
& v_{\mathrm{ci}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{r}},\\[6pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}, & v_{\mathrm{r}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{co}}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:wt}
\end{equation}
Turbine pitch is not a free decision variable. Actual wind injection obeys $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}$.

\subsection{Energy conversion units}

\subsubsection{Thermal generation}

Thermal electric power is commanded by the continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp},t}$:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{th}} = u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}},
\qquad
u_{\min}\le u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\le u_{\max},
\label{eq:th}
\end{equation}
with $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=150$~MW, $P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}=50$~MW, $u_{\min}=P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}/P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=1/3$ and $u_{\max}=1$ (no shut-down). Inter-hour ramping is enforced on the actual thermal power,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|P_t^{\mathrm{th}}-P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{th}}\bigr|
\le
\Delta_{\mathrm{th}}^{\max}
=
r_{\max}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}\,\tau_s,
\label{eq:th-ramp}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\max}=0.0025/60$~s$^{-1}$ is the Modelica load-rate limit (equivalently $0.15$~p.u.\ per hour). Fuel and O\&M costs enter $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ in~\eqref{eq:dJ}.

\subsubsection{CAES conversion}

CAES is a hybrid conversion--storage plant. The hybrid command comprises a discrete mode and continuous magnitude
\begin{equation}
m_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\},\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:caes-cmd}
\end{equation}
mapped to a signed electric power command on capacity $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}=150$~MW,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
=
\begin{cases}
+\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{charge},\\
0, & m=\mathrm{idle},\\
-\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{discharge},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:caes-p}
\end{equation}
or equivalently $P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}=u_{\mathrm{caes},t}P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}$ with the non-convex legal set
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm{caes},t}
\in
[-1,-0.33]\cup\{0\}\cup[0.86,1]
\label{eq:caes-legal}
\end{equation}
encoding minimum compressor/expander loading. Inventory margins and mode locks further restrict $\mathcal{K}(s)$ and $\mathcal{M}_k(s)$. The live stack does \emph{not} impose a consecutive-run dwell (\texttt{min\_run=1}); start-up events are priced in $C_t^{\mathrm{su}}$ following Cui et al.~\cite{cui2024caesbess}. Electric power is realised as planned ($P^{\mathrm{act}}=P^{\mathrm{plan}}$); mass-flow set-points of air and thermal fluid scale with $|P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}|$ and reverse direction between compression and expansion.

\subsection{Energy storage units}

\subsubsection{Battery}

Battery power is commanded by $u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\in[-1,1]$ on $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}=100$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{bat}} = u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}.
\label{eq:bat-p}
\end{equation}
With energy capacity $E_{\mathrm{cap}}=500$~MWh and discharge efficiency $\eta=0.85$ (charge path lossless as in the twin), the SoC dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{\mathrm{bat}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=
\begin{cases}
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/E_{\mathrm{cap}}, & P^{\mathrm{bat}}\ge 0,\\[2pt]
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/(E_{\mathrm{cap}}\,\eta), & P^{\mathrm{bat}}<0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bat-soc}
\end{equation}
or, in hourly form with $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$ and $[x]_-=\max(-x,0)$,
\begin{equation}
s_{t+1,\mathrm{bat}}
=
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}}
+
\tau_s\frac{[P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_+ - [P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_-/\eta}{E_{\mathrm{cap}}}.
\label{eq:bat-soc-d}
\end{equation}
Inventory bounds are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}\in[0.1,0.9]$.

\subsubsection{CAES inventories}

CAES energy is stored as compressed air and two thermal fluid tanks. Gas SoC is pressure-based with rating $p_{\mathrm{norm}}=100$~bar,
\begin{equation}
s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}
=
\frac{p_t}{p_{\mathrm{norm}}},
\qquad
\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}}\le s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}\le \overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},
\label{eq:gas-soc}
\end{equation}
with $(\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},\overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}})=(0.6,1.0)$. Fixed-volume gas-tank mass and energy balances (ideal-air medium) evolve pressure $p$ and specific enthalpy $h$ under port mass flows $\dot m_{\mathrm{a}},\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}$ and wall heat $q_{\mathrm{w}}$,
\begin{align}
V\bigl(\partial_p\rho\,\dot p+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b}},
\label{eq:gas-mass}\\
V\bigl(-\dot p+\partial_p\rho\,\dot p\,h+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\,h+\rho\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}h_{\mathrm{a}}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}h_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathrm{out}}
+
q_{\mathrm{w}},
\label{eq:gas-energy}
\end{align}
with $q_{\mathrm{w}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{w}}S(T_{\mathrm{w}}-T)$ and gas temperature $T=T(p,h)$ (clipped at $253$~K). Hot and cold tanks store water at fixed pressure with free surface area $A$ and total volume $V_0=1.5\times10^{4}$~m$^3$. Level-based inventories
\begin{equation}
s_{t,i}
=
\frac{\ell_{t,i}}{V_0/A},
\qquad
i\in\{\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:thermal-soc}
\end{equation}
obey mass and enthalpy balances when within $(\underline{s}_i,\overline{s}_i)=(0.05,0.95)$,
\begin{equation}
\dot m_i=\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i},\qquad
m_i\dot h_i
=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}h_{\mathrm{a},i}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i}h_{\mathrm{b},i}^{\mathrm{out}}+Q_{\mathrm{gen},i}.
\label{eq:tank-bal}
\end{equation}
Compression ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}>0$) charges the gas inventory and drives the thermal circuit one way; expansion ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}<0$) reverses the flows. Primary weekly recovery inventories are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}$ and $s_{\mathrm{gas}}$; hot/cold SoCs and temperatures $(T^{\mathrm{hot}},T^{\mathrm{cold}})$ are process states used in soft shaping. All inventories obey~\eqref{eq:socbox}.

\subsection{System operation constraints}

The electric bus closes the power balance with curtailment and unserved load. Let $P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$ be renewable availability and $P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}$ the scheduled demand. Residual power after dispatchable assets is absorbed first by curtailing wind, then PV; residual deficit is met by demand response down to $20\%$ of plan, with remainder counted as unserved:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}}+P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}+P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
+P_t^{\mathrm{w}}+P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}+P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}
=
P_t^{\mathrm{load}},\\
&0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},\quad
0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}\ge 0,\quad
P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}\ge 0,\quad
0.2\,P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
Here $P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}$ is residual surplus after wind-then-PV curtailment and $P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}$ is residual shortfall after load reduction. Box and ramp limits, inventory bounds and grid exchange limits are
\begin{equation}
\underline{P}_{i}\le P_t^{i}\le \overline{P}_{i},\qquad
\bigl|P_t^{i}-P_{t-1}^{i}\bigr|\le \Delta_i^{\max},
\quad i\in\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{bat}\},
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\underline{s}_{i}\le s_{t,i}\le \overline{s}_{i},\qquad i\in\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:socbox}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min}\le P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}\le P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max},
\label{eq:grid}
\end{equation}
with $(P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min},P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max})=(-500,500)$~MW. The feasibility oracle uses the signed command identity (grid buy positive; battery and CAES charge positive)
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}
=
P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}} u_t^{\mathrm{th}}
-
P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}} u_t^{\mathrm{bat}}
-
P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}} u_t^{\mathrm{CAES}}
-
R_t,
\label{eq:pgrid-u}
\end{equation}
where $R_t$ is the twin residual $P^{\mathrm{w}}+P^{\mathrm{pv}}+P^{\mathrm{load}}$ under FMU signs (load positive, renewable generation negative). Equation~\eqref{eq:pgrid-u} is the interchange slice used by the joint decoder;~\eqref{eq:balance} remains the bus accounting used in $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$. Actions that would violate~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:grid},~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} or CAES mode locks lie outside $\mathcal{F}(s_t)$. The analytic decoder enumerates $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dev}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{grid}}$; predicted next-state inventory and residual twin constraints remain in GiveSafe.

Weekly operation records an optional energy-SoC recovery score on primary inventories, following the soft indicator of~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} rather than a hard action override or feasibility veto,
\begin{equation}
L_1^{\mathrm{e}}
= \bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{bat}}-s_{0,\mathrm{bat}}\bigr|
+ \bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{gas}}-s_{0,\mathrm{gas}}\bigr|
\le \varepsilon,
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon=0.06$. Operating boxes remain $s_{\mathrm{bat}}\in[0.1,0.9]$. The battery command is not rewritten at week-end;~\eqref{eq:soc} only gates the terminal bonus in~\eqref{eq:term}.

\subsection{Optimization objectives}

Let $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ index hours in a weekly episode ($T=168$). Grid purchase/sale energies are $E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_+$ and $E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_-$ (MWh). Device operating costs $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ aggregate thermal fuel/O\&M and storage energy-bookkeeping cash increments exported by the twin (battery cycle life is not in Modelica income; see $C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}$). Under price-taker TOU prices $\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$, the hourly \emph{cash} increment is
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{cash}}
=
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}} E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}
-
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{op}}.
\label{eq:dJ}
\end{equation}
Step CO$_2$ mass uses emission factors $\eta_{\mathrm{th}},\eta_{\mathrm{grid}}$ (tCO$_2$/MWh) on thermal energy and grid imports,
\begin{equation}
\Delta m_t
=
\eta_{\mathrm{th}} E_t^{\mathrm{th}}
+
\eta_{\mathrm{grid}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}},
\label{eq:co2}
\end{equation}
with carbon priced at the reproducible 2024 national ETS year-end composite close $\pi_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=97.49$~CNY/tCO$_2$e (2024 band $69$--$106$, peak $105.65$)~\cite{mee2025etsnews,mee2025etsreport}. Sensitivity retains the legacy operating point $80$~\cite{icap2025chinaets} and a GHTD3 literature cross-check $\approx 86.4$~CNY/t ($0.012$~USD/kg at $7.2$~CNY/USD)~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,worldbank2024usdnyc}. The default Python settlement uses an intensity-benchmark mode: thermal output accumulates an intensity allowance $A_t=\beta E_t^{\mathrm{th}}$ against emissions $E_t=\eta_{\mathrm{th}}E_t^{\mathrm{th}}$ and settles the position $Q=A-E$ at episode end, while grid-import CO$_2$ remains a step charge $\pi_{\mathrm{CO_2}}\eta_{\mathrm{grid}}E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}$. Here $\beta=0.8049$~t/MWh is the official 2024 Class~II (conventional coal, this plant's $150$~MW rating) \emph{generation benchmark}~\cite{mee2024quota}, $\eta_{\mathrm{grid}}=0.6191$~t/MWh is the official 2023 Shandong location-based electricity factor~\cite{mee2023ef}, and $\eta_{\mathrm{th}}=0.85$~t/MWh is a plant direct-emission \emph{scenario} factor (not identical to $\beta$). A flat step tax $\pi_{\mathrm{CO_2}}\Delta m_t$ is retained as a sensitivity. Carbon is treated as an external ETS-style charge rather than a second fuel bill. Curtailment and unserved energies $E_t^{\mathrm{curt}},E_t^{\mathrm{uns}}$ follow bus residuals and enter a Cui-style spill cost~\cite{cui2025ghtd3},
\begin{equation}
C_t^{CUT}
=
\nu_{\mathrm{curt}} E_t^{\mathrm{curt}}
+
\nu_{\mathrm{uns}} E_t^{\mathrm{uns}},
\label{eq:cut}
\end{equation}
with operating points $\nu_{\mathrm{curt}}=300$~CNY/MWh (cross-checked against GHTD3 $\nu_{\mathrm{res}}=0.041$~USD/kWh~$\approx 295$~CNY/MWh) and $\nu_{\mathrm{uns}}=1000$~CNY/MWh as a conservative unserved penalty well below typical VOLL survey ranges~\cite{schroeder2015voll}---objective coefficients, not regulated tariffs. Battery \emph{cycle} cost is accounted outside pure energy bookkeeping: device-level battery income in the plant model reflects energy transfer only, whereas capital wear is represented by a convex cumulative-discharge term $\psi(\delta)=a_0\delta^{p}$ with $p\approx 2.03$~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,cui2024caesbess}. We calibrate $a_0$ so that lifetime discharge exhausts capital cost (LCOS-style amortisation~\cite{xu2022lcos}). Let $\delta_t$ be episode cumulative \emph{discharge} energy (MWh; charge does not increase $\delta$) and $\delta_0=\rho E_{\mathrm{life}}$ an optional mid-life offset. On the weekly protocol used here we set $\rho=0$, so wear starts from a zero discharge tally; a positive offset is reserved for continuous-year books. With lifetime discharge $E_{\mathrm{life}}=N_{\mathrm{cycle}}\mathrm{DoD}_{\mathrm{eq}}E_{\mathrm{cap}}$ and total capital $\mathrm{Capex}=c_{\mathrm{kWh}}E_{\mathrm{cap}}$,
\begin{equation}
\psi(\delta)=a_0\,\delta^{p},\quad
a_0=\frac{\mathrm{Capex}}{E_{\mathrm{life}}^{p}},\quad
p=2.03,\quad
C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}
=
\psi(\delta_0+\delta_t)-\psi(\delta_0+\delta_{t-1}),
\label{eq:deg}
\end{equation}
hence $\psi(E_{\mathrm{life}})=\mathrm{Capex}$ and degradation becomes strictly more expensive as cumulative discharge grows. Default plant-scale numbers match the battery capacity ($E_{\mathrm{cap}}=500$~MWh) with a stationary LFP-class economic point $c_{\mathrm{kWh}}=1000$~CNY/kWh, $N_{\mathrm{cycle}}=5000$ and $\mathrm{DoD}_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.8$~\cite{xu2022lcos}. CAES mode switches incur a Cui Table~2 start-up coefficient $c_{\mathrm{su}}=3.42$~USD at $800$~kW~\cite{cui2024caesbess}, converted at $7.2$~CNY/USD~\cite{worldbank2024usdnyc}; scaling linearly to the plant $P_{\mathrm{cap}}=150$~MW is an explicit \emph{extrapolation} (sensitivity: unscaled / linear / $\sqrt{P}$). Soft interchange-contract excess beyond a scenario band $P_{\lim}=\pm200$~MW is priced as
\begin{equation}
C_t^{\mathrm{grid}}
=
\nu_{\mathrm{grid}}\max\bigl(0,\,|P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}|-P_{\lim}\bigr)\Delta t,
\label{eq:grid-contract}
\end{equation}
with scenario $\nu_{\mathrm{grid}}=600$~CNY/MWh (not a statutory fee; the FMU hard interchange remains $\pm500$~MW). The comprehensive monetary objective is
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}
=
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{cash}}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{CO_2}}
-
C_t^{CUT}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{su}}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{grid}},
\qquad
\max_{\{a_t\}}\; J^{\mathrm{gen}} = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:pv}\text{--}\eqref{eq:grid},\
a_t\in\mathcal{A}_f(s_t)\subseteq\mathcal{F}(s_t).
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:J} is the negative of~\eqref{eq:C}, with $C_t^{\mathrm{thermal}}$ inside $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$. Policies are compared on $CC=\sum_t C_t$. Hour-scale hybrid actions couple to week-scale inventory, carbon, spill, wear, start-ups and contracted interchange;~\eqref{eq:soc} enters only as a terminal bonus.

\subsection{State-dependent hybrid-action MDP}
\label{sec:pamdp}

Closed-loop control is forecast-aware coordination of thermal generation, BESS, CAES and grid exchange. The MDP is $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A}_f(\cdot),P,r,\gamma\rangle$ with $\gamma=0.99$. The state is
\begin{equation}
s_t=[x_t,f_t,p_t],
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
where $x_t$ is the FMU physical vector (battery / gas / hot / cold SoC, gas pressure and temperatures, thermal / battery / CAES / grid / wind / PV / load / curtailment / unserved powers; 19 plant channels), $f_t$ is the 24~h-ahead forecast $\{\hat v,\hat G,\hat T_a,\hat P_{\mathrm{load}}\}_{t:t+23}$, and $p_t$ is the TOU path $\{\lambda^{\mathrm{buy}},\lambda^{\mathrm{sell}}\}_{t:t+23}$. The reported observation dimension is $166$. The heterogeneous hybrid action is
\begin{equation}
a_t
=
\bigl(u_t^{\mathrm{th}},\, u_t^{\mathrm{bat}},\, m_t^{\mathrm{CAES}},\, z_t^{\mathrm{CAES}}\bigr),
\qquad
m_t^{\mathrm{CAES}}\in\{D,I,C\},\quad
z_t^{\mathrm{CAES}}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:action-hyb}
\end{equation}
Device-level hybrid support (CAES topology only) is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dev}}(s)
=
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{tp}}(s)\times
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bat}}(s)\times
\bigcup_{k\in\mathcal{K}(s)}
\bigl(\{k\}\times\mathcal{M}_k(s)\bigr).
\label{eq:adev}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{K}(s)\subseteq\{\mathrm{dis},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{chg}\}$ is the admissible mode set from mode locks and inventory margins, and $\mathcal{M}_k(s)=[\underline{u}_k(s),\overline{u}_k(s)]$ is the dynamic magnitude interval for mode~$k$ returned by the feasibility oracle (twin physics plus engineered margins; sensitivity in Section~\ref{sec:results} separates the two). Idle is a point mass. Interchange couples the three physical commands through~\eqref{eq:pgrid-u}. Writing $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{grid}}(s)$ for the slice of $(u^{\mathrm{th}},u^{\mathrm{bat}},u^{\mathrm{CAES}})$ that meets~\eqref{eq:grid}, the legal hybrid support is the intersection, not the cartesian product of device boxes:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}_f(s)
=
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{dev}}(s)\cap\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{grid}}(s).
\label{eq:support}
\end{equation}
The physical FMU command is the continuous triple
\begin{equation}
a_t = \bigl(u_{\mathrm{tp},t},\, u_{\mathrm{bat},t},\, u_{\mathrm{caes},t}\bigr),
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
decoded from the \emph{current} interval rather than from a static device band alone,
\begin{equation}
u_t^{\mathrm{CAES}}
=
\begin{cases}
l_D(s_t)+z_t\bigl[h_D(s_t)-l_D(s_t)\bigr], & m=D,\\
0, & m=I,\\
l_C(s_t)+z_t\bigl[h_C(s_t)-l_C(s_t)\bigr], & m=C.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:decode}
\end{equation}
When margins vanish,~\eqref{eq:decode} collapses to the device envelope in Fig.~\ref{fig:caes-legal}. A Boolean mask $M(s)\in\{0,1\}^3$ encodes $\mathcal{K}(s)$. After $u^{\mathrm{CAES}}$ is fixed, the thermal and battery heads are remapped onto the residual interchange intervals (Section~\ref{sec:hybrid-actor}). Predicted next-state inventories and residual twin constraints that cannot be written as intervals remain with GiveSafe. Transitions integrate~\eqref{eq:pv}--\eqref{eq:tank-bal} for one plant hour under $a_t\in\mathcal{A}_f(s_t)\subseteq\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{fig_caes_legal}
\caption{Legal CAES electric command. (a)~Device envelope with discharge, idle and charge bands. (b)~State-dependent intervals $\mathcal{M}_k(s)$ that may shrink or drop a band.}
\label{fig:caes-legal}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Reward function for learning}
\label{sec:reward}

With calibration constant $C_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx1.565\times10^{5}$~CNY (rule-controller $p_{95}$ step cash magnitude),
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{econ}} = \frac{\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}}{C_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
Potential-based inventory shaping uses the weighted L1 distance $L_{1,t}$ of (battery, gas, hot, cold) relative to episode start:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}
= \kappa_t \bigl(L_{1,t-1}-L_{1,t}\bigr)
- \kappa_t^{\mathrm{abs}} L_{1,t},
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
with base $\kappa=5$ and a recovery-window scale-up over the last $H=40$~h. At a completed, failure-free terminal step the Cui-style indicator is
\begin{equation}
r_T^{\mathrm{term}}
=
\begin{cases}
B, & L_1^{\mathrm{e}}\le\varepsilon,\\
0, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:term}
\end{equation}
with $B=15$. Falling outside~\eqref{eq:soc} simply withholds the bonus; the week remains valid and no utilisation incentive is added. The extrinsic reward is the sum of the three terms
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{ext}}
= r_t^{\mathrm{econ}}+r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}+r_t^{\mathrm{term}}.
\label{eq:rext}
\end{equation}
Classical programmes must convexify~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} and the CAES thermo-mechanical coupling; PC-HybridTD3 (Section~\ref{sec:method}) optimises~\eqref{eq:J} by closed-loop interaction with the plant twin under support~\eqref{eq:support}. Rolling MILP optimises a linear energy surrogate and is evaluated on the same FMU.

\section{Solution methodology}

\subsection{Same-timescale hybrid policy}
\label{sec:same-timescale}

PC-HybridTD3~\cite{fujimoto2018td3,fan2019hybrid} is a single-timescale actor--critic. Thermal and battery commands are continuous; CAES uses a mode--magnitude pair because its electric envelope is disconnected. Hierarchical methods remain appropriate when independent slow inventory targets dominate; they are not required here.

\subsection{Twin-closed loop}
\label{sec:twin-loop}
At each hour the controller assembles $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$ from device boxes and interchange, decodes $\pi_{\theta}(s,\mathcal{A}_f(s))$ (greedy $\arg\max$ mode at evaluation) and offers the physical triple to GiveSafe. An accepted command steps the FMU and the resulting transition enters the economic replay $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{B}}$. Rejections do not advance the plant and are stored only in a safety set $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{S}}$. During exploration the actor may be resampled up to $N_{\mathrm{try}}=64$ times; evaluation uses a single attempt. Training begins with $N_{\mathrm{warm}}=1024$ feasible random hours so that charge and discharge are both visited before the first gradient step. Figure~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarises the loop.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_algorithm}
\caption{PC-HybridTD3. A forecast-aware state feeds a hybrid actor; a joint feasible-support decoder maps thermal, battery and CAES commands onto $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$; GiveSafe is the last physical check before the FMU steps.}
\label{fig:algorithm}
\end{figure}


\subsection{Parameterized hybrid actor}
\label{sec:hybrid-actor}

The actor emits
\begin{equation}
a_t
=
\bigl(u_t^{\mathrm{th}},\,u_t^{\mathrm{bat}},\,m_t,\,z_t\bigr),
\qquad
m_t\in\{D,I,C\},\quad
z_t\in[0,1].
\label{eq:pi-factor}
\end{equation}
Thermal and battery heads map bounded latents by a sigmoid onto the current boxes $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{tp}}(s)$ and $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bat}}(s)$. The CAES head emits three mode logits $\ell\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ and one magnitude logit. With Boolean mask $M(s)\in\{0,1\}^3$,
\begin{equation}
\tilde\ell
=
\ell + \log\bigl(M(s)+\varepsilon\bigr)
\quad\text{(illegal modes set to $-\infty$)},
\label{eq:mask}
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{softmax}(\tilde\ell)$ is supported only on $\mathcal{K}(s)$. Evaluation and the TD3 target take $m'=\arg\max_m\tilde\ell(s')$. The physical CAES command is decoded on the \emph{current} oracle intervals,
\begin{equation}
u^{\mathrm{CAES}}
=
\begin{cases}
\underline{u}_D(s)+z\bigl(\overline{u}_D(s)-\underline{u}_D(s)\bigr), & m=D,\\
0, & m=I,\\
\underline{u}_C(s)+z\bigl(\overline{u}_C(s)-\underline{u}_C(s)\bigr), & m=C,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
which is~\eqref{eq:decode}. Idle is a point mass. Device boxes are then coupled through~\eqref{eq:pgrid-u}. Let $(u^{\mathrm{th}}_{\mathrm{lo}},u^{\mathrm{th}}_{\mathrm{hi}})$ and $(u^{\mathrm{bat}}_{\mathrm{lo}},u^{\mathrm{bat}}_{\mathrm{hi}})$ be the current thermal and battery boxes, $P_T,P_B,P_C$ the device ratings and $(G_{\min},G_{\max})$ the interchange band (oracle margins included). The CAES grid window is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\underline{u}_C^{\mathrm{g}}
&=
\bigl(P_T u^{\mathrm{th}}_{\mathrm{lo}}-P_B u^{\mathrm{bat}}_{\mathrm{hi}}-R_t-G_{\max}\bigr)/P_C,\\
\overline{u}_C^{\mathrm{g}}
&=
\bigl(P_T u^{\mathrm{th}}_{\mathrm{hi}}-P_B u^{\mathrm{bat}}_{\mathrm{lo}}-R_t-G_{\min}\bigr)/P_C.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:caes-window}
\end{equation}
Each mode interval $\mathcal{M}_k(s)$ is intersected with $[\underline{u}_C^{\mathrm{g}},\overline{u}_C^{\mathrm{g}}]$; empty modes are masked. After $u^{\mathrm{CAES}}$ is decoded, thermal is remapped onto the residual interval for which some battery command still meets the band,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\underline{u}^{\mathrm{th}\star}
&=
\bigl(G_{\min}+P_B u^{\mathrm{bat}}_{\mathrm{lo}}+P_C u^{\mathrm{CAES}}+R_t\bigr)/P_T,\\
\overline{u}^{\mathrm{th}\star}
&=
\bigl(G_{\max}+P_B u^{\mathrm{bat}}_{\mathrm{hi}}+P_C u^{\mathrm{CAES}}+R_t\bigr)/P_T,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:th-window}
\end{equation}
and battery is remapped given $(u^{\mathrm{th}},u^{\mathrm{CAES}})$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\underline{u}^{\mathrm{bat}\star}
&=
\bigl(P_T u^{\mathrm{th}}-P_C u^{\mathrm{CAES}}-R_t-G_{\max}\bigr)/P_B,\\
\overline{u}^{\mathrm{bat}\star}
&=
\bigl(P_T u^{\mathrm{th}}-P_C u^{\mathrm{CAES}}-R_t-G_{\min}\bigr)/P_B.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:bat-window}
\end{equation}
No online quadratic programme is required. GiveSafe then checks predicted next-state inventories and remaining twin constraints; evaluation queries the actor once. Clamping $u^{\mathrm{CAES}}$ after the fact is used only as an audit of the decoder.

Because the discrete mode is not differentiable, actor updates use Straight-Through Gumbel--Softmax, with a hard one-hot in the forward pass, a soft gradient in the backward pass, and temperature $\tau_{\mathrm{G}}$ annealed from $1.0$ to $0.2$.

\subsection{Hybrid critic and TD3 target}
\label{sec:critic}

Twin critics $Q_{\phi_1},Q_{\phi_2}$ score the executed physical triple $(u^{\mathrm{th}},u^{\mathrm{bat}},u_{\mathrm{caes}})$. Writing $Q=\min(Q_{\phi_1},Q_{\phi_2})$ and clipping $Q$ at $\pm 200$, the Bellman target is the standard TD3 backup
\begin{equation}
y
=
r^{\mathrm{ext}}+\gamma(1-d)\,Q_{\bar\phi}\bigl(s',u^{\mathrm{th}\prime},u^{\mathrm{bat}\prime},u_{\mathrm{caes}}'\bigr),
\label{eq:soft-v}
\end{equation}
where the target actor is deterministic in the mode,
\begin{equation}
m'=\arg\max_{m}\tilde\ell_{\bar\theta}(s'),
\label{eq:target-mode}
\end{equation}
clipped Gaussian noise is added \emph{only} to the magnitude,
\begin{equation}
\tilde z'=\mathrm{clip}\bigl(z'(s')+\varepsilon,0,1\bigr),
\qquad
\varepsilon\sim\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-c,c\bigr),
\label{eq:target-mag}
\end{equation}
and $\tilde z'$ is re-decoded with the \emph{next-state} intervals $\mathcal{M}_{m'}(s')$. Noise is not added to the decoded $u_{\mathrm{caes}}$. The twin critics minimise
\begin{equation}
L_Q
=
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{B}}}
\bigl[
(Q_{\phi_1}-y)^2+(Q_{\phi_2}-y)^2
\bigr].
\label{eq:lq}
\end{equation}
The actor (delayed, policy delay $D=2$) maximises $Q_{\phi_1}$ on the currently decoded hybrid action
\begin{equation}
L_{\pi}
=
\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{B}}}
\bigl[-Q_{\phi_1}(s,u^{\mathrm{th}},u^{\mathrm{bat}},u_{\mathrm{caes}})\bigr].
\label{eq:lpi}
\end{equation}
Only physical FMU transitions in $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{B}}$ enter~\eqref{eq:lq}--\eqref{eq:lpi}.

\subsection{Network architecture}
\label{sec:network}

The actor encoder is a two-layer MLP ($256$--ReLU--$256$--ReLU) on the $166$-dimensional observation. Linear heads emit thermal and battery commands, three CAES mode logits and one magnitude logit. Twin critics take the concatenated physical packing $(s,u^{\mathrm{th}},u^{\mathrm{bat}},u_{\mathrm{caes}})$. All Adam learning rates are $3\times10^{-4}$; gradient norms are clipped at $10$. Target networks track with $\tau=5\times10^{-3}$.

\subsection{Physical-only economic replay}
\label{sec:givesafe}

During exploration the controller may draw up to $N_{\mathrm{try}}=64$ candidates from $\pi_{\theta}$; evaluation uses a single draw. A candidate is executed only if it lies in $\mathcal{F}(s)$. Accepted transitions $(s,a,r^{\mathrm{ext}},s')$ enter $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{B}}$ and drive~\eqref{eq:lq}--\eqref{eq:lpi}. Rejected proposals leave the plant unmoved and are recorded in $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{S}}$ for later audit. GiveSafe is therefore a residual screen on the joint decoder~\cite{garcia2015safe,sun2024constrained}, not a substitute for it.

\subsection{Ablations and classical baselines}
\label{sec:ablation}

Two controls isolate the two modelling claims. (i)~\textbf{Continuous-projection TD3} maps a single CAES latent by
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm{caes}}
=
\Pi_{\mathrm{bands}}\bigl(\tanh(z)\bigr),
\label{eq:project}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_{\mathrm{bands}}$ maps the open gap to idle and otherwise clamps to the nearest charge or discharge band. (ii)~\textbf{Component-support hybrid TD3} retains the mode mask~\eqref{eq:mask} but decodes magnitude on device boxes without the sequential grid-coupled decoder, isolating the joint support $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$. Fig.~\ref{fig:action-rep} places the three encodings on the same $u_{\mathrm{caes}}$ axis. Panel~(a) is~\eqref{eq:project}: a latent that lands in the illegal gap is forced to idle. Panel~(b) is the static-band hybrid: the actor first chooses a mode, then uses the full device charge or discharge band. Panel~(c) is PC-HybridTD3: the same hybrid heads map magnitude only through the currently feasible interval $\mathcal{M}_k(s)$. Section~\ref{sec:results} compares these learners with a price-aware heuristic and a rolling MILP on a linear energy surrogate, all evaluated on the same FMU.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{PC-HybridTD3 on the plant FMU}
\label{alg:pc-hybridtd3}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Actor $\pi_{\theta}$, twin critics, buffers $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{B}}$ and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{S}}$
\State Collect $N_{\mathrm{warm}}=1024$ feasible random hours on $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$
\For{episode $=1,2,\ldots$ until the physical-step budget}
  \State Reset Sysplorer FMU on a \emph{training}-week start; $s\leftarrow s_0$
  \For{$t=0$ to $T-1$}
    \State Device boxes $\leftarrow$ oracle; CAES $\cap$ grid window; mask empty modes
    \State Decode $u^{\mathrm{CAES}}$, tighten thermal, then battery (joint $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$)
    \State $a^{\star}\leftarrow$ GiveSafe($s,a$; $N_{\mathrm{try}}=64$ if exploring, $1$ if evaluating)
    \If{no legal $a^{\star}$}
      \State record the first oracle reason in $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{S}}$ and end the episode
    \EndIf
    \State Step FMU one hour with $a^{\star}$; observe $s'$, $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$
    \State store $(s,a^{\star},r^{\mathrm{ext}},s',m,z,\mathcal{M}(s),\mathcal{M}(s'))$ in $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{B}}$
    \State Target: $m'\leftarrow\arg\max$; noise on $z'$ only; re-decode with $\mathcal{M}(s')$
    \State Update critics~\eqref{eq:lq}; delayed actor~\eqref{eq:lpi} with Straight-Through Gumbel
    \State $s\leftarrow s'$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\Ensure Learned physics-constrained policy $\pi_{\theta}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Experimental setting}
\label{sec:setup}

All methods interact with the same Sysplorer FMU, the monthly 110~kV TOU of Table~\ref{tab:tou-monthly} and identical resource files. Each weekly episode lasts \(T=168\)~h. The 8760~h calendar supplies rotating start times; inventories are re-initialised at each reset. A subsequent 8760~h rollout, without retraining, is used as a deployment check and is not mixed with the held-out weekly scores. The 52 complete weeks are stratified by quarter into $9$ training, $2$ validation and $2$ test weeks per season. Training starts are drawn from the training set only; checkpoints are selected on validation; reported tables use the test weeks. Seeds $0,1,2$ share a physical interaction budget of $3$--$5\times10^{5}$ hours. Hyperparameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}. The main comparison assumes a perfect 24~h forecast; a noisy observation case is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:deployment}.

Four methods are compared: a price-aware heuristic, a rolling MILP on a linear energy surrogate, continuous-projection TD3, and PC-HybridTD3. The two learning ablations isolate the hybrid representation and the joint support $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$. Held-out evaluation is deterministic. GiveSafe remains active for every learner. Learning curves of the three DRL variants are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:training}.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:support}

The comparison is organised from operating cost to renewable uptake and reliability, then to constraint satisfaction; Figs.~\ref{fig:dispatch}--\ref{fig:kpi} explain those system quantities. Quantitative cells will be completed once each method finishes a full held-out week on the monthly 110~kV path. Appendix~\ref{app:annual-carbon} records the intensity carbon book and the weekly-reset convention; it does not by itself establish annual optimality of the learned policy.

Two limitations should be kept in view. The live magnitude intervals $\mathcal{M}_k(s)$ mix twin physics with oracle margins, and the main study resets inventories each week. GiveSafe is adopted from the safety-filter literature. September--December buy levels in Table~\ref{tab:tou-monthly} carry forward the last published month until official tables appear.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

Weekly multi-energy dispatch under a monthly Shandong TOU tariff and a national ETS carbon price is a coordinated problem: thermal generation, BESS, CAES and the grid must be moved together inside a state-dependent support $\mathcal{A}_f(s)$ induced by a DAE plant twin. The plant is a first-principles Sysplorer Modelica model, compiled as an FMI FMU, on which both classical programmes and learned policies are evaluated. A single policy issues continuous thermal and battery commands and a CAES mode--magnitude pair; interchange capacity couples the three through the sequential decoder~\eqref{eq:caes-window}--\eqref{eq:bat-window}. PC-HybridTD3 learns on that support: the TD3 target keeps a deterministic CAES mode and perturbs only magnitude, and only FMU-executed hours enter the economic Bellman update.

The numerical comparison is a weekly study on the 2026 monthly 110~kV path, with a stratified train/validation/test split and four methods scored on completed 168~h episodes. Operating cost is the ranking quantity; curtailment, unserved energy and constraint satisfaction complete the picture. Hierarchical RL remains appropriate when slow inventory targets dominate, and a learned policy need not undercut a rolling MILP on cash. The present limitations are the engineered margins inside $\mathcal{M}_k(s)$, the weekly inventory reset, and September--December buy levels that still carry the last published month.
