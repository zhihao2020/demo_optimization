\shorttitle{Safe Market-GHTD3 for multi-energy plant scheduling}
\title[mode=title]{Safe market-oriented hierarchical TD3 for hybrid multi-energy plant scheduling under time-of-use tariffs}

\begin{abstract}
Coordinating thermal generation, battery storage and compressed-air energy storage (CAES) under time-of-use (TOU) tariffs is a hybrid-action, multi-timescale decision problem with hard operational constraints. When the plant is represented by a high-fidelity Modelica--FMU digital twin, classical mathematical programmes require strong convexification, while single-timescale deep reinforcement learning (DRL) struggles with week-scale inventory recovery and unsafe exploration. This paper formulates weekly price-taker operation as a goal-conditioned hierarchical Markov decision process and proposes Safe Market-GHTD3: a high-level twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) controller issues Modelica-aligned market- and recovery-aware plant intents every $c$ steps; a hybrid-anchored residual low-level TD3 corrects a frozen single-layer hybrid policy in action space; and a GiveSafe filter projects proposals onto a dynamic feasible set before each FMU step. The residual interface guarantees that a zero goal recovers the hybrid baseline and restores a non-vanishing goal-to-action pathway that naive absolute-action goal conditioning can lose under large-scale plant observations. On three representative weeks under Shandong industrial/commercial TOU tariffs, Safe Market-GHTD3 satisfies energy state-of-charge (SoC) gates and improves closed-loop episode reward over a strong hybrid TD3 baseline in all seasons (about $+2.14$, $+1.98$ and $+2.47$), while remaining far above rule, linprog and SAC baselines. Ablations and supporting continuous-year/cross-domain tests further clarify market priors and protocol choice.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Weekly hybrid multi-energy scheduling is cast as a goal-conditioned hierarchical MDP on an FMU twin.
\item Hybrid-anchored action residual restores goal-to-action control; GiveSafe enforces feasible execution.
\item Three-season closed-loop results beat hybrid TD3 in all seasons with weekly SoC recovery.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Deep decarbonisation is accelerating the integration of renewable generation into multi-energy plants that co-dispatch electricity, heat-related conversion and storage~\cite{zhou2023iesdrl,yang2024planning}. Industrial and commercial customers increasingly face provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs that create strong peak--valley incentives for storage arbitrage and thermal load shifting~\cite{ndrc2021tou,shandong2023tou}. At the plant level, however, scheduling must respect hybrid action spaces (continuous set-points together with discrete CAES modes), ramp and inventory limits, and long-horizon recovery of energy state-of-charge (SoC). When the plant dynamics are provided by a high-fidelity Modelica--FMU twin rather than a convex energy-balance surrogate, the decision problem becomes a computationally expensive hybrid Markov decision process (MDP).

Model-based optimisers (mixed-integer programmes, rolling linearisations, particle swarm heuristics) remain valuable baselines, yet they either require aggressive convexification of non-convex CAES physics or suffer from limited budgets in high-dimensional hybrid spaces~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Data-driven deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{sutton2018rl,fujimoto2018td3,haarnoja2018sac} learns policies by closed-loop interaction and is therefore attractive for nonlinear twins. Single-layer TD3/SAC agents, however, are myopic with respect to week-scale inventory goals and may propose unsafe actions during exploration. Hierarchical goal-conditioned RL mitigates temporal abstraction: Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} develop GHTD3 for hydrogen-enhanced integrated energy systems (H-IES), coupling a semi-MDP high level with a goal-augmented low level. Multi-agent hierarchical options~\cite{jendoubi2023hier} and multi-timescale market bidding~\cite{ochoa2024bidding} similarly exploit temporal structure. Constrained and safe RL further emphasise feasible-set restriction during learning and execution~\cite{garcia2015safe,garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained,wachi2023safe}; baseline-policy residual exploration~\cite{garcia2012safe} is particularly relevant when a robust plant policy already exists. On the market side, price-taker storage models value exogenous prices without solving ISO clearing~\cite{emmanuel2020market,pei2024pricetaker,lin2019laes}. A practical caveat for goal-conditioned control on plant twins is numerical: when continuous actors map unbounded logits through saturating sigmoids on large-scale power observations, the Jacobian $\partial a/\partial g$ can collapse, so that hierarchical goals cease to affect executed actions---hierarchy then collapses to a single-layer controller.

Table~\ref{tab:lit} positions representative works. Existing hierarchical and constrained DRL studies rarely combine (i)~hybrid thermal--battery--CAES actions on a high-fidelity FMU twin, (ii)~market-conditioned hierarchical goals with an executable residual interface and pre-execution safety filtering, and (iii)~a shared weekly SoC-recovery protocol against a full baseline suite including rules, rolling LP, PSO, hybrid TD3 and SAC.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formalise weekly price-taker operation of a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a Modelica--FMU twin as a hybrid-action MDP with an explicit weekly net cash-flow objective and energy-SoC recovery gate under Shandong industrial/commercial TOU tariffs.
\item We propose Safe Market-GHTD3: a five-dimensional Modelica-aligned goal, hybrid-anchored \emph{action residual} execution $a=\mathrm{clip}(a_{\mathrm{H}}+\beta\tanh\Delta)$ with inverse-dynamics pretraining (so $g=0$ recovers hybrid TD3 and goals remain actionable), market/recovery priors, HER-mix relabeling and GiveSafe filtering.
\item We provide closed-loop three-season evidence in which Safe Market-GHTD3 improves episode reward over a strong hybrid TD3 baseline in all seasons while dominating rules, linprog, PSO and SAC, together with ablations and supporting protocol/cross-domain analyses.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:system} describes the plant and market setting. Section~\ref{sec:problem} formulates the optimisation and MDP. Section~\ref{sec:method} presents Safe Market-GHTD3. Section~\ref{sec:setup} details the case study. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports results and discussion. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Weekly economic objective}

Let $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ index hours in a weekly episode with $T=168$. Denote grid purchase/sale energies by $E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}$ and $E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$ (MWh), TOU buy/sell prices by $\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}}$ and $\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$, and device operating costs by $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ (thermal fuel, storage cycling, etc.). The incremental market cash flow and weekly net objective are
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t = \lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}} E_t^{\mathrm{sell}} - \lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}} - C_t^{\mathrm{op}},
\label{eq:dJ}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
J = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \Delta J_t.
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:J} is the primary economic KPI reported in closed-loop evaluation (sign convention: larger $J$ is better).

\subsection{Operational constraints}

Let $P_t^{\mathrm{th}},P_t^{\mathrm{bat}},P_t^{\mathrm{caes}},P_t^{\mathrm{w}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv}},P_t^{\mathrm{load}},P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}$ denote thermal, battery, CAES, wind, PV, load and grid powers (MW). The twin enforces power balance
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{th}}+P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}+P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}+P_t^{\mathrm{w}}+P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}+P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}=P_t^{\mathrm{load}},
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
together with device box and ramp limits
\begin{equation}
\underline{P}_{i}\le P_t^{i}\le \overline{P}_{i},\qquad
\bigl|P_t^{i}-P_{t-1}^{i}\bigr|\le \Delta_i^{\max},
\quad i\in\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{bat}\},
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
CAES mode exclusivity and minimum-run locks (encoded in the FMU mode machine), and inventory bounds
\begin{equation}
0\le s_{t,i}\le 1,\qquad i\in\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\}.
\label{eq:socbox}
\end{equation}
Actions that would violate~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:socbox} or mode locks lie outside the dynamic feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s_t)$ used by GiveSafe.

Weekly operation further imposes an energy-SoC recovery gate on primary energy inventories (battery and CAES gas; thermal tanks are secondary diagnostics only),
\begin{equation}
L_1^{\mathrm{e}}
= w_{\mathrm{bat}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{bat}}-s_{0,\mathrm{bat}}\bigr|
+ w_{\mathrm{gas}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{gas}}-s_{0,\mathrm{gas}}\bigr|
\le \varepsilon,
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
with $(w_{\mathrm{bat}},w_{\mathrm{gas}})=(1.5,1.0)$ and $\varepsilon=0.06$. Soft SoC shaping during the episode and a terminal bonus/penalty encourage~\eqref{eq:soc} without inventory dumping.

\subsection{Hybrid-action MDP}

We cast closed-loop control as an MDP $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma\rangle$ with discount $\gamma=0.99$. The state $s_t\in\mathcal{S}$ concatenates
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_t=\bigl[&
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}},s_{t,\mathrm{gas}},s_{t,\mathrm{hot}},s_{t,\mathrm{cold}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}},P_t^{\mathrm{bat}},P_t^{\mathrm{caes}},P_t^{\mathrm{grid}},
P_t^{\mathrm{w}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv}},P_t^{\mathrm{load}},\\
&\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\ \text{TOU band features},\ \text{optional forecast residuals}
\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
normalised for the actor--critic networks. The hybrid action is
\begin{equation}
a_t = \bigl(u_{\mathrm{tp},t},\, u_{\mathrm{bat},t},\, m_{\mathrm{caes},t},\, \mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\bigr)\in\mathcal{A},
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
where $u_{\mathrm{tp}},u_{\mathrm{bat}},\mu_{\mathrm{caes}}\in[-1,1]$ (scaled to physical limits) and $m_{\mathrm{caes}}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\}$. Transitions $P$ are induced by one FMU hour under GiveSafe-filtered actions. The scalar training reward is defined next; primary evaluation still reports physical $J$~\eqref{eq:J} and gate~\eqref{eq:soc}.

\subsection{Reward function}

Let $C_{\mathrm{ref}}>0$ be a calibration constant (seasonal rule-controller $p_{95}$ step cash-flow magnitude, $C_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx1.565\times10^{5}$~CNY). The economic step reward is
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{econ}} = \frac{\Delta J_t}{C_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
Potential-based SoC shaping uses the full weighted inventory L1 distance $L_{1,t}$ (battery, gas, hot, cold) relative to the episode start:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}
= \kappa_t \bigl(L_{1,t-1}-L_{1,t}\bigr)
- \kappa_t^{\mathrm{abs}} L_{1,t},
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
with base $\kappa=5$ and a small absolute penalty; in the last $H=40$~h both coefficients are linearly scaled up (recovery window) so that late inventory drift is strongly discouraged. At the final step of a completed, failure-free episode,
\begin{equation}
r_T^{\mathrm{term}}
=
\begin{cases}
B, & L_1^{\mathrm{e}}\le\varepsilon,\\
-p\,L_1^{\mathrm{e}}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:term}
\end{equation}
with $B=15$ and $p=30$. The extrinsic reward used by both hierarchical layers is
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{ext}}
= \mathrm{clip}\bigl(r_t^{\mathrm{econ}}+r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}+r_t^{\mathrm{term}},\, r_{\min},\, r_{\max}\bigr),
\label{eq:rext}
\end{equation}
with $(r_{\min},r_{\max})=(-25,25)$. Figure~\ref{fig:reward} summarises the stack.

\section{Proposed method: Safe Market-GHTD3}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Hierarchical goal-conditioned control}

Following goal-conditioned hierarchical TD3~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,fujimoto2018td3}, we split decision-making across timescales (Figs.~\ref{fig:algorithm}--\ref{fig:cstep}). Every $c$ steps, a high-level policy $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$ issues a storage goal $g_t$ (intended battery/CAES inventory progress). A low-level policy $\mu^{\mathrm{lo}}$ selects hybrid actions conditioned on $(s_t,g_t)$. Between high-level decisions, residual goals are updated by a transition map $h(s_i,g_i,s_{i+1})$ so that the low level tracks remaining intent. Twin critics, delayed actor updates and soft target networks stabilise both layers as in TD3~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}.

\subsection{Modelica-aligned goals, residual execution and hierarchical rewards}

The high-level action is a five-dimensional \emph{Modelica-aligned plant intent}
\begin{equation}
g=\bigl[g^{\Delta\mathrm{bat}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{gas}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{th}},\,g^{u_{\mathrm{tp}}},\,g^{\mathrm{arb}}\bigr]^{\top}
\in [g_{\min},g_{\max}],
\label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
with box $[-0.30,0.30]\times[-0.12,0.12]\times[-0.10,0.10]\times[-0.20,0.20]\times[0,1]$, packing battery and CAES-gas energy increments, a soft CAES thermal-process target (mean of hot/cold inventories), a thermal load-rate bias relative to a frozen hybrid baseline, and an arbitrage intensity. Raw high-level outputs are blended with a market-conditioned prior (charge in valleys, discharge in peaks; annealed weight $0.55\rightarrow0.12$) and a recovery prior over the last $H=40$~h (weight up to $0.95$). Historical goal relabeling uses HER-mix over achieved intents.

Intrinsic rewards track residual intent errors $e=s^{\mathrm{int}}+g-s^{\mathrm{int}\prime}$ (energy and soft thermal dimensions) and a thermal-bias term relative to the hybrid load rate~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{int}} = -\sqrt{\sum_i w_i e_{i,t}^{2}}+\alpha\, r_t^{\mathrm{ext}},
\qquad \alpha\in[0.20,0.45],
\label{eq:rint}
\end{equation}
while the low-/high-level training targets use
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{lo}} = r_t^{\mathrm{int}}, \qquad
R_k^{\mathrm{hi}} = \frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1} r_{t+j}^{\mathrm{ext}},
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with subgoal interval $c=8$ and high-level discount $\gamma^{c}$ (SMDP macro-action)~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}.

\paragraph{Hybrid-anchored action residual.}
Naive goal-conditioned absolute actors on raw plant observations can saturate sigmoid heads so that $\partial a/\partial g\approx0$. We therefore freeze a strong Hybrid TD3 policy $a_{\mathrm{H}}(s)$ (raw observations) and learn only a residual corrector $\Delta_{\theta}$ on normalised observations $s_{\mathrm{n}}$:
\begin{equation}
a = \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}\!\Big(
a_{\mathrm{H}}(s)
+\beta\odot\tanh\big(
\mathrm{clip}\big(\Delta_{\theta}(s_{\mathrm{n}},g)-\Delta_{\theta}(s_{\mathrm{n}},0),\,[-z_{\max},z_{\max}]\big)
\big)
\Big),
\label{eq:ares}
\end{equation}
with component-wise $\beta=(\beta_{\mathrm{tp}},\beta_{\mathrm{bat}},\beta_{\mathrm{mag}})$ (default $0.15/0.30/0.20$), $z_{\max}=8$, and CAES \emph{mode locked} to $a_{\mathrm{H}}$ by default (continuous magnitude residual only). Centring $\Delta(s,g)-\Delta(s,0)$ enforces $g=0\Rightarrow a=a_{\mathrm{H}}$ as a structural lower bound. Before TD3 fine-tuning, $\Delta_{\theta}$ is warm-started by inverse-dynamics / hindsight maximum-likelihood fitting of achieved intents from hybrid and rule trajectories.

\subsection{GiveSafe pre-execution filter}

Unsafe trial-and-error is unacceptable on the FMU twin. Before execution, GiveSafe queries a dynamic feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s_t)$ (oracle of~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:socbox} and mode locks) and replaces or rejects infeasible proposals~\cite{garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained}:
\begin{equation}
a_t^{\star} = \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s_t)}(\tilde a_t),
\label{eq:givesafe}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde a_t$ is the exploratory or greedy proposal. Only transitions generated by $a_t^{\star}$ enter the economic replay buffers.

\subsection{Training algorithm and hyperparameters}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:ghtd3} summarises Safe Market-GHTD3. Both layers use TD3 with twin critics, delayed policy updates and target smoothing~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}. Key hyperparameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Safe Market-GHTD3 (sketch)}
\label{alg:ghtd3}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Initialise high/low actors--critics, targets, buffers $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}},\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$
\For{episode $=1,2,\ldots$}
  \State Reset FMU; $s\leftarrow s_0$; clear residual goal
  \For{$t=0$ to $T-1$}
    \If{$t \bmod c = 0$}
      \State Sample/issue $g$ from $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$; blend market \& recovery priors
    \EndIf
    \State $a_{\mathrm{H}}\leftarrow\mu_{\mathrm{H}}(s)$; $\tilde a \leftarrow a_{\mathrm{H}}+\mathrm{residual}_{\theta}(s,g)+\mathrm{noise}$; $a\leftarrow \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}(\tilde a)$ \Comment{GiveSafe}
    \State Step FMU with $a$; observe $s'$, $\Delta J$, compute $r^{\mathrm{ext}},r^{\mathrm{int}}$ by~\eqref{eq:rext}--\eqref{eq:reward}
    \State Store low-level transition in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; update residual goal $g\leftarrow h(s,g,s')$
    \If{$t \bmod c = c-1$} store high-level transition in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$ (optional relabel)
    \EndIf
    \State TD3 updates on $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$ each step; on $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$ every $c$ steps with $\gamma^{c}$ and HER-mix goal relabel
    \State $s\leftarrow s'$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Case study setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Scenarios, baselines and metrics}

Three representative weeks (winter, summer, transition) are evaluated on the FMU twin under identical TOU and resource tables. Baselines share the same environment interface: original conservative plant rule (B0; high thermal, storage largely idle), price-aware heuristic rule (B1), rolling linear programme / linprog MPC, parametric particle swarm optimisation (PSO), hybrid single-layer GiveSafe-TD3, and soft actor--critic (SAC) trained to about $8\times10^{4}$ valid steps. Hierarchical and hybrid TD3 agents use GiveSafe during interaction. Primary metrics are weekly net cash flow $J$~\eqref{eq:J}, episode reward, energy-SoC gate~\eqref{eq:soc}, thermal energy and storage throughput.

\subsection{Implementation notes}

Policies are trained with off-policy TD3/SAC updates on FMU rollouts. Unless stated otherwise, primary ranking uses weekly episodes with SoC re-initialisation (weekly-reset protocol), consistent with terminal recovery and hierarchical multi-energy case studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. A continuous-year protocol (single FMU reset) and a Mengxi AI4E domain without the plant FMU are reported as supporting analyses. Figure~\ref{fig:training} reports training curves under \emph{matched logging protocols}: TD3-family agents dump a scalar every 500 valid steps from step 500 onward, whereas the SAC-80k log retained in the repository covers only the final resume window (dense steps near $6.45\times10^{4}$--$6.5\times10^{4}$). Panel~(b) therefore must not be read as a 0--80k absolute-time alignment with panel~(a).

\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Three-season economic performance}

Safe Market-GHTD3 and hybrid TD3 dominate weekly economics across seasons, while rule-level thermal-idle operation is far from TOU-optimal (Fig.~\ref{fig:j}, Table~\ref{tab:main}). Relative to B0, both RL families improve weekly cash-flow metrics by on the order of $10^{7}$~CNY in representative weeks (Fig.~\ref{fig:dj}).

\paragraph{Hierarchy exceeds Hybrid via expandable teacher-anchored residual (TEA).}
Hybrid is a strong single-layer GiveSafe-TD3 agent trained on the same FMU, reward~\eqref{eq:rext} and SoC gate~\eqref{eq:soc}. With teacher-anchored \emph{expandable} residuals~\eqref{eq:ares} (curriculum on continuous $\beta$, teacher BC annealing, CAES mode/magnitude locked to the hybrid teacher for throughput stability), Safe Market-GHTD3 improves closed-loop episode reward in \emph{all three} seasons: winter $128.10\rightarrow130.24$ ($\Delta{+}2.14$), transition $110.37\rightarrow112.35$ ($\Delta{+}1.98$), summer $91.70\rightarrow94.17$ ($\Delta{+}2.47$), with SoC gates satisfied throughout (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Mean $\Delta\approx{+}2.2$ is substantially larger than a fixed small-residual interface, while CAES weekly throughput stays near the hybrid baseline (no thrashing). Operational signatures show increased battery cycling under hierarchical intents; GiveSafe preserves feasibility; market-prior ablations still show large early-learning gaps (Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}).

SAC recovers SoC but stays close to rule economics under the same weekly horizon. Rolling linprog is feasible but lags RL on $J$ under hybrid non-convex CAES modes. PSO can improve summer/transition cash flow over B0 yet remains well below RL.

\subsection{Ablation of hierarchical market components}

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} reports matched-budget ablations at $1.2\times10^{4}$ valid steps. Removing the market goal prior yields the largest reward degradation (about $62\rightarrow40$), confirming that TOU-informed high-level goals are essential for early economic learning. Removing the recovery goal harms both reward and terminal SoC shaping. Setting $c=1$ (high-level update every step) can raise short-horizon reward under the ablation budget but changes the intended SMDP abstraction; the main experiments therefore retain $c>1$ with longer hierarchical training. Hierarchical BC pretraining trades early reward for more stable long-horizon schedules in the full training regime.

With resource channels fixed, replacing perfect TOU observations by residual price forecasts (settlement still uses realised prices) shifts GHTD3 weekly $J$ by about 7--8\% in winter/summer (Fig.~\ref{fig:info}). \subsection{Supporting analyses: continuous year and cross-domain tests}

When the FMU is reset only once and SoC carries across weeks, the original rule completes 8760~h with year-end energy SoC satisfied, whereas weekly-trained Hybrid, GHTD3 and SAC abort near hour~1276 (Fig.~\ref{fig:cont}). Continuous inventory carry is therefore a harder deployment regime than weekly recovery and should not be conflated with the primary weekly protocol used for hierarchical training and ranking~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. \subsection{Discussion}

Relative to Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, the present work targets a thermal--battery--CAES Modelica--FMU twin under Shandong TOU price-taker settlement, adds GiveSafe execution filtering, and---critically---replaces naive absolute goal-conditioned actuators by the hybrid-anchored residual~\eqref{eq:ares} so that hierarchical intents remain causal. Empirically, Safe Market-GHTD3 improves episode reward over a strong hybrid TD3 baseline in all three seasons while keeping CAES modes hybrid-locked for throughput stability. The residual trust-region ($\beta$-bounded continuous corrections) explains why gains are consistent yet modest; larger improvements would require controlled expansion of the residual set (TOU-aligned $c$, price-gated $\beta$, or curriculum mode unlock). Price-taker assumptions ignore storage feedback on clearing prices~\cite{emmanuel2020market}; FMU resource tables represent a temperate typical year rather than co-located Shandong meteorology; tariffs are volumetric TOU rather than nodal LMPs. Ablations use a shorter budget than the main agents; multi-seed confidence intervals are left to future work.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

This paper presented Safe Market-GHTD3 for weekly scheduling of a thermal--battery--CAES plant on a Modelica--FMU twin under Shandong TOU tariffs. By combining Modelica-aligned market/recovery goals, hybrid-anchored action residual TD3, inverse-dynamics warm-start and GiveSafe filtering, the method achieves large closed-loop gains over original plant rules and classical baselines, \emph{improves} a strong hybrid TD3 baseline in all three seasons, and satisfies weekly energy-SoC recovery. Seasonal dispatch and SoC analyses explain the mechanism; ablations highlight market priors; continuous-year and cross-domain tests clarify protocol choice and paradigm transfer. Future work includes multi-seed testing, TOU-aligned subgoal intervals, price-gated residual scales, continuous-SoC curricula and plant-level renewable scenarios aligned with the tariff region.
