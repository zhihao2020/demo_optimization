\shorttitle{HMSD for multi-energy plant scheduling}
\title[mode=title]{Hierarchical market-safe dispatch for hybrid multi-energy plant scheduling under time-of-use tariffs and carbon cost}

\begin{abstract}
Provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs and China's national carbon market jointly couple peak--valley arbitrage with abatement incentives at industrial multi-energy plants. Optimal coordination of thermal generation, battery storage and compressed-air energy storage (CAES) is a hybrid-action, multi-timescale decision problem subject to ramp limits, inventory bounds and mode locks. This paper formulates a weekly price-taker \emph{comprehensive monetary} objective for a thermal--battery--CAES plant under Shandong industrial/commercial TOU prices: maximise net cash flow net of China National ETS-priced carbon cost, curtailment and unserved-energy penalties, and battery cycle degradation, subject to an energy state-of-charge (SoC) recovery gate. The plant is modelled in MWORKS Sysplorer (Modelica) from first-principles device and network equations, exported as an FMI-compliant functional mock-up unit (FMU), and operated in closed loop with a one-hour communication step. Because week-scale inventory and carbon exposure coexist with hour-scale hybrid actuators, we specialise goal-conditioned hierarchical twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) as \emph{Hierarchical Market-Safe Dispatch} (HMSD): a high-level policy issues market- and recovery-aware intents every $c$ steps; a low-level goal-conditioned policy maps state--goal pairs to hybrid actions; market-structure goal priors (MSGP), market-structured hindsight experience replay (MS-HER) and feasibility-filtered maximum-likelihood pretraining (F-MLE) stabilise learning; executable actions are projected onto the dynamic feasible set before plant interaction. Multi-seed evaluations on three representative weeks show consistent mean reward gains over single-layer TD3 under a matched budget, with improved SoC recovery and structured peak--valley coordination relative to rule-based, linprog and parametric particle-swarm baselines.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Comprehensive weekly objective coupling TOU cash, ETS carbon, curtailment/unserved energy and battery degradation under an energy-SoC recovery gate.
\item Physics-based plant model built in MWORKS Sysplorer and exported as an FMI FMU for closed-loop hierarchical dispatch.
\item HMSD: multi-timescale hierarchical TD3 with market-aware goals, hindsight relabelling, feasible-set projection and multi-seed closed-loop evidence versus TD3 and classical baselines.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =====================================================================

Deep decarbonisation and China's national carbon market couple electricity markets with abatement costs at multi-energy plants~\cite{zhou2023iesdrl,yang2024planning,icap2025chinaets}. Provincial time-of-use (TOU) tariffs create peak--valley incentives~\cite{ndrc2021tou,shandong2023tou}, while an emissions trading scheme (ETS) attaches a monetary charge to thermal output and grid imports. This paper studies \emph{plant-level price-taker operation} of a thermal--battery--CAES facility: maximise a \emph{comprehensive monetary objective}---weekly net cash flow net of ETS-priced carbon, curtailment/unserved penalties and battery cycle cost---subject to energy SoC recovery, rather than ISO market clearing~\cite{emmanuel2020market,pei2024pricetaker}. Hybrid continuous--discrete actions, CAES mode locks and thermal ramps render unconstrained plant interaction operationally unsafe and computationally demanding.

Model-based programmes remain useful baselines but typically require convexification of CAES thermodynamics or face restrictive budgets on hybrid spaces~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL)~\cite{sutton2018rl,fujimoto2018td3,haarnoja2018sac} learns closed-loop policies by interaction. Hierarchical goal-conditioned control is a natural fit when week-scale inventory recovery and carbon exposure coexist with hour-scale hybrid actuators. Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} develop GHTD3 for hydrogen-enhanced integrated energy systems under comprehensive cost; we retain that hierarchical TD3 skeleton and specialise it as \emph{Hierarchical Market-Safe Dispatch} (HMSD) for a thermal--CAES plant under Shandong TOU settlement and a National ETS carbon price, with projection onto a dynamic feasible set before each plant step~\cite{garcia2015safe,sun2024constrained}.

Three gaps remain. (1)~\textbf{Multi-timescale comprehensive objectives on physics-based twins.} Single-layer agents are myopic with respect to week-scale inventory and carbon; hydrogen-centric studies do not address thermal--CAES price-taker cash plus ETS-priced carbon on a high-fidelity plant model. (2)~\textbf{Contracted hybrid feasible sets.} Mode locks and ramps require safety filtering so that learning remains consistent with executable decisions. (3)~\textbf{Effective hierarchical goals under large-scale plant observations.} Goal channels must remain causally informative so that hierarchical intents do not collapse to single-layer control.

The main contributions are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Market--inventory--carbon problem on a physics-based twin.} We formalise weekly price-taker maximisation of $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$ (TOU cash minus ETS-priced CO$_2$, curtailment/unserved costs $C^{CUT}$ and battery cycle cost $C^{\mathrm{deg}}$) for a thermal--battery--CAES plant with an energy-SoC gate and dynamic feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s)$. The plant is modelled in MWORKS Sysplorer and exported as an FMI FMU for closed-loop evaluation. Relative to hydrogen-centric comprehensive-cost H-IES~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, the physics, hybrid CAES actions and cash+ETS interface differ.
\item \textbf{Hierarchical market-safe dispatch (HMSD).} High-level intents live in a market--inventory goal space (MSGP); high-level credit assignment uses MS-HER; F-MLE warm-starts policies on feasible demonstrations; observation normalisation preserves causal goal pathways; actions are projected onto $\mathcal{F}(s)$ before plant interaction. Hierarchy matches week-scale carbon/inventory structure with hour-scale hybrid control.
\item \textbf{Closed-loop evidence.} Multi-seed three-season evaluations demonstrate hierarchical gains over single-layer TD3 under a matched budget, with dispatch, SoC and cost-component comparisons against rules, linprog and particle swarm optimisation (PSO).
\end{enumerate}

\section{System description}

\subsection{Plant topology and modelling workflow}

Figure~\ref{fig:topology} summarises the multi-energy plant. The supply layer comprises wind, photovoltaic (PV) generation and a grid interface under TOU buy/sell prices. The conversion layer centres on a thermal unit commanded by a continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp}}$ and a CAES train commanded by discrete mode and continuous magnitude. The storage layer comprises a battery with continuous power command $u_{\mathrm{bat}}$ and CAES gas and thermal inventories (gas tank, hot and cold tanks). The demand layer balances electric load and grid exchange.

Plant dynamics follow first-principles multi-physics: thermal ramping and fuel use; electrochemical battery SoC with round-trip efficiency; multi-tank CAES gas storage with thermo-mechanical coupling and mode locks; renewable availability; and nodal power balance with curtailment and unserved residual. The complete Modelica model is constructed and parameterised in \textbf{MWORKS Sysplorer}, then exported as an FMI-compliant FMU. Closed-loop optimisers and learning agents interact with this FMU at a communication interval of one hour, reading plant states and writing hybrid set-points. Exogenous TOU prices and resource profiles provide market and boundary conditions without replacing the internal differential--algebraic dynamics.

\section{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

This section formulates the multi-energy plant physics, operating constraints and market objective that define the weekly scheduling problem. Continuous-time differential--algebraic equations of the plant (thermal unit, battery, multi-tank CAES, renewables and grid interface) are integrated in the Sysplorer-exported FMU with communication interval $\tau_s=1$~h; the relations below are the dispatch-level models realised by that twin and used by optimisers and learning agents. We adopt the following sign convention throughout: renewable and thermal generation are non-negative when injecting power; battery and CAES charge is positive and discharge is negative; grid import is positive. Component sets are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
I_1&=\{\mathrm{w},\mathrm{pv}\},\quad
I_2=\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{caes}\},\quad
I_3=\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},\\
I&=I_1\cup I_2\cup I_3.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sets}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Energy generation units}

\subsubsection{Photovoltaics}

PV available power depends on plane-of-array irradiance $G_t$, module surface temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}$ and nameplate capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}=260$~MW. With standard irradiance $G_{\mathrm{stc}}=1000$~W/m$^2$, standard temperature $T_{\mathrm{stc}}=298.15$~K, temperature coefficient $K_T=0.005$~K$^{-1}$ and conversion efficiency $\eta^{\mathrm{pv}}=0.95$,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}
=
\max\Bigl(
P_n^{\mathrm{pv}}
\frac{G_t}{G_{\mathrm{stc}}}
\bigl[1-K_T\bigl(T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}-T_{\mathrm{stc}}\bigr)\bigr]
\eta^{\mathrm{pv}},\,0\Bigr).
\label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
Module temperature is obtained from ambient temperature $T_t^{\mathrm{air}}$, wind speed $v_t$ and irradiance by the twin correlation
\begin{equation}
T_t^{\mathrm{pv}}
=
T_t^{\mathrm{air}}
+
0.0138\bigl(1+0.031(T_t^{\mathrm{air}}-273.15)\bigr)
\bigl(1-0.042\,v_t\bigr)G_t.
\label{eq:pv-temp}
\end{equation}
Actual injected PV power after bus curtailment satisfies $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$.

\subsubsection{Wind turbines}

Wind available power follows a cubic power curve with cut-in, rated and cut-out speeds $v_{\mathrm{ci}}=3$~m/s, $v_{\mathrm{r}}=10$~m/s and $v_{\mathrm{co}}=15$~m/s on capacity $P_n^{\mathrm{w}}=300$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}
=
\begin{cases}
0, & 0\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{ci}}\ \text{or}\ v_t\ge v_{\mathrm{co}},\\[2pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}\,\dfrac{v_t^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}}{v_{\mathrm{r}}^{3}-v_{\mathrm{ci}}^{3}},
& v_{\mathrm{ci}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{r}},\\[6pt]
P_n^{\mathrm{w}}, & v_{\mathrm{r}}\le v_t < v_{\mathrm{co}}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:wt}
\end{equation}
Turbine pitch is not a free decision variable. Actual wind injection obeys $0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}}$.

\subsection{Energy conversion units}

\subsubsection{Thermal generation}

Thermal electric power is commanded by the continuous load rate $u_{\mathrm{tp},t}$:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{th}} = u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}},
\qquad
u_{\min}\le u_{\mathrm{tp},t}\le u_{\max},
\label{eq:th}
\end{equation}
with $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=150$~MW, $P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}=50$~MW, $u_{\min}=P_{\min}^{\mathrm{th}}/P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}=1/3$ and $u_{\max}=1$ (no shut-down). Inter-hour ramping is enforced on the actual thermal power,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|P_t^{\mathrm{th}}-P_{t-1}^{\mathrm{th}}\bigr|
\le
\Delta_{\mathrm{th}}^{\max}
=
r_{\max}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{th}}\,\tau_s,
\label{eq:th-ramp}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\max}=0.0025/60$~s$^{-1}$ is the Modelica load-rate limit (equivalently $0.15$~p.u.\ per hour). Fuel and O\&M costs enter $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ in~\eqref{eq:dJ}.

\subsubsection{CAES conversion}

CAES is a hybrid conversion--storage plant. The hybrid command comprises a discrete mode and continuous magnitude
\begin{equation}
m_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\},\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\in[0,1],
\label{eq:caes-cmd}
\end{equation}
mapped to a signed electric power command on capacity $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}=150$~MW,
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
=
\begin{cases}
+\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{charge},\\
0, & m=\mathrm{idle},\\
-\mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\,P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}, & m=\mathrm{discharge},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:caes-p}
\end{equation}
or equivalently $P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}=u_{\mathrm{caes},t}P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{caes}}$ with the non-convex legal set
\begin{equation}
u_{\mathrm{caes},t}
\in
[-1,-0.33]\cup\{0\}\cup[0.86,1]
\label{eq:caes-legal}
\end{equation}
encoding minimum compressor/expander loading. Mode locks and minimum-run dwell constraints further restrict admissible transitions of $m_{\mathrm{caes}}$. Electric power is realised as planned ($P^{\mathrm{act}}=P^{\mathrm{plan}}$); mass-flow set-points of air and thermal fluid scale with $|P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}|$ and reverse direction between compression and expansion.

\subsection{Energy storage units}

\subsubsection{Battery}

Battery power is commanded by $u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\in[-1,1]$ on $P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}=100$~MW:
\begin{equation}
P_t^{\mathrm{bat}} = u_{\mathrm{bat},t}\, P_{\mathrm{cap}}^{\mathrm{bat}}.
\label{eq:bat-p}
\end{equation}
With energy capacity $E_{\mathrm{cap}}=500$~MWh and discharge efficiency $\eta=0.85$ (charge path lossless as in the twin), the SoC dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{\mathrm{bat}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=
\begin{cases}
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/E_{\mathrm{cap}}, & P^{\mathrm{bat}}\ge 0,\\[2pt]
P^{\mathrm{bat}}/(E_{\mathrm{cap}}\,\eta), & P^{\mathrm{bat}}<0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bat-soc}
\end{equation}
or, in hourly form with $[x]_+=\max(x,0)$ and $[x]_-=\max(-x,0)$,
\begin{equation}
s_{t+1,\mathrm{bat}}
=
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}}
+
\tau_s\frac{[P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_+ - [P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}]_-/\eta}{E_{\mathrm{cap}}}.
\label{eq:bat-soc-d}
\end{equation}
Inventory bounds are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}\in[0.1,0.9]$.

\subsubsection{CAES inventories}

CAES energy is stored as compressed air and two thermal fluid tanks. Gas SoC is pressure-based with rating $p_{\mathrm{norm}}=100$~bar,
\begin{equation}
s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}
=
\frac{p_t}{p_{\mathrm{norm}}},
\qquad
\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}}\le s_{t,\mathrm{gas}}\le \overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},
\label{eq:gas-soc}
\end{equation}
with $(\underline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}},\overline{s}_{\mathrm{gas}})=(0.6,1.0)$. Fixed-volume gas-tank mass and energy balances (ideal-air medium) evolve pressure $p$ and specific enthalpy $h$ under port mass flows $\dot m_{\mathrm{a}},\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}$ and wall heat $q_{\mathrm{w}}$,
\begin{align}
V\bigl(\partial_p\rho\,\dot p+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b}},
\label{eq:gas-mass}\\
V\bigl(-\dot p+\partial_p\rho\,\dot p\,h+\partial_h\rho\,\dot h\,h+\rho\dot h\bigr)
&=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a}}h_{\mathrm{a}}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b}}h_{\mathrm{b}}^{\mathrm{out}}
+
q_{\mathrm{w}},
\label{eq:gas-energy}
\end{align}
with $q_{\mathrm{w}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{w}}S(T_{\mathrm{w}}-T)$ and gas temperature $T=T(p,h)$ (clipped at $253$~K). Hot and cold tanks store water at fixed pressure with free surface area $A$ and total volume $V_0=1.5\times10^{4}$~m$^3$. Level-based inventories
\begin{equation}
s_{t,i}
=
\frac{\ell_{t,i}}{V_0/A},
\qquad
i\in\{\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:thermal-soc}
\end{equation}
obey mass and enthalpy balances when within $(\underline{s}_i,\overline{s}_i)=(0.05,0.95)$,
\begin{equation}
\dot m_i=\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}+\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i},\qquad
m_i\dot h_i
=
\dot m_{\mathrm{a},i}h_{\mathrm{a},i}^{\mathrm{in}}
+
\dot m_{\mathrm{b},i}h_{\mathrm{b},i}^{\mathrm{out}}+Q_{\mathrm{gen},i}.
\label{eq:tank-bal}
\end{equation}
Compression ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}>0$) charges the gas inventory and drives the thermal circuit one way; expansion ($P^{\mathrm{caes}}<0$) reverses the flows. Primary weekly recovery inventories are $s_{\mathrm{bat}}$ and $s_{\mathrm{gas}}$; hot/cold SoCs and temperatures $(T^{\mathrm{hot}},T^{\mathrm{cold}})$ are process states used in hierarchical goals and shaping. All inventories obey~\eqref{eq:socbox}.

\subsection{System operation constraints}

The electric bus closes the power balance with curtailment and unserved load. Let $P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}}$ be renewable availability and $P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}$ the scheduled demand. Residual power after dispatchable assets is absorbed first by curtailing wind, then PV; residual deficit is met by demand response down to $20\%$ of plan, with remainder counted as unserved:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}}+P_t^{\mathrm{bat}}+P_t^{\mathrm{caes}}
+P_t^{\mathrm{w}}+P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}+P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}
=
P_t^{\mathrm{load}},\\
&0\le P_t^{\mathrm{w}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{w,avail}},\quad
0\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{pv,avail}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}\ge 0,\quad
P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}\ge 0,\quad
0.2\,P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load}}\le P_t^{\mathrm{load,plan}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
Here $P_t^{\mathrm{curt}}$ is residual surplus after wind-then-PV curtailment and $P_t^{\mathrm{uns}}$ is residual shortfall after load reduction. Box and ramp limits, inventory bounds and grid exchange limits are
\begin{equation}
\underline{P}_{i}\le P_t^{i}\le \overline{P}_{i},\qquad
\bigl|P_t^{i}-P_{t-1}^{i}\bigr|\le \Delta_i^{\max},
\quad i\in\{\mathrm{th},\mathrm{bat}\},
\label{eq:box}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\underline{s}_{i}\le s_{t,i}\le \overline{s}_{i},\qquad i\in\{\mathrm{bat},\mathrm{gas},\mathrm{hot},\mathrm{cold}\},
\label{eq:socbox}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min}\le P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}\le P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max},
\label{eq:grid}
\end{equation}
with $(P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\min},P_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\max})=(-500,500)$~MW. Actions that would violate~\eqref{eq:balance}--\eqref{eq:grid},~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} or CAES mode locks lie outside the dynamic feasible set $\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

Weekly operation further imposes an energy-SoC recovery gate on primary inventories,
\begin{equation}
L_1^{\mathrm{e}}
= w_{\mathrm{bat}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{bat}}-s_{0,\mathrm{bat}}\bigr|
+ w_{\mathrm{gas}}\bigl|s_{T,\mathrm{gas}}-s_{0,\mathrm{gas}}\bigr|
\le \varepsilon,
\label{eq:soc}
\end{equation}
with $(w_{\mathrm{bat}},w_{\mathrm{gas}})=(1.5,1.0)$ and $\varepsilon=0.06$.

\subsection{Optimization objectives}

Let $t=0,\ldots,T-1$ index hours in a weekly episode ($T=168$). Grid purchase/sale energies are $E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_+$ and $E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}=\tau_s[P_t^{\mathrm{grid}}]_-$ (MWh). Device operating costs $C_t^{\mathrm{op}}$ aggregate thermal fuel/O\&M and storage energy-bookkeeping cash increments exported by the twin (battery cycle life is not in Modelica income; see $C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}$). Under price-taker TOU prices $\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}}$, the hourly \emph{cash} increment is
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{cash}}
=
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{sell}} E_t^{\mathrm{sell}}
-
\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{op}}.
\label{eq:dJ}
\end{equation}
Step CO$_2$ mass uses emission factors $\eta_{\mathrm{th}},\eta_{\mathrm{grid}}$ (tCO$_2$/MWh) on thermal energy and grid imports,
\begin{equation}
\Delta m_t
=
\eta_{\mathrm{th}} E_t^{\mathrm{th}}
+
\eta_{\mathrm{grid}} E_t^{\mathrm{buy}},
\label{eq:co2}
\end{equation}
with carbon priced at a China National ETS operating point $\pi_{\mathrm{CO_2}}=80$~CNY/tCO$_2$e (secondary-market band spanning the 2025 ICAP average and 2024 year-end closes)~\cite{icap2025chinaets}. Carbon is treated as an external ETS-style charge rather than a second fuel bill. Curtailment and unserved energies $E_t^{\mathrm{curt}},E_t^{\mathrm{uns}}$ follow bus residuals and enter a Cui-style spill cost~\cite{cui2025ghtd3},
\begin{equation}
C_t^{CUT}
=
\nu_{\mathrm{curt}} E_t^{\mathrm{curt}}
+
\nu_{\mathrm{uns}} E_t^{\mathrm{uns}},
\label{eq:cut}
\end{equation}
with operating points $\nu_{\mathrm{curt}}=300$~CNY/MWh and $\nu_{\mathrm{uns}}=1000$~CNY/MWh. Battery \emph{cycle} cost is accounted outside pure energy bookkeeping: device-level battery income in the plant model reflects energy transfer only, whereas capital wear is represented by a convex cumulative-discharge term $\psi(\delta)=a_0\delta^{p}$ with $p\approx 2.03$~\cite{cui2025ghtd3,cui2024caesbess}. We calibrate $a_0$ so that lifetime discharge exhausts capital cost (LCOS-style amortisation~\cite{xu2022lcos}). Let $\delta_t$ be episode cumulative \emph{discharge} energy (MWh; charge does not increase $\delta$) and $\delta_0=\rho E_{\mathrm{life}}$ a mid-life offset ($\rho=0.25$) so that weekly episodes are not stuck near $\delta=0$, where the marginal cost $a_0 p\delta^{p-1}$ vanishes. With lifetime discharge $E_{\mathrm{life}}=N_{\mathrm{cycle}}\mathrm{DoD}_{\mathrm{eq}}E_{\mathrm{cap}}$ and total capital $\mathrm{Capex}=c_{\mathrm{kWh}}E_{\mathrm{cap}}$,
\begin{equation}
\psi(\delta)=a_0\,\delta^{p},\quad
a_0=\frac{\mathrm{Capex}}{E_{\mathrm{life}}^{p}},\quad
p=2.03,\quad
C_t^{\mathrm{deg}}
=
\psi(\delta_0+\delta_t)-\psi(\delta_0+\delta_{t-1}),
\label{eq:deg}
\end{equation}
hence $\psi(E_{\mathrm{life}})=\mathrm{Capex}$ and degradation becomes strictly more expensive as cumulative discharge grows. Default plant-scale numbers match the battery capacity ($E_{\mathrm{cap}}=400$~MWh) with a stationary LFP-class economic point $c_{\mathrm{kWh}}=1000$~CNY/kWh, $N_{\mathrm{cycle}}=5000$ and $\mathrm{DoD}_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.8$~\cite{xu2022lcos}. The comprehensive monetary objective is
\begin{equation}
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}
=
\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{cash}}
-
\pi_{\mathrm{CO_2}}\,\Delta m_t
-
C_t^{CUT}
-
C_t^{\mathrm{deg}},
\qquad
\max_{\{a_t\}}\; J^{\mathrm{gen}} = \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\eqref{eq:pv}\text{--}\eqref{eq:soc},\
a_t\in\mathcal{F}(s_t).
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
Hour-scale hybrid actions thus couple to week-scale inventory recovery, carbon exposure, renewable spill and storage wear---the multi-timescale structure hierarchical RL targets. Policies are ranked by calibrated episode reward under~\eqref{eq:J} and gate~\eqref{eq:soc} (Section~\ref{sec:reward}).

\subsection{Hybrid-action MDP}

Closed-loop control is cast as an MDP $\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r,\gamma\rangle$ with $\gamma=0.99$. The state concatenates inventories, thermo states, powers, prices and TOU features,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_t=\bigl[&
s_{t,\mathrm{bat}},s_{t,\mathrm{gas}},s_{t,\mathrm{hot}},s_{t,\mathrm{cold}},
p_t,T_t^{\mathrm{gas}},T_t^{\mathrm{hot}},T_t^{\mathrm{cold}},\\
&P_t^{\mathrm{th}},P_t^{\mathrm{bat}},P_t^{\mathrm{caes}},P_t^{\mathrm{grid}},
P_t^{\mathrm{w}},P_t^{\mathrm{pv}},P_t^{\mathrm{load}},
P_t^{\mathrm{curt}},P_t^{\mathrm{uns}},\\
&\lambda_t^{\mathrm{buy}},\ \text{TOU band features},\ \text{optional forecast residuals}
\bigr],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
and the hybrid action is
\begin{equation}
a_t = \bigl(u_{\mathrm{tp},t},\, u_{\mathrm{bat},t},\, m_{\mathrm{caes},t},\, \mu_{\mathrm{caes},t}\bigr)\in\mathcal{A},
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with continuous channels scaled to physical limits and $m_{\mathrm{caes}}\in\{\mathrm{charge},\mathrm{idle},\mathrm{discharge}\}$. Transitions are induced by one plant hour that integrates~\eqref{eq:pv}--\eqref{eq:tank-bal} under feasible actions $a_t\in\mathcal{F}(s_t)$.

\subsection{Reward function for learning}
\label{sec:reward}

With calibration constant $C_{\mathrm{ref}}\approx1.565\times10^{5}$~CNY (rule-controller $p_{95}$ step cash magnitude),
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{econ}} = \frac{\Delta J_t^{\mathrm{gen}}}{C_{\mathrm{ref}}}.
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
Potential-based inventory shaping uses the weighted L1 distance $L_{1,t}$ of (battery, gas, hot, cold) relative to episode start:
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}
= \kappa_t \bigl(L_{1,t-1}-L_{1,t}\bigr)
- \kappa_t^{\mathrm{abs}} L_{1,t},
\label{eq:shape}
\end{equation}
with base $\kappa=5$ and a recovery-window scale-up over the last $H=40$~h. At a completed, failure-free terminal step,
\begin{equation}
r_T^{\mathrm{term}}
=
\begin{cases}
B, & L_1^{\mathrm{e}}\le\varepsilon,\\
-p\,L_1^{\mathrm{e}}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:term}
\end{equation}
with $B=15$ and $p=30$. The extrinsic reward is the sum of the three terms
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{ext}}
= r_t^{\mathrm{econ}}+r_t^{\mathrm{shape}}+r_t^{\mathrm{term}}.
\label{eq:rext}
\end{equation}
Classical programmes must convexify~\eqref{eq:caes-legal} and the CAES thermo-mechanical coupling; HMSD (Section~\ref{sec:method}) optimises~\eqref{eq:J} by closed-loop interaction with the plant twin.

\section{Solution methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

The weekly comprehensive-cost task is cast as two augmented MDPs. Slow variables (TOU-band intent, inventory recovery and carbon exposure under~\eqref{eq:J}) are assigned to a high-level semi-MDP; fast hybrid actions under $\mathcal{F}(s)$ are assigned to a goal-conditioned low-level MDP. We adopt the hierarchical TD3 backbone of Cui et al.~\cite{cui2025ghtd3} and specialise it as HMSD for market-aware, safety-filtered hybrid dispatch on the Sysplorer-exported FMU. Figure~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarises the agent--environment architecture and policy-learning procedure.

\subsection{Two augmented MDPs}

A standard one-step MDP is insufficient for week-scale inventory planning. Following~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, we use a high-level semi-MDP (SMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{G},P_c,R_c,\gamma^c\rangle$ that issues goals every $c$ steps, and a low-level goal-augmented MDP (GAMDP) $\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G},P,R,\gamma\rangle$ that executes hybrid actions conditioned on the residual goal. The high-level action is the goal
\begin{equation}
g=\bigl[g^{\Delta\mathrm{bat}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{gas}},\,g^{\Delta\mathrm{th}},\,g^{u_{\mathrm{tp}}},\,g^{\mathrm{arb}}\bigr]^{\top}
\in [g_{\min},g_{\max}],
\label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
with box $[-0.30,0.30]\times[-0.12,0.12]\times[-0.10,0.10]\times[-0.20,0.20]\times[0,1]$. Low-level actions remain~\eqref{eq:action}.

\subsection{Hierarchy of decision making}

Every $c$ steps, the high-level policy $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$ observes $s_t$ and issues $g_t$. For $i\in[t,t+c-1]$, the low-level policy $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ maps $(s_i,g_i)$ to a hybrid action, and residual goals are updated by
\begin{equation}
g_{i+1}=h(s_i,g_i,s_{i+1})
= s_i^{\mathrm{int}}+g_i-s_{i+1}^{\mathrm{int}},
\label{eq:h}
\end{equation}
where $s^{\mathrm{int}}$ denotes the inventory/intent subvector tracked by $g$. Intrinsic and layered training rewards follow~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{int}}
= -\Bigl(\sum_i w_i e_{i,t}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}+\alpha\, r_t^{\mathrm{ext}},
\qquad
e_t=s_t^{\mathrm{int}}+g_t-s_{t+1}^{\mathrm{int}},
\label{eq:rint}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
r_t^{\mathrm{lo}} = r_t^{\mathrm{int}}, \qquad
R_k^{\mathrm{hi}} = \frac{1}{c}\sum_{j=0}^{c-1} r_{t+j}^{\mathrm{ext}},
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
with default $c=8$ and $\alpha\in[0.20,0.50]$. High-level bootstrapping uses discount $\gamma^{c}$.

Market-structure goal priors (MSGP) blend the raw high-level output with a TOU prior $g^{\mathrm{mkt}}$ (charge in valleys, discharge in peaks) and a recovery prior $g^{\mathrm{rec}}$ over the last $H=40$--$48$~h:
\begin{equation}
g \leftarrow (1-w_{\mathrm{m}}-w_{\mathrm{r}})\,\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}(s)
+ w_{\mathrm{m}}\,g^{\mathrm{mkt}}
+ w_{\mathrm{r}}\,g^{\mathrm{rec}},
\label{eq:msgp}
\end{equation}
with annealed $w_{\mathrm{m}}:0.55\rightarrow0.12$ and recovery weight up to $w_{\mathrm{r}}\in[0.95,0.98]$.

\subsection{Internal controllers (TD3)}

Both layers use TD3~\cite{fujimoto2018td3}. Write $x^{\mathrm{hi}}=s$, $a^{\mathrm{hi}}=g$ and $x^{\mathrm{lo}}=[s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g]$, $a^{\mathrm{lo}}=a$, with observation normalisation $s_{\mathrm{n}}=\tanh(s/s_0)$ and goal scale $\kappa=4$. Each layer maintains twin critics $Q_{\theta_1},Q_{\theta_2}$, actor $\mu_{\phi}$ and target networks. Critic losses and targets are
\begin{equation}
L(\theta_j)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(Q_{\theta_j}(x,a)-y\bigr)^2\bigr],\quad j=1,2,
\label{eq:td3-L}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
y=r+\gamma\min_{k=1,2}Q_{\bar\theta_k}\bigl(x',\tilde a'\bigr),
\qquad
\tilde a'=\mu_{\bar\phi}(x')+\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-\epsilon,\epsilon\bigr).
\label{eq:td3-y}
\end{equation}
The actor follows the deterministic policy gradient
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{\phi}J
=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}
\Bigl[
\nabla_a Q_{\theta_1}(x,a)\big|_{a=\mu_{\phi}(x)}
\nabla_{\phi}\mu_{\phi}(x)
\Bigr],
\label{eq:td3-pi}
\end{equation}
with delayed actor updates and soft targets $\bar\psi\leftarrow\tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi$.

\subsection{Feasible-set projection}

Executable actions are projected onto the dynamic feasible set before each plant step~\cite{garcia2012safe,sun2024constrained}:
\begin{equation}
a_t^{\star}
= \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s_t)}\!\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}\bigr).
\label{eq:givesafe}
\end{equation}
Only transitions generated by executable $a_t^{\star}\in\mathcal{F}(s_t)$ enter the replay buffers. Absolute (non-residual) low-level execution is
\begin{equation}
a = \Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}\!\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)\bigr).
\label{eq:abs}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Historical goal relabeling (MS-HER)}

High-level transitions $(s_t,g_t,R_{t:t+c-1}^{\mathrm{ext}},s_{t+c})$ become stale as $\pi_{\mathrm{lo}}$ evolves. Market-structured hindsight experience replay extends HER-style goal relabeling~\cite{andrychowicz2017her,cui2025ghtd3}: it replaces $g_t$ by a candidate maximising a storage-activity-weighted log-likelihood under the current low-level policy,
\begin{equation}
\tilde g_t
\in
\arg\max_{g\in\mathcal{C}_t}
\sum_{i=t}^{t+c-1}
w_i^{\mathrm{act}}
\log \pi_{\mathrm{lo}}(a_i\mid s_i,g_i),
\label{eq:msher}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_t$ mixes the original goal, achieved $\Delta s^{\mathrm{int}}$, future goals and optional market-prior candidates, and subsequent residuals follow~\eqref{eq:h}.

\subsection{Feasibility-filtered MLE pretraining (F-MLE)}

Before RL, low-level (and high-level goal) parameters are warm-started by maximum likelihood on \emph{physically valid} price-aware/rule trajectories only:
\begin{equation}
\phi^{\star}
\in
\arg\max_{\phi}
\sum_{(s,g,a)\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{demo}}}
\log \pi_{\phi}(a\mid s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
a\in\mathcal{F}(s).
\label{eq:fmle}
\end{equation}
No frozen hybrid teacher residual is used at runtime.

\subsection{Policy learning}

Algorithms~\ref{alg:hmsd}--\ref{alg:td3-update} summarise hierarchical interaction and internal TD3 updates; Table~\ref{tab:hyper} lists main hyperparameters.

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{HMSD hierarchical policy learning}
\label{alg:hmsd}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require High/low actors $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}},\mu^{\mathrm{lo}}$ and twin critics with targets; buffers $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}},\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; exploration coefficient $\varepsilon\leftarrow\varepsilon_0$
\State F-MLE warm-start of low- (and high-) level parameters on feasible demonstrations~\eqref{eq:fmle}
\For{episode $=1,2,\ldots,E_{\max}$}
  \State Reset plant twin; $s\leftarrow s_0$; clear residual goal; $s_{\mathrm{prev}}\leftarrow s$
  \For{$t=0$ to $T-1$}
    \If{$t \bmod c = 0$}
      \State With probability $1-\varepsilon$: $g\leftarrow\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}(s)$; otherwise sample $g$ uniformly in the goal box
      \State Blend $g$ with market/recovery priors (MSGP)~\eqref{eq:msgp}; record block start $s^{\mathrm{blk}}\leftarrow s$, $g^{\mathrm{blk}}\leftarrow g$
    \Else
      \State $g\leftarrow h(s_{\mathrm{prev}},g,s)$ \Comment{residual goal~\eqref{eq:h}}
    \EndIf
    \State $\tilde a\leftarrow\mu^{\mathrm{lo}}(s_{\mathrm{n}},\kappa g)+\mathrm{noise}$; $a\leftarrow\Pi_{\mathcal{F}(s)}(\tilde a)$ \Comment{\eqref{eq:givesafe}}
    \State Apply $a$ to the plant twin; observe $s'$, $r^{\mathrm{ext}}$, $r^{\mathrm{int}}$ via~\eqref{eq:rext}--\eqref{eq:reward}
    \State $g'\leftarrow h(s,g,s')$; store $(s,g,a,r^{\mathrm{int}},s',g')$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$
    \If{$t \bmod c = c-1$}
      \State Store high-level transition $(s^{\mathrm{blk}},g^{\mathrm{blk}},R^{\mathrm{hi}},s',\tau_{0:c-1})$ in $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$ with $R^{\mathrm{hi}}$ from~\eqref{eq:reward}
    \EndIf
    \State Sample a mini-batch from $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{lo}}$; update the low-level controller (Algorithm~\ref{alg:td3-update}, discount $\gamma$)
    \If{$t \bmod c = 0$ \textbf{and} $|\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}|$ is sufficient}
      \State Sample a mini-batch from $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{hi}}$; relabel goals by MS-HER~\eqref{eq:msher}
      \State Update the high-level controller (Algorithm~\ref{alg:td3-update}, discount $\gamma^{c}$)
    \EndIf
    \State $s_{\mathrm{prev}}\leftarrow s$; $s\leftarrow s'$
  \EndFor
  \State $\varepsilon\leftarrow\max(\varepsilon_{\min},\varepsilon-\Delta\varepsilon)$
\EndFor
\Ensure Learned policies $\mu^{\mathrm{hi}}$, $\mu^{\mathrm{lo}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[!htbp]
\caption{Internal TD3 controller update (high or low level)}
\label{alg:td3-update}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Mini-batch $\mathcal{B}$; networks $(\mu_{\phi},Q_{\theta_1},Q_{\theta_2})$ and targets $(\mu_{\bar\phi},Q_{\bar\theta_1},Q_{\bar\theta_2})$; discount $\Gamma\in\{\gamma,\gamma^{c}\}$
\For{each transition $(x,a,r,x')\in\mathcal{B}$}
  \State $\tilde a'\leftarrow\mu_{\bar\phi}(x')+\mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma),-\epsilon,\epsilon\bigr)$
  \State $y\leftarrow r+\Gamma\min_{k=1,2}Q_{\bar\theta_k}(x',\tilde a')$
\EndFor
\State $\theta_j\leftarrow\theta_j-\alpha_{Q}\nabla_{\theta_j}L(\theta_j)$ for $j=1,2$ with $L$ in~\eqref{eq:td3-L}
\If{update index is a multiple of the policy delay $d$}
  \State $\phi\leftarrow\phi+\alpha_{\mu}\nabla_{\phi}J$ with $J$ in~\eqref{eq:td3-pi}
  \State Soft-update targets: $\bar\psi\leftarrow\tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi$ for $\psi\in\{\phi,\theta_1,\theta_2\}$
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Simulation results}

\subsection{Simulation settings}

Three representative weeks (winter, summer and transition) are evaluated on the plant twin under identical TOU and resource tables. Unless stated otherwise, primary ranking uses weekly episodes with SoC re-initialisation (weekly-reset protocol), consistent with hierarchical multi-energy case studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Conservative plant rule (B0; high thermal commitment with storage largely idle); price-aware heuristic rule (B1); rolling linprog model-predictive control; parametric PSO; and single-layer \textbf{TD3} with the same hybrid action space, network capacity and seeds as the low-level controller of HMSD, trained without hierarchical goals and without behaviour cloning, so that differences isolate the hierarchical design~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Feasible-set projection is applied to both TD3 and HMSD for a fair closed-loop comparison.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Primary metrics are calibrated episode reward under~\eqref{eq:J}, the energy-SoC gate~\eqref{eq:soc}, thermal energy and storage throughput. Multi-seed tables report mean$\pm$standard deviation and SoC pass counts.

\paragraph{Experimental setting.}
Training and closed-loop evaluations are performed on a multi-core workstation with GPU acceleration for actor--critic updates. Plant dynamics advance through the Sysplorer-exported FMU with one-hour steps. Classical baselines use the same plant interface for protocol consistency. HMSD hyperparameters are listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyper}; unless otherwise noted, multi-seed comparisons use seeds $\{0,1,2\}$.\footnote{Quantitative tables and figures in this section correspond to matched multi-seed closed-loop evaluations under the weekly protocol; training budgets for hierarchical and single-layer agents are identical within each comparison.}

Extreme single-step outliers are clipped for display. Primary claims rest on fixed seasonal evaluations (Table~\ref{tab:main}), where hierarchical policies reduce transition/summer variance relative to TD3 while remaining competitive in winter.

\subsubsection{Scheduling performance}

Closed-loop dispatch is examined for seed~$1$ (strong economic seed with full SoC pass on all three weeks), following seasonal protocols in hierarchical multi-energy studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. Figures~\ref{fig:bal-w}--\ref{fig:bal-s} compare electric power balances of HMSD, TD3, parametric PSO and rolling linprog; Fig.~\ref{fig:soc-mat} summarises battery and CAES gas SoC across seasons and methods (including rule B0).

\paragraph{Winter.}
Figure~\ref{fig:bal-w}(a) shows that HMSD couples thermal set-points with battery/CAES activity aligned to TOU bands: storage charges in valleys and discharges toward peaks while the grid exchange remains structured rather than oscillatory. TD3 (panel~b) attains competitive winter reward for this seed but exhibits less coherent multi-day inventory planning. Parametric PSO (panel~c) fails early under hybrid CAES mode constraints (short valid horizon), illustrating the difficulty of low-dimensional peak--valley heuristics on the twin. Linprog (panel~d) produces a feasible but conservative schedule near rule-level economics. The SoC matrix (Fig.~\ref{fig:soc-mat}, top row) confirms hierarchical inventory motion under HMSD versus near-idle battery under B0.

\paragraph{Transition.}
In the transition week (Fig.~\ref{fig:bal-t}), HMSD maintains the largest and most regular storage throughput while recovering energy SoC at week end. TD3 largely under-acts on storage for this seed (reward collapse relative to hierarchy), consistent with the ``inaction'' failure mode reported for single-layer TD3 in hierarchical multi-energy studies~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}. PSO can score a high cash-flow proxy by aggressive cycling yet still fails the energy-SoC gate, so it is not an operationally acceptable weekly plan. Linprog remains smooth but economically mediocre.

\paragraph{Summer.}
Under higher cooling-season load and TOU stress (Fig.~\ref{fig:bal-s}), HMSD again realises peak--valley-oriented storage use with full SoC pass on seed~$1$, while TD3 improves over B0 but trails hierarchy on reward. PSO trajectories remain irregular and fail recovery; linprog stays near the rule band. Across seasons, hierarchy therefore repairs both \emph{myopic under-use of storage} (TD3) and \emph{infeasible/irregular parametric search} (PSO) on the same FMU protocol.

\paragraph{Why classical optimisers underperform the plant rule B0---and what this implies for RL.}
\label{sec:why-or-pso}
A striking feature of Table~\ref{tab:main} is that rolling linprog and parametric PSO often fail to beat the conservative plant rule B0 on the \emph{same} closed-loop FMU protocol, even though both are ``optimisation'' methods. This is not an artefact of a weak rule baseline; it reflects a structural mismatch between simplified optimisers and the hybrid non-convex twin.

\textbf{B0 as a strong feasibility baseline.}
B0 keeps thermal output near the upper feasible band, holds the battery near idle and the CAES mode at idle whenever allowed, and therefore almost always completes the 168\,h week with the energy-SoC gate satisfied. Under TOU settlement, high thermal injection and limited storage cycling already yield competitive cash flow without mode-lock violations or inventory dumping. Any optimiser that intends to beat B0 must extract peak--valley value \emph{without} destroying feasibility---a harder task than improving a myopic or infeasible search.

\textbf{Rolling linprog: optimising the wrong model.}
The linprog controller solves a short-horizon linear programme on a \emph{relaxed surrogate}: continuous battery and gas-tank powers, linear fuel proxy, power balance and box SOC dynamics, then maps the first-step solution to hybrid CAES modes for FMU execution. Three gaps explain its mediocre closed-loop scores. (i)~\emph{Model mismatch}: discrete CAES modes, minimum-run locks and thermal inventories in the twin are only approximated; surrogate-optimal set-points are frequently projected or rewritten by the feasible set, so open-loop LP optimality does not transfer. (ii)~\emph{Myopic horizon}: a horizon of only a few hours cannot coordinate week-scale recovery and multi-day TOU structure. (iii)~\emph{Objective mismatch}: the LP minimises a linear buy/fuel/sell proxy, whereas evaluation ranks calibrated episode reward and the energy-SoC gate built from the true FMU cash-flow. Empirically, linprog induces much larger storage throughput than B0 yet returns lower winter/summer rewards (Table~\ref{tab:main}), i.e.\ aggressive cycling on a wrong model does not improve true economics.

\textbf{Parametric PSO: searching a narrow and brittle policy class.}
PSO in this study does not optimise free hybrid action sequences. It tunes a six-dimensional peak--valley heuristic (price thresholds, storage magnitudes, thermal bias, recovery strength), with each particle evaluated by a full FMU week. The searchable class is far smaller than the hybrid MDP; many parameter vectors trigger early constraint failures (e.g.\ winter trajectories that terminate after a few steps), and large failure penalties dominate the fitness. PSO can exceed B0 in transition cash flow for some seeds, but winter/summer SoC failures and irregular switching (Figs.~\ref{fig:bal-w}--\ref{fig:soc-mat}) show that heuristic search on the twin is brittle. Enlarging the search to full action sequences would be combinatorially prohibitive under FMU evaluation cost.

\textbf{Implication for reinforcement learning.}
These baselines therefore play complementary diagnostic roles rather than ``weak straw men''. Rules prove that \emph{feasibility-first} operation already scores well; linprog shows that \emph{convexification-plus-mapping} is insufficient for CAES hybrids on a high-fidelity twin; PSO shows that \emph{low-dimensional market heuristics} cannot reliably navigate mode locks and weekly recovery. HMSD (and, to a lesser extent, single-layer TD3) instead optimises policies by direct interaction with the twin under feasible-set projection, so credit is assigned to actions that remain executable. Hierarchy further supplies week-scale market and recovery intents (MSGP/MS-HER), repairing both the \emph{inaction} of under-trained single-layer agents and the \emph{infeasible aggression} of parametric search. The multi-seed lifts in Table~\ref{tab:main} should thus be read as gains over a strong rule and over method classes that are theoretically attractive but misaligned with the FMU hybrid decision structure---which is precisely where model-free hierarchical RL is most useful.

\paragraph{Remark on seed variance.}
Among RL seeds, evaluation-week rewards still differ because hybrid CAES modes induce multi-modal policies; hierarchy mainly stabilises transition/summer relative to TD3. Training-curve scatter (Fig.~\ref{fig:training}) should not be read as the primary multi-seed metric.

\paragraph{Surrogate weekly optimality gap.}
\label{sec:opt-gap}
A recurring Q1 question is how far closed-loop policies sit from an optimising programme. A non-convex hybrid FMU admits no cheap global optimum, so we report a \emph{transparent surrogate} rather than a false FMU dual. For each seasonal week we assemble perfect-foresight load, renewable availability and Shandong TOU prices from the twin logs, and solve a $168$\,h linear programme over continuous thermal power, battery/CAES-equivalent charge--discharge, and grid buy/sell, with box SOC dynamics, thermal ramps and a terminal inventory band matching the energy-SoC tolerance. The objective is a linear cash-flow proxy calibrated to twin economics (thermal O\&M $400$~CNY/MWh; renewable O\&M rates fitted on B0; must-take renewables; load revenue exogenous). Let $J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}$ be the LP optimum and $J_{\mathrm{surr}}(\pi)$ the \emph{same} proxy evaluated on a method's closed-loop trajectory. The gap
\[
\mathrm{gap}_{\mathrm{surr}}(\pi)=\frac{J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}-J_{\mathrm{surr}}(\pi)}{|J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}|}
\]
is therefore a true relative gap \emph{on one model}. Closed-loop FMU cash flow $J$ remains the engineering KPI and is \emph{not} claimed to be bounded by $J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}$.

Table~\ref{tab:gap} shows that HMSD recovers most of the surrogate value: winter $\mathrm{gap}_{\mathrm{surr}}\approx8\%$ ($J_{\mathrm{surr}}/J_{\mathrm{surr}}^{\star}\approx0.92$), transition $\approx23\%$ and summer $\approx20\%$. Single-layer TD3 is farther in winter ($\approx32\%$) where a comparable log exists; B0 and rolling linprog leave $57$--$92\%$ of surrogate value on the table. Early-failed PSO weeks omit proxy rows. The residual gaps are consistent with hybrid mode locks, efficiency losses and feasible-set rewrites absent from the continuous relaxation---i.e.\ the price of executability on the twin---while still placing hierarchical market-safe dispatch close to a favourable convex benchmark.

\paragraph{Ablation of MSGP, MS-HER and F-MLE.}
\label{sec:ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:abl} report \emph{matched-budget} multi-seed component ablations: full HMSD and three dropouts (MSGP, MS-HER, F-MLE) are each trained for $1.5\times10^{4}$ valid steps with seeds $\{0,1,2\}$ under the same FMU, TOU and weekly evaluation protocol. For each seed we average the three seasonal episode rewards, then report mean$\pm$std of those seed means; seasonal columns give mean$\pm$std over seeds and SoC pass counts $x/3$. These runs are \emph{diagnostic under a short budget} and are intentionally \emph{not} mixed with the main $3.5\times10^{4}$-step multi-seed ranking in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

All four variants remain far above single-layer TD3 on average short-budget weeks (overall mean seasonal $\Delta$ versus the paired TD3 seeds of roughly $+26$ to $+36$). At $1.5\times10^{4}$ steps, however, the full stack is \emph{not} the mean-reward maximiser: w/o F-MLE and w/o MSGP attain higher three-season means ($95.2\pm7.9$ and $94.3\pm7.7$) than full HMSD ($85.3\pm9.1$) or w/o MS-HER ($85.9\pm24.0$). The full run is pulled down by a single poor transition seed (high transition std); removing MS-HER yields the largest \emph{seed-to-seed} variance, including a near-collapse seed, so HER mainly stabilises learning rather than uniformly lifting the short-budget mean. Energy-SoC pass rates over the nine season--seed cells are $7/9$ (full, w/o MSGP), $8/9$ (w/o MS-HER) and $9/9$ (w/o F-MLE).

We interpret these diagnostics as follows. (i)~Short budget is not the deployment budget: market priors and F-MLE inject structure that can slow pure economic exploration early in training, so ablating them can raise 15k mean reward while the $3.5\times10^{4}$-step full stack remains the primary economic configuration (Table~\ref{tab:main}). (ii)~MS-HER should be read as a \emph{reliability} module under seed noise at fixed budget. (iii)~MSGP does not show a clean short-budget SoC advantage over full in this multi-seed table (both $7/9$); its value is better tied to recovery curricula and longer training rather than 15k mean reward. (iv)~F-MLE is consistent with a cold-start aid that online interaction can eventually replace. Overall, hierarchy still dominates TD3 under every dropout; the modules primarily shape variance, recovery and trainability rather than acting as short-horizon mean-reward additives.

\paragraph{Feasible-set projection and stress evidence.}
\label{sec:givesafe-stats}
Projection onto $\mathcal{F}(s)$ is applied to both TD3 and HMSD in the main comparison, so hierarchical economic gains should not be attributed solely to the safety filter. Two complementary measurements separate its role. (i)~\emph{Offline footprint on nominal weeks.} Comparing requested versus executed commands on seed~1 seasonal trajectories (Table~\ref{tab:givesafe}), HMSD requires adjustment on only about $14\%$ of steps with mean $\ell_2$ change $\approx0.13$, zero invalid transitions and zero plant failures, and low CAES lock occupancy ($\approx8\%$). Single-layer TD3 triggers adjustments on about $66\%$ of steps with mean $\ell_2\approx0.71$, nonzero invalid transitions, occasional plant failures and lock occupancy $\approx40\%$. Thus the same filter intervenes far more often under myopic policies. (ii)~\emph{Action-space stress.} Under strong exploration-like perturbations on a winter week (Gaussian action noise with $\sigma=0.4$ and $25\%$ random CAES mode flips), disabling the projection causes episode collapse with weekly $J\approx0$ and invalid transitions, whereas enabling it preserves $J\approx1.24\times10^{7}$~CNY (reward $\approx89.9$) with zero invalid transitions. Safety is therefore claimed as a \emph{pre-execution filter under distribution shift and exploration}, not as the source of peak--valley skill under nominal multi-seed ranking.

\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:support}

The scientific core is \emph{market--inventory--carbon--feasibility coupling}: maximise comprehensive monetary $J^{\mathrm{gen}}$ under TOU and a National ETS carbon price while recovering energy SoC and executing only in $\mathcal{F}(s)$. Hierarchical control matches week-scale inventory/carbon structure with hour-scale hybrid actions. HMSD mechanisms (market--inventory goals, MS-HER, F-MLE, causal goal pathways and feasible-set projection) target that coupling rather than a new actor--critic family. Relative to hydrogen-centric comprehensive-cost H-IES~\cite{cui2025ghtd3}, plant physics, hybrid CAES actions and the cash+ETS interface differ. Classical optimisers often fail to beat a conservative rule on the physics twin; feasible-set statistics and annual weekly-reset covers support hierarchical gains. Limitations include price-taker assumptions, fixed emission factors and ETS operating point, temperate-year resources, and incomplete summer SoC pass rates on some multi-seed tables.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

This paper addressed market--inventory--carbon--feasibility coupled scheduling of a thermal--battery--CAES multi-energy plant under Shandong TOU tariffs and a China National ETS carbon price. The plant is modelled in MWORKS Sysplorer and exported as an FMI FMU for closed-loop evaluation. Hierarchical Market-Safe Dispatch (HMSD) combines multi-timescale hierarchical intents, market-structured hindsight relabelling, feasibility-filtered pretraining, causal goal pathways and projection onto dynamic feasible sets. Multi-seed closed-loop results show gains over single-layer TD3 and over rules and classical optimisers that often underperform a conservative baseline on the same twin. Future work includes multi-region tariffs, tighter hybrid mathematical programmes on the same physics model, and extended risk-sensitive evaluation under extreme weather.
